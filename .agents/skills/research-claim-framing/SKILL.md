# Research Claim Framing

Use only when a real research manuscript or rebuttal has actual claims and evidence. This is not a general chat tone rule and not a request to suppress scientific uncertainty.

## Claim-first writing method

1. Collect the current question, exact study artifacts, experimental setup, baselines, primary results, negative controls and limitations before editing rhetoric.
2. Formulate the narrowest material contribution whose novelty or utility the evidence supports. Differentiate a new capability, boundary condition, empirical result and hypothesis.
3. Shape the narrative around problem, gap, evidenced contribution and scope, rather than chronology of implementation attempts. Every retained figure or experiment must answer a claim or challenge.
4. Select meaningful comparisons by an explicit, justified task definition. Do not move goalposts, cherry-pick metrics or omit an unfavorable baseline that changes the interpretation.
5. Remove unnecessary apologetic hedging; retain uncertainty, failed tests, adverse results, sample-size restrictions and threats to validity *where needed to evaluate the claim*. Replace unwarranted superlatives with measured conditions.
6. If the paper's strongest previous claim is falsified, revise or withdraw the claim and its narrative before editing style. The original data and known counterexamples remain available.
7. Validate factual coverage and target venue requirements, then stop. Carrier QA remains with existing research-publication-closure when relevant.

## Human / Agent contract

- Human: specify manuscript, target audience and intended contribution; receive a stronger but still falsifiable argument.
- Agent: bind manuscript passages to actual figures, tests, citations and contradictory evidence; do not rewrite scientific truth, create fictitious experiments or turn a style method into a proof.
- For reproducibility, separate manuscript presentation from exact source results and published artifacts.
