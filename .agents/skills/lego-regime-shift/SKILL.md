# LEGO Regime Shift Lens

Use this lens to distinguish visible output from latent system state and to turn uncertain turning-point hypotheses into testable, reversible decisions.

## Procedure

1. Define system boundary, horizon, population, and decision.
2. Separate latent state from observed outputs and narrative.
3. Audit LEVEL, FLOW, first derivative, and where useful second derivative.
4. Identify buffers and propagation delays.
5. Search for stock-flow or output-input divergence.
6. Identify the dominant constraint and possible constraint migration.
7. Maintain competing hypotheses: noise, cycle, structural change, regime/threshold shift, exogenous shock.
8. For each hypothesis state predictions, contradictions, discriminating evidence, and falsifiers.
9. Apply early-warning statistics only when a mechanistic model makes them meaningful; record false-alarm/miss risk.
10. Model actor adaptation and reflexivity.
11. Separate chronology, mechanism, causal effect, necessity, and counterfactual.
12. Stress-test actions across plausible futures.
13. Prefer staged, reversible actions with information gain and preserved option value when uncertainty is deep.
14. Define measurable signposts for scale/pause/reverse/switch.

## Output

Produce one Regime Card containing:
- system/horizon;
- stock-flow-derivative map;
- buffers/delays;
- constraint migration;
- competing hypotheses;
- early-warning evidence and limitations;
- falsifiers;
- actor adaptations;
- counterfactual gaps;
- robust actions;
- signposts.

## Hard guards

warning signal != forecast
forecast != decision
high current level != healthy marginal flow
temporal precedence != causality
coherent story != validated mechanism
robust action != globally optimal action

## Promotion law

Promote only durable architecture or evidence-capture consequences into project LEGO. Keep regime labels and forecasts as derived analysis unless independently validated.

## Stop condition

Stop when discriminating evidence, falsifiers, buffers/delays, robust actions, and next signposts are explicit enough to change or test a decision.
