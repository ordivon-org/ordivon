# C-K Design

Use this method when the desired object is not fully known and the task requires generating new design possibilities and new knowledge together.

## Core distinction

- K-space: propositions currently treated as knowledge/evidence within the scoped design effort.
- C-space: design propositions/concepts whose truth or feasibility is not yet established in K.

Do not put speculative design claims into K.

## Procedure

1. State the initial concept C0 without prematurely selecting an implementation.
2. Build the smallest relevant K-space:
   - constraints;
   - known mechanisms;
   - counterexamples;
   - existing designs;
   - measured evidence;
   - unknowns.
3. Expand C-space through meaningful partitions:
   - with/without property;
   - alternative mechanisms;
   - changed relation/topology;
   - changed actor/control allocation;
   - changed temporal/spatial assumption.
4. Mark expansion moves:
   - C -> C: concept partition/refinement;
   - C -> K: experiment/analysis that creates knowledge;
   - K -> C: new concept enabled by knowledge;
   - K -> K: deduction/research that expands knowledge.
5. Prefer expansive partitions that create materially new possibilities over repetitive parameter tuning.
6. Identify "knowledge holes" blocking promising concepts.
7. Design the cheapest experiment/research action that can convert a useful unknown into K.
8. Preserve counterexamples and rejected branches; do not erase them from the design history.
9. Separate:
   - generative design;
   - feasibility;
   - product selection;
   - optimization.
10. Stop before C-space becomes unlimited brainstorming; every active branch must connect to a decision or knowledge-expansion path.

## Output

Produce:
- C0;
- current K-space;
- concept tree/partitions;
- C/K operator trace;
- knowledge holes;
- experiment/research actions;
- surviving/rejected/deferred concepts;
- explicit product-selection non-claim.

## Promotion law

Promote only evidence-backed mechanisms, constraints, or interfaces. Concepts remain candidates until converted into knowledge/evidence through appropriate tests.

## Non-claims

- A concept is not a fact.
- A large concept tree is not creativity quality.
- C-K analysis does not prove market value, fun, usefulness, or novelty against the literature.
- Optimization among known alternatives is not automatically innovative design.
- Product selection remains with the domain owner and evidence process.

## Stop condition

Stop when the active concept frontier and the next knowledge-generating experiment are explicit enough to continue ordinary domain work.
