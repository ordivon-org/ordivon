# External Innovation Theory Census R1

Date: 2026-09-21
Status: EXTERNAL-AUTHORITY CENSUS / THEORY-OWNERSHIP AUDIT

## Purpose

This census asks a deliberately adversarial question:

> Which ideas currently expressed in Ordivon's "Composition Science" framing are already owned by mature external theories, methods, or empirical research programs?

The goal is not to prove novelty. The goal is to minimize private theory.

Decision vocabulary:

- **ADOPT_LENS** — use the mature external theory/method directly.
- **ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT** — retain as an optional lens selected by problem shape.
- **ENGINEERING_ONLY** — Ordivon may implement routing, evidence, or tooling, but does not own a new theory.
- **WATCH_AI_EXTENSION** — mature pre-AI theory exists; AI may change parameters, economics, or feasible scale, but a distinct new law is not yet established.
- **RESIDUAL_OPEN** — no mature owner has yet been shown to fully cover the exact Ordivon question.
- **REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION** — do not keep an Ordivon-specific theory name for this construct.

## A. Innovation as recombination and architecture

### T01 — Schumpeterian "new combinations"

Core construct:
Innovation can arise through new combinations of economic resources, products, methods, markets, sources, and organization.

Use in Ordivon:
Historical root for treating innovation as more than invention of a new technical component.

Boundary:
Too broad to tell us how to search, verify, modularize, or govern combinations.

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS / REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION**

### T02 — Weitzman: Recombinant Growth

Core construct:
New ideas can arise by recombining existing ideas; as the stock of ideas grows, the number of possible combinations can grow faster than the capacity to process them.

Formal relevance:
Direct owner for the intuition that primitive abundance can shift the bottleneck from element creation toward combination processing/search.

Boundary:
Macroeconomic growth model; not an architecture or verification method.

Primary source:
Martin L. Weitzman, "Recombinant Growth," Quarterly Journal of Economics 113(2), 1998.
https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-853d-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS / REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION**

### T03 — Fleming: Recombinant Uncertainty

Core construct:
Search using unfamiliar components and unfamiliar combinations increases outcome variance; novel combinations can produce more failures as well as breakthroughs.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct corrective against "novel composition = valuable composition."

Boundary:
Patent-based technological invention context; transport to software/agent systems requires evidence.

Primary source:
Lee Fleming, "Recombinant Uncertainty in Technological Search," Management Science 47(1), 2001.
https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.47.1.117.10671

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T04 — Henderson & Clark: Architectural Innovation

Core construct:
A major innovation can result from reconfiguring linkages among existing core components without changing the core concepts of those components.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct external owner for much of what R1 called "relation/composition novelty."

Boundary:
Originally product architecture and incumbent-firm knowledge; not every relation change is architectural innovation.

Primary source:
Rebecca Henderson and Kim Clark, "Architectural Innovation," Administrative Science Quarterly 35(1), 1990.
https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/3affcf33-4dea-4371-ab4c-4767380018ab

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS / REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION**

## B. Complexity, modularity, and design structure

### T05 — Simon: Near-Decomposability / Architecture of Complexity

Core construct:
Complex systems can often be understood as hierarchies of subsystems with stronger interactions within subsystems than between them.

Use in Ordivon:
Foundation for treating LEGO boundaries as interaction/coupling hypotheses rather than arbitrary labels.

Boundary:
Near-decomposability is a structural property, not a guarantee that a given decomposition is good.

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T06 — Baldwin & Clark: Design Rules / Modularity

Core construct:
Modularity makes independent experimentation and substitution possible when modules obey stable design rules.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct owner for replaceability, design-rule interfaces, and the value of modular experimentation.

Boundary:
Creating modularity has cost; modules differ in option value; modularity is not universally optimal.

Primary source:
Carliss Baldwin and Kim Clark, Design Rules, Volume 1: The Power of Modularity, MIT Press, 2000.
https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262538206/design-rules-volume-1/

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T07 — Baldwin & Clark: Real-Option Value of Modularity

Core construct:
Independent module experimentation can be treated as a real option; option value depends on technical potential, experiment cost, module visibility, and the cost of creating modular architecture.

Use in Ordivon:
Mature foundation for "replaceability has economic value" and for deciding when extra modularity is worth paying for.

Boundary:
Requires meaningful estimates/assumptions; not a universal scalar for architecture quality.

Reference:
"The Option Value of Modularity in Design."
https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8944/

Verdict:
**ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT**

### T08 — Baldwin: Design Rules Volume 2 / Technology Shapes Organizations

Core construct:
Technology's complementarity structure influences viable organizational structure.

Use in Ordivon:
Relevant to Runtime/Host/Gateway/Plugin boundaries and modular-monorepo organizational decisions.

Boundary:
Organization design is not mechanically derivable from code topology.

Primary source:
Carliss Baldwin, Design Rules, Volume 2: How Technology Shapes Organizations, MIT Press, 2024.
https://direct.mit.edu/books/oa-monograph/5887/Design-Rules-Volume-2How-Technology-Shapes

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T09 — Axiomatic Design

Core construct:
Maintain independence among functional requirements where possible; among acceptable designs, prefer lower information content / higher probability of success.

Use in Ordivon:
Useful coupling diagnostic when mapping functional requirements to design parameters.

Boundary:
Do not universalize its axioms beyond appropriate engineering design settings.

Reference:
MIT Axiomatic Design introduction.
https://web.mit.edu/axiom/www/introduction.shtml

Verdict:
**ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT**

## C. Search, exploration, and adaptation

### T10 — Nelson & Winter: Evolutionary Search and Routines

Core construct:
Organizations rely on routines, bounded search, variation, selection, and retention rather than global optimization over all possibilities.

Use in Ordivon:
Corrective against exhaustive "search all LEGO combinations."

Boundary:
Broad evolutionary economics, not a concrete optimizer.

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T11 — March: Exploration vs Exploitation

Core construct:
Organizations face a persistent allocation tension between exploration of new possibilities and exploitation of known certainties; adaptive systems can over-favor short-term exploitation.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct owner for deciding when to continue external search versus stabilize and reuse a mature composition.

Primary source:
James March, "Exploration and Exploitation in Organizational Learning," Organization Science 2(1), 1991.
https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T12 — Katila & Ahuja: Search Depth × Search Scope

Core construct:
Search is at least two-dimensional: depth (reuse of existing knowledge) and scope (breadth of exploration).

Use in Ordivon:
Better model for discovery policy than a single "search more" knob.

Primary source:
Riitta Katila and Gautam Ahuja, "Something Old, Something New," Academy of Management Journal 45(6), 2002.
https://doi.org/10.5465/3069433

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T13 — Rosenkopf & Nerkar: Boundary-Spanning Search

Core construct:
Exploration can span organizational boundaries, technological boundaries, both, or neither; different boundary-spanning patterns show different innovation impact.

Use in Ordivon:
Mature lens for "search adjacent repos" versus "search another field" versus "search another organization/ecosystem."

Primary source:
Lori Rosenkopf and Atul Nerkar, Strategic Management Journal 22(4), 2001.
https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.160

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T14 — Gavetti & Levinthal: Cognitive vs Experiential Search

Core construct:
Forward-looking cognitive representations can guide search on complex landscapes; backward-looking experiential search refines via observed outcomes.

Use in Ordivon:
Maps naturally to agent-generated architecture hypotheses followed by Runtime experiments and evidence.

Boundary:
Cognitive maps may be crude and can misdirect search.

Primary source:
Giovanni Gavetti and Daniel Levinthal, Administrative Science Quarterly 45(1), 2000.
https://doi.org/10.2307/2666981

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T15 — Dosi: Technological Paradigms and Trajectories

Core construct:
Technological search is structured by paradigms and trajectories; continuous progress within a trajectory differs from paradigm change.

Use in Ordivon:
Prevents treating all search as an unconstrained flat space.

Primary source:
Giovanni Dosi, Research Policy 11(3), 1982.
https://doi.org/10.1016/0048-7333(82)90016-6

Verdict:
**ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT**

### T16 — Kauffman / NK Rugged Fitness Landscapes

Core construct:
Interactions among elements can make performance landscapes rugged, generating many local optima and difficult combinatorial search.

Use in Ordivon:
Foundation for the idea that interaction structure, not merely component count, drives search difficulty.

Boundary:
NK is a stylized model; mapping K to real systems must be justified.

Reference:
Kauffman & Weinberger, Journal of Theoretical Biology, 1989.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2632988/

Verdict:
**ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT**

### T17 — Siggelkow & Levinthal: Temporarily Divide to Conquer

Core construct:
Different organizational structures may be useful at different search stages; temporary decentralization followed by reintegration can improve exploration/adaptation.

Use in Ordivon:
Potential lens for multi-agent decomposition followed by integration.

Boundary:
Simulation/model context; not a universal prescription for agent organizations.

Primary source:
Organization Science 14(6), 2003.
https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.14.6.650.24840

Verdict:
**WATCH_AI_EXTENSION / ROUTE_BY_CONTEXT**

## D. Knowledge acquisition, integration, and boundary crossing

### T18 — Cohen & Levinthal: Absorptive Capacity

Core construct:
Innovation depends on the ability to recognize the value of external information, assimilate it, and apply it; this depends strongly on prior related knowledge.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct owner for the distinction "external knowledge exists != external knowledge is usable."

Primary source:
Cohen & Levinthal, Administrative Science Quarterly 35(1), 1990.
DOI 10.2307/2393553

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T19 — Kogut & Zander: Combinative Capabilities

Core construct:
Firms create new capabilities by recombining existing capabilities; prior cooperative and organizational knowledge constrains future recombination.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct owner for organizational "ability to combine" as a capability in itself.

Primary source:
Bruce Kogut and Udo Zander, Organization Science 3(3), 1992.
https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.3.3.383

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS / REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION**

### T20 — Grant: Knowledge Integration / Knowledge-Based View

Core construct:
A central organizational capability is integrating specialized knowledge residing in different individuals/units.

Use in Ordivon:
Strong external foundation for multi-specialist Agent/Plugin composition.

Primary source:
Robert Grant, Strategic Management Journal 17(S2), 1996.
https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.4250171110

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T21 — Hargadon & Sutton: Technology Brokering

Core construct:
Organizations can innovate by acquiring solutions from diverse industries, retaining them in organizational memory, and transferring/recombining them through analogy.

Use in Ordivon:
Very close empirical precedent for cross-domain primitive discovery + composition memory.

Primary source:
Andrew Hargadon and Robert Sutton, Administrative Science Quarterly 42(4), 1997.
https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/research/publications/technology-brokering-innovation-product-design-firm

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T22 — Carlile: Knowledge Boundaries

Core construct:
Cross-domain work encounters syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic knowledge boundaries requiring transfer, translation, and transformation respectively.

Use in Ordivon:
Direct corrective to the assumption that schema/API compatibility implies semantic composability.

Primary source:
Paul Carlile, Organization Science 15(5), 2004.
https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1040.0094

Verdict:
**ADOPT_LENS**

### T23 — Chesbrough: Open Innovation

Core construct:
Organizations can and should use external as well as internal ideas and paths to market rather than assuming useful innovation must originate internally.

Use in Ordivon:
Mature innovation-management owner for "external-first / adopt-before-build."

## F. Engineering methods for systematic composition

Core construct:

**ADOPT_LENS**

### T29 — NASA V-model / Integration and Verification

Core construct:

Use in Ordivon:
External owner for much of "compose -> run -> verify" lifecycle structure.

NASA Systems Engineering Handbook.

**ADOPT_LENS / REJECT_PRIVATE_REINVENTION**

Core construct:
System properties can be established compositionally by checking component guarantees under assumptions that are discharged by the rest of the system/environment.

Use in Ordivon:

## I. Residual audit

After this census, no strong evidence remains for a new general-purpose "Composition Science" theory owned by Ordivon.

The plausible residuals are narrower and mostly engineering/research-program questions:

### R1 — Theory routing

Given a real problem, can an agent system identify which mature lens applies, activate it at the right time, and avoid activating irrelevant lenses?

Standing:
**ENGINEERING_ONLY / RESIDUAL_OPEN**

This is primarily a method-selection and orchestration problem, not yet a new theory.

### R2 — Evidence-backed composition memory

Can a system retain and retrieve:
problem -> context -> composition -> assumptions -> evidence -> failure -> substitution
in a way that transfers across tools, versions, domains, and providers?

Standing:
**ENGINEERING_ONLY / RESIDUAL_OPEN**

Hargadon/Sutton, absorptive capacity, organizational memory and case-based/analogy research cover much of the conceptual ground. The residual may be implementation and evaluation in agent systems.

### R3 — Agent-induced change in search economics

Do agents materially change the relative costs of:
- discovering alternatives;
- translating knowledge across boundaries;
- implementing candidates;
- testing/falsifying candidates;
- switching between candidates?

Standing:
**WATCH_AI_EXTENSION / RESIDUAL_OPEN**

Research Policy 2024 already models AI-prioritized search, and 2025-2026 empirical work is starting to measure AI-enabled recombination and uneven productivity. The remaining question is quantitative and domain-specific, not a blank theoretical field.

### R4 — Machine-actionable authority-aware composition

Can technical compatibility, semantic authority, effect authority, evidence provenance, and dynamic execution contracts be composed in one operational agent system without collapsing natural owners?

Standing:
**ENGINEERING_ONLY / RESIDUAL_OPEN**

Pieces exist in systems engineering, formal contracts, security, provenance, workflow, and organizational theory. No claim of theoretical novelty is justified yet.

## J. Routing policy for Ordivon LEGO

Do not create a new private lens where one of these mature owners fits.

Suggested routing:

- "Is this a new arrangement of known components?" -> Architectural Innovation.
- "Where should module boundaries be?" -> Simon + DSM + Design Rules.
- "Is extra modularity worth it?" -> Baldwin/Clark real options.
- "Search more or exploit current composition?" -> March.
- "Search narrowly/deeply or broadly?" -> Katila/Ahuja.
- "Cross organization/technology boundaries?" -> Rosenkopf/Nerkar.
- "Reason first or learn from trials?" -> Gavetti/Levinthal.
- "Can we understand/use external knowledge?" -> Absorptive Capacity.
- "How do specialists' knowledge combine?" -> Grant / Kogut-Zander.
- "Are domain meanings actually aligned?" -> Carlile.
- "Should we source externally?" -> Open Innovation.
- "What complementary assets are missing?" -> Teece.
- "How do we continually reconfigure?" -> Dynamic Capabilities.
- "How do independent owners coordinate?" -> Ecosystem/Platform theory.
- "Generate combinations systematically?" -> Morphological analysis.
- "Avoid premature architecture commitment?" -> SBCE.
- "Compare many multi-objective architectures?" -> MATE.
- "Are assumptions/guarantees compositionally valid?" -> Assume-Guarantee.
- "What states are actually reachable?" -> Control/formal reachability.
- "How do decomposition, integration, verification and validation connect?" -> Systems Engineering.
- "How do reusable families manage variants?" -> Software Product Lines.
- "Does AI change the economics of search/testing?" -> AI prioritized-search / AI innovation literature; treat as an empirical question.

## K. Current verdict

The external-theory census supports a strong convergence result:

> Ordivon independently arrived at a useful cluster of intuitions, but the cluster is largely covered by mature theories distributed across innovation economics, organizational learning, design science, systems engineering, formal methods, software product lines, platform/ecosystem strategy, and knowledge management.

That is a positive result.

It means the next methodological step is not to defend a new theory name. It is to adopt the mature lenses, preserve their applicability boundaries, and make Ordivon better at routing, composing, executing, and testing them.

No theoretical novelty gap is established by R1.
