# Commercial Channel Experiment R1

Date: 2026-09-19
Status: ACTIVE HYPOTHESIS / NO OUTBOUND OR PUBLICATION EFFECT AUTHORIZED BY THIS DOCUMENT
Scope: first real distribution/customer-acquisition falsification for the current technical-delivery business candidate.

This is a task-local experiment, not an Ordivon marketing framework.

## 1. Objective

Determine which low-cost channel can produce real buyer learning and progress toward the first paid, accepted technical delivery.

The experiment optimizes for:

```text
qualified conversation
  -> proposal
  -> paid delivery
  -> acceptance
```

It does not optimize for follower count, raw impressions, generic traffic or content volume.

## 2. Offer hypothesis

Candidate offer:

> Reproducible, verifiable technical delivery for difficult software / automation / research-engineering problems: reproduce or diagnose the problem, make a bounded improvement where authorized, verify the outcome, preserve evidence, and hand off a usable result.

This is a hypothesis until buyers respond and pay.

Do not market internal Ordivon architecture, Agent count, LEGO terminology, Runtime internals or standards inventory as the primary product.

## 3. Buyer hypotheses

Initial buyer segments to falsify:

1. AI/software startup founder or CTO with a difficult reliability, reproducibility, automation or integration problem.
2. Small engineering/R&D team that lacks time to reproduce, validate or harden a technically messy workflow.
3. Open-source commercial / technical-service team that occasionally needs bounded expert delivery plus evidence rather than another permanent employee.

These are hypotheses, not ICP truth.

## 4. Channel A — founder-led outbound

Purpose: maximize direct learning per founder hour.

Preparation may be automated:

- identify a small set of public organizations/roles matching the buyer hypotheses;
- research only publicly relevant business/technical context;
- prepare a short reason why one concrete problem pattern may be relevant;
- draft individualized outreach;
- record source/provenance and avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.

Human/authorized owner retains:

- whether to contact the prospect;
- exact message;
- any consequential send;
- conversation and pricing judgement.

Do not automate high-volume personalised messaging or infer sensitive traits.

Evidence to record:

- prospect/account;
- channel/source;
- problem hypothesis;
- contact attempt if authorized;
- response;
- qualified/not-qualified reason;
- next step;
- proposal/paid outcome if any.

## 5. Channel B — evidence-driven content

Purpose: turn already-performed real technical work into useful public evidence.

Eligible content forms:

- reproducible case study;
- benchmark with method/data limitations;
- failure analysis;
- before/after engineering evidence;
- research result with source/provenance;
- technical teardown;
- small open-source utility or verifier.

Content requirements:

- useful to the intended technical audience even if they never buy;
- claims traceable to evidence;
- limitations explicit;
- no invented customer/result claims;
- no confidential/client data without explicit authorization;
- ICC Marketing Code applies when the piece is commercial marketing communication;
- Google Search Essentials applies when organic Google discovery is an intended channel.

Do not mass-produce low-information SEO pages.

## 6. Channel C — technical community distribution

Purpose: put one useful evidence artifact in front of communities where the underlying technical problem is actually discussed.

Candidate surfaces are selected per artifact/audience, not hardcoded as a universal channel list.

Rules:

- follow provider/community rules;
- disclose affiliation when materially relevant;
- contribute useful information rather than link-drop spam;
- do not simulate grassroots endorsement;
- preserve exact post/link identity where a consequential publish effect is authorized;
- distinguish provider acceptance/read-back from downstream buyer interest.

## 7. Research vs marketing boundary

Use ISO 20252:2026 and ICC/ESOMAR 2025 when performing research/interviews/structured insight work.

Do not treat direct outbound as ISO 20252 research. ISO explicitly excludes non-research activities such as direct marketing.

Use the ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code 2024 for marketing communications, together with applicable jurisdiction/platform rules.

## 8. Measurement

Primary outcome ladder:

| Level | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| exposure/touch | provider or manually recorded occurrence |
| response | real counterparty response |
| qualified conversation | current relevant problem + plausible buyer/decision path + agreed useful next step |
| proposal | concrete scope/price/acceptance sent to a real prospect |
| paid delivery | payment/contract/order evidence |
| accepted delivery | customer acceptance or agreed acceptance evidence |
| repeat/referral | second purchase, expansion or attributable referral |

Secondary diagnostics:

- response rate;
- qualified-conversation rate;
- proposal conversion;
- founder time per qualified conversation;
- founder time per paid delivery;
- direct external/API/content cost;
- channel/source provenance.

For web analytics, use provider-native source/medium/campaign semantics if deployed. For early B2B work, also ask the buyer how they found Ordivon; do not assume analytics attribution is causal truth.

## 9. First pilot batches

These are operational pilot sizes, not statistical sufficiency claims.

### Outbound pilot

Prepare up to 15 high-fit prospect/account candidates across the three buyer hypotheses.

No message is sent solely because it appears in the batch.

### Content pilot

Prepare up to 2 evidence-first technical pieces from work that can be shared lawfully and accurately.

Publication requires explicit publication authority and final claim review.

### Community pilot

For each authorized published evidence piece, identify up to 3 genuinely relevant communities/surfaces.

Do not cross-post automatically merely to meet a quota.

## 10. Decision rule

After the pilot, compare channels on downstream commercial evidence rather than reach.

Keep/increase a channel only when its evidence justifies another iteration.

Change the offer/segment/message when conversations reveal a repeated mismatch.

Pause a channel when it creates attention but no useful buyer learning or when founder/operational cost is disproportionate to the evidence gained.

Do not activate paid advertising until a real offer/segment/conversion path is sufficiently understood that paid reach would test a defined hypothesis rather than amplify uncertainty.

## 11. What R1 does not authorize

This document does not authorize:

- contacting any specific person;
- sending email/DMs;
- scraping protected/private personal data;
- publishing content;
- buying ads;
- installing analytics cookies/tracking;
- creating advertising audiences;
- transferring personal data to marketing providers;
- making customer-result claims not supported by evidence.

Those are separate consequential effects with their own authority/compliance/provider requirements.
