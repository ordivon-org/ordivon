# Ordivon Game Deep Foundations — GDF0-A

## 0. Research boundary

R1–R29 solved a different problem from GDF0.

R29 asked for the smallest semantic coordinate stack that can preserve tested Game counterfactuals across diverse forms. It explicitly stated:

```text
FoundationClosure != metaphysical completeness
EncodingSufficiency != ExplanatorySufficiency
Primitive != Important
Derived != Disposable
```

GDF0 therefore does **not** reopen F1–F9 by default. It asks a deeper derived-science question:

> What phenomena are we pointing at when we say Play and Game, how are those phenomena related, and what mechanisms generate them?

This is not Pre-G0 product selection and not canonical G0.

Current product search remains parked as retained evidence. GDF0 exists partly because the prior Pre-G0 search exposed theory-depth asymmetry: decision/systemic forms were easier for Ordivon to formalize than embodied, aesthetic, playful and expressive forms.

---

# 1. Why a single dictionary definition is the wrong first move

The external literature does not merely disagree over wording. Different fields often target different **objects** while using the same word `play`.

Examples:

- comparative ethology asks how an observer can identify play behavior in animals without verbal self-report;
- developmental psychology often asks about motivation, flexibility, learning and child-led activity;
- philosophy of games asks what is distinctive about game-playing, rules, goals and the attitude toward constraints;
- Game Studies asks about artifacts, practices, cultural frames, player appropriation and historically changing categories;
- design theory asks what structures can be constructed to generate valued player experience.

Therefore:

```text
Definition disagreement
may partly be
Target-object disagreement.
```

A theory of observable animal play behavior and a theory of designed game agency are not automatically competitors at the same level.

This is the first GDF0 level correction.

# 2. Initial target separation

GDF0-A introduces six provisional targets. These are research coordinates, not new F1–F9 primitives.

## 2.1 PlayBehavior

Observable organization of behavior that is classified as play-like from structure/context.

Candidate evidence includes:

```text
modified or recombined functional behavior
repetition without rigid stereotypy
self-handicapping / exaggeration / incomplete sequences
limited immediate instrumental function
occurrence under sufficiently permissive / low-pressure context
```

This target is especially important where subjective access is unavailable: nonhuman animals, infants, historical traces, synthetic agents.

`PlayBehavior` does not imply we have proved a positive subjective feeling.

## 2.2 PlayExperience / PlayMode

Participant-relative organization of attention, motivation, valuation and action while engaging playfully.

Potential components under investigation:

```text
process / means valued in themselves
locally adopted goals or constraints
reduced dominance of immediate external utility
willing engagement with variation / uncertainty / challenge
permission to experiment, fail, transform or reframe
```

This is not yet a definition.

A person can enact a GameStructure while PlayMode is weak or absent: coercive classroom exercise, some professional work, compulsory gamified labor.

Conversely PlayMode can exist with no formal game artifact.

## 2.3 PlayFrame

A self-, jointly-, culturally- or institutionally recognized frame that changes what actions mean, what consequences count, which constraints bind, and what kinds of experimentation are permitted.

Examples:

```text
rough-and-tumble bite
vs
serious attack

pretend sword
vs
weapon

chess sacrifice
vs
destroying valuable property
```

A PlayFrame can modify stakes and interpretation without making consequences unreal.

```text
PlayFrame != zero real consequence
```

Sports injuries, gambling losses, social humiliation and professional stakes remain real.

## 2.4 PlayPractice

A culturally or socially stabilized recurring way of playing.

This includes conventions not necessarily encoded in one artifact:

```text
house rules
sports etiquette
speedrunning norms
role-play conventions
play signals
community metas
```

PlayPractice helps distinguish the designed/physical object from the living activity around it.

## 2.5 GameStructure

A relatively stable normative/causal possibility structure that shapes participant action through some combination of:

```text
state / roles
allowed or meaningful actions
constraints / constitutive rules
transition / resolution semantics
local goals or evaluation structure
information conditions
success/failure/score/comparison where applicable
```

This remains intentionally broader than Suits' fixed-goal form because sandbox, role-play and open-ended game forms may permit player-authored/local goals.

A future round must determine whether `goal/evaluation structure` is mandatory, optional or supplied by PlayPractice/Player rather than artifact.

## 2.6 Gameplay

Actual participant enactment of a GameStructure through observation, interpretation, intent, action and consequence.

```text
GameStructure
+ participant coupling
→ Gameplay episode
```

Gameplay may be playful, professional, compulsory, instrumental or mixed in motive.

Therefore:

```text
Gameplay != PlayMode
GameStructure != Play
```

This separation is a major GDF0-A candidate and must be attacked later.

# 3. First strong separation — Play and Game are not synonyms

The current evidence supports at least a provisional orthogonality.

## 3.1 Play without a formal GameStructure

Examples include:

```text
animal locomotor play
rough-and-tumble play
object play
child free pretend play
improvised vocal play
doodling / material experimentation
unstructured make-believe
```

These can exhibit play behavior or PlayMode without fixed victory conditions, quantifiable outcomes or stable formal rules.

## 3.2 GameStructure without current play

A chess ruleset can exist while nobody is playing it.

A digital executable can retain game state/rules while inactive.

A tournament rulebook is a GameStructure even between matches.

Thus GameStructure can be an artifact/practice-level object rather than an episode.

## 3.3 Gameplay with weak PlayMode

Examples pressure any definition equating game-playing with intrinsic playfulness:

```text
compulsory classroom game
professional player performing under contract
AI/synthetic player executing a game policy
worker forced through gamified workflow
training simulation treated only as assessment
```

These may instantiate rules, actions and outcomes while voluntariness/process-valued engagement is weak or absent.

This does not prove that ordinary language must call every case "play". It proves that our model should not silently collapse structure, enactment and participant experience.

# 4. Adjacent-term separation

The following terms are deliberately kept distinct. Boundaries are expected to be graded/overlapping rather than a brittle partition.

## 4.1 Playfulness

A disposition or temporary stance that increases the probability of entering/reframing activity toward PlayMode.

```text
Playfulness != actual Play episode
```

A playful person may be doing serious work; a non-playful participant may still perform Gameplay.

## 4.2 Toy / Plaything

An object or medium with affordances that support play but does not necessarily specify a sufficiently complete GameStructure.

```text
Toy
≈ affordance-rich play substrate
```

A ball can be a toy, sports equipment, material, projectile or game object depending on frame/practice.

Toyhood is partly relational.

## 4.3 Puzzle

A problem structure in which a target solution/answer/criterion is central.

A puzzle may be embedded in a game, may itself be played as a game-like challenge, or may be solved instrumentally for work/science.

```text
Puzzle != Game by definition
Puzzle != non-Game by definition
```

The key research question is whether repeatable transition structure/opportunity for strategy is required for GameStructure beyond one problem instance.

## 4.4 Sport

A culturally institutionalized physical/skill practice, often competitive and rule-governed.

Many sports are clear games; some sport-like pursuits such as mountaineering, exercise or skill exhibition pressure any simple equivalence.

Institutional recognition and embodied performance belong to separate coordinates from GameStructure itself.

## 4.5 Contest

A comparison or competition among parties under some criterion.

```text
Contest can be Game, Sport, Work, ritualized status competition, or none of them.
Competition != Game
```

A sales contest and chess tournament share comparison but not necessarily PlayMode/purpose.

## 4.6 Simulation

A system/process representing selected dynamics of another system.

Simulation can support gameplay, training, prediction or scientific inference.

```text
Simulation fidelity != Game status
```

A game may have almost no simulation; a simulation may have no play.

## 4.7 Ritual

A repeated symbolically/normatively loaded practice whose constraints may be treated as constitutively serious rather than adopted primarily to enable a valued form of action.

Ritual is a crucial boundary case because it can be:

```text
rule-governed
bounded
repeated
symbolic
social
performative
```

while still differing strongly from game/play in authority, purpose and consequence interpretation.

## 4.8 Performance

Activity organized partly around presentation/expression for self or audience.

Dance, music, speedrunning, role-play and sport can be performance and play simultaneously.

```text
Performance != Play
but may couple to PlayMode / GameStructure.
```

## 4.9 Exploration

Information-seeking or possibility-testing activity.

Exploration can be serious/instrumental or playful.

Animal-play literature explicitly treats play as related to but distinct from exploration; developmental causal-learning research suggests exploratory activity can reveal possible causal structures.

GDF0 must not define all play as exploration without explaining repetitive/ritualized/social/performative play.

## 4.10 Creation

Deliberate production/transformation of form or artifacts.

Creation can be work, art, play or gameplay.

R20 already established:

```text
Creation != Generation != Creativity != Authorship
```

GDF0 adds:

```text
Creation != Play
```

while retaining creative play as an important intersection.

## 4.11 Make-believe / Pretend

A representational frame in which entities/actions are treated under imagined roles/meanings.

Pretend play strongly pressures definitions requiring explicit score/win conditions.

But chess, rhythm play and many sports show:

```text
Fiction / make-believe is not required for all games/play.
```

## 4.12 Gambling

A game/practice coupling uncertain outcomes to material stakes.

It is a major falsifier against:

```text
Play = unproductive
Play = low-stakes
Game purpose must be intrinsic
```

GameStructure and external stakes can coexist, and external purpose may dominate participant motivation.

## 4.13 Work

Activity organized around externally consequential production/obligation is not the logical negation of Play.

Possible relations include:

```text
work with PlayMode
playing a Game professionally as work
using a game for training/work
play that accidentally produces valuable artifacts/skills
```

The important distinction is likely not `difficulty` or `effort` but how goals/constraints/value are authorized and why the activity is pursued.

# 6. First competing-model map

GDF0-B will analyze these in depth. GDF0-A records only their target and initial pressure.

## M1 — Rule/constraint/goal model (Suits)

Core idea:

```text
prelusory goal
+ restricted permissible means
+ constitutive rules
+ acceptance of those constraints
```

Strength:

- explains why intentionally inefficient constraints can create a distinctive activity;
- separates game goal from real-world efficient achievement;
- gives a powerful account of many sports, board games and challenge games.

Pressure:

- primarily a model of **playing a game**, not animal/free play;
- fixed prelusory goal is awkward for some sandbox, improvisational and open-ended forms;
- voluntariness/lusory attitude requires care in professional/coerced participation.

## M2 — Cultural/frame model (Huizinga / Caillois family)

Common emphasis includes:

```text
freedom
bounded/separate frame
special order/rules
uncertainty
different relation to ordinary life
```

Strength:

- sees play as a framed social/cultural phenomenon rather than only formal state transitions;
- explains changed meaning of actions inside play.

Pressure:

- the play/non-play boundary is porous;
- productive, political, professional and high-stakes play violate strong separation/unproductivity readings;
- formal games can be deeply embedded in ordinary economic/social life.

## M3 — Family-resemblance / anti-essentialist model (Wittgensteinian pressure)

Core claim:

```text
no single essence may be common to all ordinary-language games;
concept use may form overlapping similarities.
```

Strength:

- protects against brittle universal definitions;
- matches historically changing cultural categories.

Pressure:

- classification humility alone does not provide a mechanism;
- Ordivon still needs causal models that predict when structures create player experience.

GDF0 should therefore distinguish:

```text
No lexical essence
!=
No discoverable mechanisms.
```

## M4 — Ethological operational model (Burghardt family)

Five widely used criteria characterize play behavior through:

```text
limited immediate functionality
endogenous/voluntary/rewarding quality
structural/temporal modification
repeated but non-stereotyped performance
relaxed field / low competing pressure
```

Strength:

- cross-species operationalization;
- avoids requiring language, explicit game rules or human reports;
- strongly suggests play cannot be reduced to formal GameStructure.

Pressure:

- observational application can be difficult;
- motivation/affect may need inference;
- identifying behavior is not the same as explaining why play exists.

## M5 — Practice / developmental-function models

Play may practice motor, cognitive or social capacities.

Strength:

- explains why juveniles rehearse altered fragments of serious behavior;
- generates developmental predictions.

Pressure:

```text
Function of play
!=
definition of a play episode.
```

Adult play, novel play forms and activities with no obvious future skill target limit narrow practice accounts.

## M6 — Training-for-the-unexpected model

Špinka, Newberry & Bekoff propose that play may train flexible motor/emotional responses to unexpected loss of control; self-handicapping can deliberately generate perturbations.

Strength:

- explains playful generation of difficulty/disadvantage;
- directly connects variation, uncertainty, recovery and resilience;
- produces discriminating predictions rather than a vague benefit claim.

Pressure:

- likely explains an important subset/function of mammalian play rather than every human symbolic/digital form;
- must not be promoted to universal Game ontology.

## M7 — Exploration / causal-learning model

Developmental work links extended childhood, exploration, pretend/counterfactual reasoning and causal learning; computational intrinsic-motivation work similarly studies self-generated exploration without external task rewards.

Strength:

- explains why low-immediate-utility interaction can generate world-model knowledge;
- links play to counterfactual possibility discovery.

Pressure:

- exploration and play are empirically/conceptually distinguishable;
- repeated mastery, competition, performance and familiar ritualized play may have little new information gain.

## M8 — Intrinsic/autotelic/process-valued model

Common definitions emphasize voluntary engagement and activity valued for its own sake.

Strength:

- captures why means/process can matter independently of external outputs;
- applies to many child/adult play experiences.

Pressure:

- mixed motives are common;
- professional, gambling, competitive status and compulsory contexts challenge a strict intrinsic criterion;
- subjective motive is hard to infer in nonhuman/synthetic cases.

## M9 — Agency / striving-play model (Nguyen)

Games can specify temporary goals/abilities and thereby design a form of agency. In striving play, players adopt a temporary goal such as winning for the sake of the valuable struggle/means.

Strength:

- explains the goal/purpose split;
- explains why constraints can be attractive rather than merely restrictive;
- points toward Game as a medium for designed action/agency.

Pressure:

- strongest for game-playing rather than free/object/animal play;
- not all game participation is striving play;
- needs coupling to perceptual, affective, social and embodied mechanisms.

## M10 — Play-as-spectrum models

Some developmental accounts place free play, guided play and games on a spectrum rather than enforcing sharp category walls.

Strength:

- captures mixed structure/guidance and graded transitions.

Pressure:

- a spectrum still requires dimensions;
- one scalar from "free" to "structured" may erase distinct axes such as motive, rule authority, representation, stakes and agency.

# 7. Candidate synthesis C0 — layered target ontology

GDF0-A's strongest current synthesis is not a universal definition but a level model:

```text
PlayBehavior
        ↕ imperfect inference
PlayExperience / PlayMode
        ↕ framed by
PlayFrame / PlayPractice
        ↕ may couple to
GameStructure / Toy / Puzzle / Simulation / Sport / Ritual ...
        ↓ when enacted
Gameplay / concrete activity trajectory
```

This explains why prior definitions appear to conflict:

```text
Burghardt → primarily PlayBehavior identification
Autotelic accounts → primarily PlayMode/motivation
Huizinga/Caillois → primarily Frame/Practice
Suits → primarily GameStructure + attitude of game-playing
Nguyen → GameStructure as designed agency + player motive
Wittgenstein → warning about lexical/category closure across all levels
```

Evidence status:

```text
Novelty: N1 candidate synthesis
Status: UNFROZEN
```

The components are not new individually. The proposed value is explicit level separation and the prediction that many classical disagreements should dissolve when target levels are specified.

Falsifier:

> Find recurring cases where two theories target the same level, make incompatible predictions, and cannot be represented as different dimensions/views of this layered model.

# 8. Candidate synthesis C1 — Counterfactual Slack

A second, more speculative candidate emerges from the convergence among relaxed-field ethology, self-handicapping, process-valued engagement, striving play and exploratory learning.

Candidate concept:

```text
CounterfactualSlack =
degrees of behavioral / interpretive possibility
not completely dictated by immediate externally binding necessity,
allowing action to be selected for locally adopted, process-internal,
exploratory, expressive or challenge-producing reasons.
```

Potential PlayMode hypothesis:

```text
Play often recruits CounterfactualSlack
and then spends it on
variation / exploration / self-imposed constraint /
pretence / challenge / expression / social negotiation.
```

This is deliberately weaker than:

```text
Play = low stakes
Play = purposeless
Play = safe
```

A play episode may have real stakes and useful outputs. The claim concerns **proximate action selection**: not every available action is dictated solely by immediate external necessity.

Why this is interesting:

- Burghardt's relaxed field supplies ecological slack;
- modified/recombined play behavior spends slack on non-primary-context actions;
- training-for-unexpected actively uses slack to self-handicap;
- Suitsian games voluntarily add constraints rather than use the most externally efficient means;
- Nguyen's striving player adopts a local goal for the value of the struggle;
- pretend/creative play uses slack to reinterpret objects/actions;
- exploratory play uses slack to sample possibilities.

Potential Game relation:

```text
Play opens/uses slack.
GameStructure can deliberately constrain slack
into a shaped possibility / agency problem.
```

This may explain the apparent paradox:

```text
Why can more constraints create more valued freedom?
```

Because constraints can transform undifferentiated possibility into legible, learnable or expressive differences.

Evidence status:

```text
Novelty: N1/N2 candidate hypothesis
Status: VERY PROVISIONAL
```

Known pressures:

- professional high-stakes play;
- coerced game participation;
- compulsive play;
- repetitive mastery with little exploration;
- ritual and art practices that also contain slack;
- serious scientific/creative exploration.

Therefore `CounterfactualSlack` is not admitted as a definition. GDF0-B/C must derive predictions that distinguish it from generic agency, autonomy, spare time and exploration.

# 9. Candidate synthesis C2 — Constraint Transformation

A related candidate:

```text
Play does not simply maximize freedom.
Play can transform which constraints are binding and why.
```

Examples:

```text
physical necessity → temporarily irrelevant
self-imposed challenge → locally binding
ordinary object meaning → pretend meaning
serious fight tactic → modified play-fight tactic
external outcome goal → local game goal
```

A GameStructure can be viewed as a **constraint transformer**:

```text
ambient possibility space
→ selected rules / goals / roles / information / feedback
→ shaped action problem
```

This provides a deeper candidate explanation for the existing R11 principle:

```text
Freedom != Agency
```

and for Suits' unnecessary obstacles without requiring all play to have explicit obstacles.

Evidence status:

```text
Novelty: N1 candidate synthesis
Status: UNFROZEN
```

# 11. Cross-owner boundary

GDF0 should not clone Human or Media foundations.

Likely owner split:

```text
Human:
  motivation, affect, motor learning, developmental/evolutionary mechanisms,
  attention, cognitive control, social development

Media:
  signal, perception, representation, expression, multimodal presentation

World:
  reality coupling, cross-domain causal semantics

Game:
  how these mechanisms are recruited/reframed by PlayMode,
  GameStructure, participant coupling and ludic constraints
```

This boundary is provisional because GDF0 may discover a genuinely cross-domain structure worth promotion later.

# 12. GDF0-A dependency discoveries

The current evidence suggests GDF0 cannot proceed as one flat "what is a game?" question.

At least four dependency branches are emerging:

```text
A. Play mode / motivation / valuation
B. Constraint / rule / goal / frame structure
C. Possibility exploration / variation / learning
D. Enactment / action / embodiment / feedback
```

Social recognition/practice cuts across all four.

This already suggests why earlier Game research was uneven: R1–R29 mapped many derived nouns but did not vertically decompose these generative mechanisms.
