## 1. Common evidence protocol

### 1.1 Three evidence levels

**C0 — apparatus canary**

One human may repeat a prototype enough to establish that input, rules, feedback, failure and reset are usable. C0 may repair apparatus and perceptual legibility defects. It may **not** rank forms or count as G0-direction evidence by itself.

**C1 — mechanism falsifier**

For an individual-play form, obtain at least three independent play sessions spanning at least two humans before treating a positive mechanism as directional evidence. For social form J, the proof requires real simultaneous human participants; bots or synthetic populations are structural controls only.

C1 is qualitative/causal evidence, not population inference. The question is whether the claimed Player Value mechanism appears under a bounded realization and whether a cheaper explanation accounts for the observation.

**C2 — directional comparison**

Only C1 survivors may enter cross-form narrowing. Compare the mechanism-specific evidence, causal/perceptual legibility, replay/depth indications and conventional burden. Do not collapse unlike Player Values into one score.

### 1.2 What every session records

Record before looking at cross-form results:

- packet ID and prototype revision;
- participant/session identity using a non-sensitive local label;
- first-play vs repeated-play status;
- completion/failure only as apparatus context;
- choices/revisions/retries relevant to the packet mechanism;
- player explanation of what they believed caused success/failure;
- one mechanism-specific post-play question;
- one open question: `What, if anything, made you want to try/change/understand something?`;
- obvious confusion, control failure or presentation defect;
- whether the observed value could be explained by novelty, task completion, puzzle answer exposure, aesthetic polish, or social demand characteristics.

Do not collect demographic or sensitive personal data for this Pre-G0 proof.

### 1.3 Repair boundary

A prototype may be repaired after C0 only when the repair addresses an apparatus/legibility defect that prevents the declared mechanism from being exercised.

A repair must not silently change:

- the Player Value claim;
- the central repeated activity;
- the proof success criterion;
- the falsifier;
- the non-Agent baseline.

If one of those changes, freeze a new packet revision before further play.

### 1.4 Selection boundary

A packet survives C1 when evidence shows the declared value mechanism is causally plausible in the bounded prototype and the main negative controls do not explain it away.

A packet fails when the prototype is mechanically usable but the claimed Player Value does not appear, or appears only because of a substitute mechanism outside the claim.

A packet remains unresolved when apparatus quality is too poor to exercise the claim.

No G0 admission occurs from this document.

# 2. Packet A — PGP-A Precision Traversal

**GameForm:** F01 precision movement / platforming
**Player Value center:** embodied mastery
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

Repeated direct control over a small movement vocabulary can produce self-attributed improvement, flow and a desire to retry because the player can perceive why movement succeeded or failed.

### Cheapest playable

Three short graybox rooms, each completable in under one minute by a competent player:

1. basic horizontal movement + jump;
2. jump timing with one moving or collapsing constraint;
3. a route where the same verbs permit a safer and a faster line.

Use only a tiny stable verb set. Reset must be near-instant. No progression rewards, story or collectible layer.

### C1 evidence

Look for:

- later attempts changing timing/route because of interpreted prior failure;
- improvement the player attributes to their control rather than luck;
- voluntary retry after a non-terminal miss or after already completing a room;
- ability to predict what they would change on the next attempt.

Post-play mechanism question:

> Did you feel you were learning the movement itself? What changed in how you played?

### Falsifiers / negative controls

Fail or narrow the claim if:

- completion improves only from memorizing one scripted timing;
- failures are perceived as arbitrary/collision noise;
- retries are driven only by an explicit score/reward;
- success is mostly puzzle-route discovery rather than motor execution.

### Conventional B0 watch

Control feel, collision, camera/readability, level design, animation/audio feedback and repeated human tuning dominate. Do not infer full-product affordability from a cheap graybox.

# 3. Packet B — PGP-B Rhythm Performance

**GameForm:** F06 rhythm / timing
**Player Value center:** embodied mastery / entrainment
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

Perceiving a stable rhythmic structure and synchronizing action to it can create performance mastery and flow beyond generic reaction-time challenge.

### Cheapest playable

One 45–60 second chart with two input types, a clearly audible beat, and at least one pattern transformation that is learnable rather than random. Immediate timing feedback; no meta-progression.

### C1 evidence

Look for:

- timing accuracy and confidence changing across repeats;
- anticipation of pattern structure rather than visual reaction alone;
- player-described entrainment/flow;
- deliberate recovery after a miss without losing the perceived phrase.

Mechanism question:

> Were you reacting to symbols, or did you start feeling/predicting the rhythm? Give one example.

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if performance is explained by visual reaction alone, audio timing feels decorative, or repeated play does not change anticipation.

### Conventional B0 watch

Audio synchronization, latency, chart design, feedback, music/content production and validation dominate.

# 4. Packet C — PGP-C Tactical Commitment

**GameForm:** F10 tactical / squad commitment
**Player Value center:** strategic mastery
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A small deterministic tactical state can create value from predicting consequences, committing under constraints and revising a plan after attributed outcomes.

### Cheapest playable

One small board/skirmish with:

- 3–5 controllable pieces;
- 2–3 enemy types or hazards;
- a tiny action set;
- no hidden RNG in the primary proof;
- at least two viable plans with materially different risk/position tradeoffs.

### C1 evidence

Look for:

- explicit prediction before commitment;
- plan revision after a consequence;
- more than one viable line recognized by players;
- disagreement/tradeoff rather than a dominant obvious move.

Mechanism question:

> Which commitment mattered most, what did you expect, and what would you do differently now?

### Structural controls

Synthetic search may test reachability, dominance, deadlocks and trivial winning policies. It **cannot** prove strategic Player Value.

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if one line dominates, choices are cosmetic, outcomes cannot be attributed, or the main enjoyment is execution/animation rather than tactical reasoning.

### Conventional B0 watch

Systems, balance, UI/state legibility, encounter/content breadth and validation dominate.

# 5. Packet D — PGP-D Investigation / Causal Reconstruction

**GameForm:** F08 investigation
**Player Value center:** knowledge / inference
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A compact authored evidence set can produce an earned `aha` when the player forms competing hypotheses, tests/reconciles them and reaches a causal explanation that was not simply handed over by the interface.

### Cheapest playable

One 10–20 minute case containing:

- one bounded event to explain;
- 5–8 evidence items with explicit provenance;
- 3 plausible hypotheses early in play;
- at least one misleading-but-fair observation;
- one decisive relation requiring combination of two or more clues;
- a final determination that asks for mechanism/reason, not only culprit selection.

### C1 evidence

Look for:

- multiple live hypotheses before resolution;
- a specific evidence relation causing revision;
- correct causal explanation before reveal;
- surprise that is retrospectively judged fair rather than arbitrary.

Mechanism question:

> What evidence changed your model of what happened, and why?

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if answer follows from one highlighted clue, success is brute-force option elimination, players cannot explain causality after solving, or ambiguity comes from missing information rather than competing explanations.

### Conventional B0 watch

Authored case design, writing, clue consistency, pacing, UI/provenance legibility and fairness testing dominate.

# 6. Packet E — PGP-E Build / Logistics Optimization

**GameForm:** F13 build / automation / management
**Player Value center:** construction / optimization
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

Building a small resource-flow system can create ownership and systems mastery when the player observes a bottleneck, diagnoses it, and intentionally redesigns the system to improve a self-understood objective.

### Cheapest playable

One-screen micro-factory with:

- source, transport, converter and sink;
- 2–3 resource/process types;
- visible throughput/queue state;
- at least two viable layouts;
- one initially non-obvious bottleneck;
- cheap undo/rebuild.

### C1 evidence

Look for diagnosis-driven redesign, ownership of the resulting layout, and explanation of why the new design is better.

Mechanism question:

> What did you change after noticing the bottleneck, and what did you expect that change to do?

### Structural controls

Automated optimization may identify dominance/throughput maxima. It does not prove construction ownership or discovered understanding.

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if the best solution is visually obvious, improvement comes from following tutorial instructions, or throughput numbers change without understandable causal feedback.

### Conventional B0 watch

Simulation/UI, content grammar, balance, scaling, progression and validation dominate.

# 7. Packet F — PGP-F Exploration / Discovery

**GameForm:** F09 exploration / adventure
**Player Value center:** exploration / discovery
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A compact spatial world can produce curiosity and discovery when navigation reveals stable world rules/topology that let players form and test route hypotheses.

### Cheapest playable

A small connected graybox of 4–6 regions with:

- visible landmarks;
- at least two initial route choices;
- one stable traversal/world rule;
- one shortcut or hidden connection discoverable by applying that rule;
- no combat or collectible checklist in the primary proof.

### C1 evidence

Look for spontaneous detours/questions, remembered landmarks, explicit route/world-rule hypotheses and discovery that changes later navigation.

Mechanism question:

> What did you become curious about, and what did you learn about how this place connects or works?

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if movement is only corridor completion, discoveries are arbitrary hidden pixels, curiosity is driven only by explicit markers/rewards, or the world rule does not change navigation.

### Conventional B0 watch

Level topology, landmarks, content density, environmental expression, navigation QA and full-world production dominate.

# 8. Packet G — PGP-G Role / Progression Ownership

**GameForm:** F15 RPG / role progression
**Player Value center:** role / progression
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A mutually exclusive capability commitment can create role ownership when it changes the kinds of plans and consequences available, rather than merely changing numbers.

### Cheapest playable

One compact scenario offered under three mutually exclusive capability paths. Each path must enable at least one qualitatively different solution and block or complicate another. Include one follow-up consequence that reflects the chosen route.

### C1 evidence

Look for players explaining their role choice in terms of preferred possibilities, using different plans, and interpreting follow-up consequences as belonging to their chosen role.

Mechanism question:

> What did your chosen role let you do or make you give up, and did that matter to how you saw the character/playstyle?

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if paths differ only by damage/stat magnitude, converge to the same decision sequence, or players choose solely from obvious power ranking.

### Conventional B0 watch

Content breadth, balance, progression integration, writing/presentation and long-horizon consequence scale dominate.

# 9. Packet H — PGP-H Narrative / Meaning

**GameForm:** F17 narrative
**Player Value center:** story / meaning
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A short authored situation can create responsibility or reinterpretation when the player makes a value-relevant choice, observes an attributable consequence, and then revises the meaning of prior information.

### Cheapest playable

A short text/2D vignette with:

- one clear situation and relationship/value tension;
- two consequential decisions;
- divergent but authored outcomes;
- one later fact/consequence that can change interpretation of an earlier choice;
- no procedural dialogue required.

### C1 evidence

Look for remembered reasons for choices, attribution of consequence to those choices, and post-outcome reinterpretation not reducible to branch-count novelty.

Mechanism question:

> Did anything in the outcome change how you interpreted an earlier choice or character? Explain.

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if outcomes feel arbitrary, choice consequences are unreadable, players optimize obvious utility rather than encounter a meaningful tension, or value comes only from prose quality detached from agency.

### Conventional B0 watch

Writing, editing, presentation, pacing, voice/art/audio if expanded, content branching and qualitative validation dominate.

# 10. Packet I — PGP-I Creation / Expression

**GameForm:** F18 creative sandbox / UGC
**Player Value center:** creation / expression
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

A constrained creation grammar can produce authorship and discovery of personal intent when players compose, inspect and deliberately revise an artifact rather than merely trigger generated output.

### Cheapest playable

A tiny creation canvas with:

- 4–6 primitives;
- a small transform set;
- one meaningful constraint/resource budget;
- undo plus explicit `save revision`;
- side-by-side or sequential comparison of at least two saved revisions.

No generative model in the primary proof.

### C1 evidence

Look for divergent artifacts under the same grammar, deliberate revision tied to an articulated intention, and ownership of choices.

Mechanism question:

> What were you trying to make or express, and what did you intentionally change between revisions?

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if artifacts differ only randomly, players cannot articulate an intention, revision is only error correction, or the tool's presets dominate authorship.

### Conventional B0 watch

Tool UX, expressive primitive design, asset/content library, saving/sharing, moderation if socialized, and craft validation dominate.

# 11. Packet J — PGP-J Human Social Coordination / Deception

**GameForm:** F19 social / multiplayer
**Player Value center:** human social interaction
**Runtime Agent:** none

### Claim

Asymmetric/private information and bounded commitments can create trust, suspicion, coordination and jointly authored stories that are not reducible to solo optimization.

### Cheapest playable

A local/pass-and-play or lightweight web rules prototype for 3–5 humans with:

- small private information packets;
- at least one shared objective or public resource;
- at least one individually conflicting incentive or hidden role/constraint;
- explicit communication/signaling opportunity;
- short rounds with visible adjudication.

No online account infrastructure and no AI participants in the primary proof.

### C1 evidence

Look for belief about other humans changing actions, negotiated commitments, trust/deception attribution and memorable interpersonal episodes created by the rules.

Mechanism question:

> Which other player's action or statement changed what you believed or did, and why?

### Structural controls

Synthetic populations may test deadlocks, dominant coalitions or incentive bugs. They cannot prove trust tension, social meaning or interpersonal story value.

### Falsifiers

Fail or narrow if optimal play ignores communication/other minds, private information is strategically irrelevant, social interaction is merely downtime, or adjudication feels unfair/opaque.

### Conventional B0 watch

At product scale networking, matchmaking, moderation, abuse handling, social balance, community/ops and content support dominate despite a cheap local proof.

# 12. Frozen first implementation wave

Before observing any player result, the first implementation wave is fixed to three deliberately different packets:

```text
PGP-A — Precision Traversal      (embodied mastery)
PGP-D — Investigation            (knowledge / inference)
PGP-I — Creation / Expression    (authorship)
```

Reason for this wave is **method calibration across unlike Player Value**, not a belief that A/D/I are better game directions.

Selection constraints:

- all three can be implemented as small local browser proofs without Runtime Agents;
- none requires online infrastructure or multiple simultaneous participants;
- their decisive human evidence is qualitatively different;
- together they expose whether Ordivon can collect non-systemic Player Value evidence without collapsing everything into simulation metrics.

The wave is not allowed to eliminate B/C/E/F/G/H/J. Those packets remain untested, not inferior.

# 13. After a proof session

For every packet that reaches C1, record in this order:

1. **Player Value observation** — mechanism-specific evidence and falsifier status;
2. **causal/perceptual legibility** — could the player explain the relevant relation?;
3. **replay/depth indication** — did play expose a reason to return/revise, without mistaking novelty for depth?;
4. **conventional B0 observation** — what burden became visible from the prototype?;
5. **Production-Agent delta** — only now estimate which P01–P12 roles reduce or expand work, including review/rework/provenance/tool/model cost;
6. **Runtime-Agent question** — normally remains `none`; open only if a surviving Player Value distinction cannot be realized by the cheapest authored/script/policy/human baseline.

Do not create a scalar portfolio winner from these fields. G0 requires an intentional direction whose evidence makes definition preferable to continued broad search.
