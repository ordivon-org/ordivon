# Ordivon Game Deep Foundations — GDF2-E

## 0. E is a deletion round

A→D produced many useful research constructs.

That is not evidence that they all deserve to survive.

E therefore asks only:

> Which responsibilities remain necessary when the complete GDF2 model is pressured across materially different GameForms?

Corpus:

```text
precision platforming
puzzle/search
rhythm
racing
competitive PvP/team play
roguelike/permadeath
sandbox/self-authored challenge
creative/expressive practice
accessibility/shared control
synthetic controllers
speedrun/community categories
adaptive/live-service version change
```

Canonical artifacts:

```text
evidence/gdf2-e/cross-gameform-matrix.json

evidence/gdf2-e/integration-probes.json
```

# 1. Challenge survives; `Difficulty` as an object does not

Across every form, challenge statements require a question such as:

```text
for whom / which controller?
under which evaluation criterion?
under what rules/state/history/opponent/access condition?
over what horizon?
which outcome/capability distinction is being queried?
```

Nothing in the corpus forces:

```text
Difficulty = one intrinsic scalar field.
```

Platforming can care about a timing threshold.
PvP can care about opponent-conditioned win distributions.
Sandbox can care about a self-authored constraint.
Creative practice can care about continuous quality.
Roguelike analysis can care about outcome risk and recovery cost.

Those are legitimate **projections**, not evidence for one hidden universal number.

---

# 2. E merges the challenge vocabulary into one responsibility

B/C research constructs such as:

```text
ChallengeConditionSpec
FunctionalDifficultyProjection
CapabilityThresholdRegion
CapabilityDiscriminationProfile
FeasibilityRegion
```

remain useful.

But cross-form pressure shows they do not need to be independent foundation nouns.

E provisionally compresses them into:

# **ChallengeAssessmentContract**

Responsibility:

> A challenge/difficulty claim binds current evaluation criterion/authority, attribution target, condition/horizon and relevant control/observation/opponent/history context, then reports only the query-specific property actually identified or bounded by the available structural/observational/interventional evidence.

Therefore:

```text
ChallengeAssessmentContract
!= universal challenge metric.
```

It is a responsibility/claim contract over frozen coordinates.

---

# 3. Measurement constructs stay measurement constructs

`CapabilityOutcomeSurface` was already demoted in C.

E confirms:

```text
CapabilityOutcomeSurface
CapabilityThresholdRegion
CapabilityDiscriminationProfile
AttributionResidualClass
FunctionalDifficultyProjection
```

are research/measurement views.

They can be excellent tools.

They are not new Game primitives.

The surviving law is the separation:

```text
OutcomeProbability
!= threshold/location
!= discrimination
!= risk
!= recovery cost.
```

---

# 4. Precision platforming does not rescue scalar difficulty

A jump can have:

```text
tight timing threshold
high local capability discrimination
cheap checkpoint
```

or the same immediate movement requirement with a severe reset.

Thus even a seemingly simple motor challenge already separates:

```text
capability demand
failure probability
recovery cost.
```

A low-cost checkpoint does not make the jump itself mechanically easier.

---

# 5. Puzzle/search makes history part of challenge scope

Same artifact and rules:

```text
novel puzzle
vs
memorized solution
```

can produce radically different current challenge.

This again argues against content-attached difficulty.

It also attacks D's `LearningOpportunityProfile`.

A puzzle can teach useful world/search information even when:

```text
the observed variable itself is not controllable
there is no same-instance retry
one individual feedback signal is weak/non-diagnostic
```

if information accumulates and changes a future policy.

So several D dimensions are useful prompts but not universal necessary conditions.

---

# 6. `LearningOpportunityProfile` does not survive as a foundation construct

D proposed seven dimensions:

```text
Diagnosticity
AttributionReliability
Actionability
Controllability
ReExposureAccess
ComparabilityMemory
SRVSRelevance
```

E rejects freezing this profile.

Counterexamples:

## Uncontrollable information

A player can learn that an uncontrollable hazard follows a pattern and later route around it.

Therefore:

```text
DirectControllability
is not necessary for learnable information.
```

## No same-instance retry

A one-shot encounter can teach a distinction that transfers to a later related encounter.

Therefore:

```text
Cheap/SameInstanceRetry
is not necessary for learning opportunity.
```

## Distributed diagnosis

No single observation may identify the rule; several weak signals together can.

Therefore:

```text
SingleFeedbackDiagnosticity
is not necessary.
```

E disposition:

```text
LearningOpportunityProfile
→ RETIRE as universal profile.
```

---

# 7. What survives from LearningOpportunity is much smaller

The important anti-collapse law remains:

```text
Failure / Feedback
!= LearningOpportunity
!= ActualLearning.
```

If a researcher claims an opportunity for learning, the claim should say:

```text
what information/distinction became available
and
what future SkillProfile/policy probe could reveal its use.
```

That is enough for Game-side evidence discipline.

The mechanism by which a Human or System actually encodes/learns from it belongs to its owner.

No new Game core object is required.

---

# 8. Rhythm and continuous performance reject binary-failure centrality

A rhythm chart can expose:

```text
timing accuracy
variance
combo stability
score quality
```

without reducing every event to a terminal pass/fail.

One miss can be:

```text
ErrorEvent
```

without being:

```text
FailureAssessment of the song-clear commitment.
```

And zero misses on a saturated easy chart does not establish broad mastery.

Thus:

```text
BinaryFailure is not a prerequisite for Challenge or Mastery.
```

---

# 9. Racing and accessibility pressure the same attribution boundary

Assistance can:

```text
remove steering instability
preserve line-choice challenge
shift attention to strategy
flatten one Human capability dimension
```

rather than simply `reduce difficulty`.

A 2026 Rocket League shared-control study with participants who had upper-limb impairments compared human cooperation and software partial automation. Both modalities reduced the number of controls assigned to the pilot, while the modalities differed in collaboration/autonomy and action attribution could become confused.

The relevance for GDF2 is narrow but strong:

```text
SharedControlOutcome
requires contribution/attribution boundaries.
```

It does not justify any claim about legitimacy or PlayerValue by identity.

---

# 10. PvP/team play exposes a missing attribution law, not a new ontology

Suppose:

```text
Team loses match.
Player A satisfies support-role criterion.
Player B fails another role criterion.
```

Then:

```text
TeamLoss
```

cannot automatically be projected onto:

```text
PlayerAFailure
PlayerASkillDeficit.
```

E therefore adds the guard:

```text
TeamLoss
!= IndividualFailure
!= IndividualSkillDeficit.
```

The same logic applies to:

```text
individual mastery
role mastery
duo/team mastery
joint-controller mastery.
```

Attribution target must be explicit.

Deeper opponent/team strategic structure remains a later Strategy/Social problem rather than expanding GDF2.

---

It remains useful for controlled cross-play and matchup analysis.

But its deeper contents are:

```text
strategy
counterplay
adaptation
nontransitivity
opponent modeling
team interaction
```

which belong downstream.

Therefore:

```text
OpponentInteractionSurface
→ HANDOFF to later Strategy/Counterplay/Balance foundations.
```

GDF2 keeps only:

```text
OpponentChallenge != ContentDifficulty
```

and attribution/condition guards.

---

# 12. Failure survives—but as assessment, not event essence

Every form that meaningfully uses failure preserves D's result:

```text
FailureAssessment
requires
Evaluation criterion + Scope + Horizon.
```

A speedrun can complete while failing a time target.
A rhythm player can miss a note while clearing a song.
A team can lose while one role criterion is satisfied.
A roguelike character can die while meta-progression succeeds.

Thus FailureAssessment survives strongly.

---

# 13. `PursuitTerminalityClaim` does not deserve independent core status

D separated terminality from failure correctly.

E asks whether it needs its own foundation object.

Given:

```text
FailureAssessment
current pursuit/evaluation identity
horizon
RecoveryTransitionSet
```

we can ask:

> Is there any authoritative continuation preserving this pursuit?

The terminality answer is a derived query.

Therefore:

```text
PursuitTerminalityClaim
→ DERIVED.
```

The distinction survives; the noun does not need core status.

---

# 14. Recovery is strong but optional

Roguelike/permadeath and checkpoint games make recovery structure central.

Sandbox/creative/open-ended practice can have challenge without any authoritative failure/recovery transition at all.

Therefore:

```text
RecoveryTransitionSet
```

is not a mandatory field on every Game challenge.

But whenever failure/recovery is being analyzed, the structural responsibility is strong:

```text
represent authoritative continuation/retry/reset/revival/alternate transitions
separately from any scalar severity/value projection.
```

This responsibility is merged with FailureAssessment below.

---

# 15. E provisionally compresses failure/recovery into one responsibility

# **FailureRecoveryContract**

Responsibility:

> Failure is evaluation/scope/horizon-relative nonattainment; where post-outcome recovery matters, represent authoritative continuation transitions separately from terminality and from any query-specific severity/cost projection.

This preserves:

```text
Failure != Error != Punishment != Loss != Death
Failure != Terminality
RecoveryTransitionSet != UniversalSeverity
```

without freezing a forest of separate objects.

---

# 16. Mastery survives only if it can be falsified without circular rescue

D's `MasteryClaimSpec` is powerful.

Its danger is exactly that power.

If the analyst may choose any scope after seeing the evidence, then every good performance can be rescued as:

```text
"mastery of exactly the thing the player just succeeded at."
```

That is scientifically empty.

E therefore adds a non-circularity requirement.

---

# 17. Mastery non-circularity guard

A mastery claim's:

```text
evaluation criterion
claimed condition/probe region
```

must have **independent provenance** from the target performance used as mastery evidence,

or the claim must be validated on:

```text
held-out / independent ProbeTransformations.
```

Rejected construction:

```text
Define mastered region
=
all conditions where Player X scored >= .90.

Observe Player X scored >= .90 there.

Therefore Player X mastered the region.
```

This is tautological.

Acceptable evidence can instead use:

```text
predeclared community category/criterion
independently derived Game/evaluation target
held-out perturbation/transfer conditions
independent validation population/run set
```

as appropriate to the claim.

---

# 18. StarCraft transfer pressure supports currentness-bound mastery

Within-expertise research on StarCraft 2 has directly examined what happens to skilled players when the domain changes through major game updates. The broader lesson GDF2 consumes is not a universal effect direction; it is that expertise/mastery evidence is tied to a domain/condition and transfer after a change is an empirical question.

Therefore:

```text
Mastery(version/context A)
!= Mastery(version/context B) by identity.
```

The change must be represented as a ProbeTransformation/currentness boundary.

---

# 19. Telemetry also blocks score-only mastery

Large StarCraft telemetry studies demonstrate that expertise can involve multiple behavioral markers whose predictive importance changes across expertise levels.

GDF2's conservative inference:

```text
TotalScore/Rank
can be useful evidence
but
!= complete Mastery structure.
```

This fits GDF1's non-scalar SkillProfile rather than adding a new expertise scalar.

---

# 20. Creative/open-ended mastery survives the non-circularity test

No terminal completion is required.

A bounded claim can use:

```text
independently declared evaluator/criterion
+
held-out quality/control probes
```

and support:

```text
mastery over this declared expressive region.
```

It still does not support:

```text
maximum possible creativity
exhaustive domain mastery
universal PlayerValue.
```

So open-ended cases do not break mastery—they force narrower claim semantics.

---

# 21. Accessibility/shared control also forces mastery attribution

If a Human+assist joint controller satisfies the criterion while the Human after assist removal does not, then the evidence supports a joint-system claim, not a Human-independent claim.

Thus:

```text
JointControllerMastery
!= HumanIndependentMastery.
```

If the target of practice is explicitly:

```text
skillfully operating the coupled Human+assist system,
```

then that is a legitimate distinct mastery claim.

No contradiction exists.

---

# 22. Synthetic mastery is legitimate—but only for the synthetic target

A synthetic policy can satisfy a declared mastery criterion across controlled perturbations.

That can support:

```text
CapabilityMasteryClaim(synthetic policy family).
```

It cannot create:

```text
Human mastery
Human subjective competence
Human PlayerValue
```

evidence.

This is another reason the attribution target belongs inside the claim contract.

---

# 23. E provisionally compresses mastery into one responsibility

# **MasteryClaimContract**

Responsibility:

> A mastery claim is a scoped, independently anchored evidence claim over frozen SkillProfile under declared condition/probe/attribution/currentness boundaries; demonstrated performance, completion, score, zero errors, social rank and assisted outcomes are evidence or neighboring targets rather than Mastery identity.

This merges:

```text
MasteryClaimSpec
CapabilityMasteryClaim
DemonstratedMasteryEvidence
```

without losing their important separations.

This can later be shared with other Ordivon foundations rather than copied into every domain ontology.

# 27. What happened to LearningOpportunity?

It does **not** become a fourth core responsibility.

The surviving law is sufficient:

```text
Failure/Feedback
!= LearningOpportunity
!= ActualLearning.
```

Any concrete learning-opportunity analysis can be represented as a research claim about information/distinction exposure and future probes, with Human/System owning the actual learning mechanism.

This avoids duplicating Human learning foundations inside Game.
