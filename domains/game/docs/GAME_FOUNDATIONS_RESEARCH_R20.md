# Ordivon Game Foundations Research — R20 Creation, Creativity, Expression, Authorship and Style

## 0. Status and boundary

R20 continues the foundation programme after R1–R19. It is a **research record**, not a product specification and not a G0–G8 promotion.

R19 asked:

> How do multiple Subjects strategically alter one another's reachable futures?

R20 asks:

> How does a participant deliberately bring new artifacts, forms, meanings or possibilities into existence, and when does that process create player value rather than merely content output?

The overloaded terms under attack are:

```text
Creation
Creativity
Construction
Composition
Expression
Authorship
Customization
Style
Taste
Tool
Material
Constraint
Grammar
Artifact
Ownership
Attribution
Curation
Co-creation
Generation
```

The starting warnings are:

```text
Creation != Creativity
Generation != Creation by the player
Construction != Expression
Customization != Authorship
Selection != automatically trivial
More options != More expression
More output != More creativity
AI contribution != Player authorship
Authorship != Legal ownership
Style != cosmetic skin
```

No product is selected by R20.

# 1. External anchors and what they do — and do not — establish

R20 triangulates creativity research, design studies, constructionism, creativity-support HCI, mixed-initiative systems, computational creativity and recent human–GenAI co-creation work. None is adopted wholesale as the Game ontology.

## 1.1 Creativity is not one object

Mel Rhodes' classic 1961 synthesis grouped creativity research around four recurring perspectives: Person, Process, Press/environment and Product. This remains a useful warning against treating creativity as merely an intrinsic property of one output or one model.

Game lesson:

```text
Creative product quality
!= creative process quality
!= creator capability
!= creative environment quality
```

A system can generate unusual products while providing a poor creative process to the player.

## 1.2 Novelty is insufficient

A long creativity-research tradition evaluates creative products through some combination of novelty/originality and appropriateness/usefulness/value relative to a domain or task. Amabile's componential work also emphasizes domain knowledge, creativity-relevant processes and motivation rather than novelty alone.

Game lesson:

```text
Randomness can increase novelty
without increasing creativity.
```

The relevant value criterion can be plural and context-specific:

```text
functional
expressive
aesthetic
narrative
social
strategic
personal
```

R18 therefore still applies: creative evaluation should not automatically collapse all values into one scalar.

## 1.3 Creative systems operate in possibility spaces

Wiggins' formalization of Boden's creative-systems ideas is useful because it distinguishes a structured conceptual/search space from the processes used to traverse or alter it. Combination, exploration and transformation are different ways novelty can arise.

Game lesson:

```text
Creativity is not equivalent to sampling more outputs.
```

A creative mechanic can let players:

```text
combine existing primitives
explore an expressive grammar
transform the grammar / rule space itself
```

These are materially different affordances.

## 1.4 Constructionism makes the artifact-process relation explicit

Papert's constructionist tradition and later MIT work emphasize learning and meaning-making through constructing shareable artifacts. Resnick's Creative Learning Spiral emphasizes imagining, creating, playing, sharing and reflecting; his design language of low floors, high ceilings and wide walls is especially relevant to open creative systems.

Game lesson:

```text
Artifact output is not the whole value.
The iterative making process can itself be play, learning and identity formation.
```

A blank infinite canvas is not automatically more empowering than a structured system with a low entry barrier, deep compositional ceiling and many meaningful directions.

## 1.5 Creativity-support tools should empower rather than merely automate

Shneiderman's creativity-support work asks how interactive tools can enable more people to create and discover more effectively. Horvitz's mixed-initiative work rejects a simple direct-manipulation-versus-automation binary and instead studies how user control and automated services can be interleaved.

Game lesson:

```text
Automation is not the opposite of creative agency.
The important question is which decisions are automated.
```

Automating tedious realization can expand creative agency; automating the value-bearing decisions can remove the intended creative game.

## 1.6 Mixed-initiative co-creativity distributes initiative dynamically

Mixed-initiative creative-interface research describes human and computer as potentially suggesting, producing, evaluating, modifying and selecting outputs in response to one another. Systems such as Loose Ends show that mixed initiative can support a feeling of coauthorship when the interaction structure helps players manage and close creative commitments rather than merely generate more material.

Game lesson:

```text
Co-creation is a distribution of creative roles,
not simply “human prompt + AI output.”
```

The distribution can change over time and need not be symmetric.

## 1.7 Creative intent can co-evolve with the artifact

Dorst and Cross' design-process work provides empirical support for a co-evolution view: problem/framing and solution space can develop together during design.

Game lesson:

```text
Intent does not always precede making.
Making can discover the intent.
```

This is important for sandbox play, improvisation, tinkering and generative co-creation. A system that demands a fully specified goal before allowing creation can destroy part of the creative process.

## 1.8 Recent GenAI evidence makes authorship/ownership a first-class design variable

A 2026 Harvard Business School field experiment on GenAI-assisted ideation reports that dedicated human-only ideation time, especially beginning independently, increased participants' perceived ownership of ideas; participants could also gain ownership by injecting domain expertise into AI-sparked ideas, while ownership could decrease when AI elaboration diluted domain-specific human knowledge. Treat this as current empirical evidence, not a universal law.

Recent Creativity & Cognition work likewise reports that physical or direct creative contribution can increase felt authorship, and preliminary 2026 work suggests that perceived authorship can track the **locus of meaningful decision-making** more than the raw proportion of machine-generated surface content.

Game lesson:

```text
SurfaceBytesByHuman
is a poor proxy for
PlayerAuthorship.
```

# 2. Core term separation

| Term | Working meaning | Not equivalent to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Creation** | Deliberate organization/transformation of material, state, symbols or behavior into a form whose structure depends meaningfully on participant choices. | Creativity, generation, any state change. |
| **Creativity** | Capacity/process/product relation that produces meaningfully novel and contextually valuable/appropriate possibilities or forms. | Randomness, productivity, creation count. |
| **Construction** | Creation primarily through assembling/configuring components under structural rules and constraints. | Expression, creativity, crafting menu completion. |
| **Composition** | Arrangement of parts/relations into a coherent or intended form. | Construction only; composition may be symbolic, musical, narrative or spatial. |
| **Expression** | Choice or created form that communicates/reifies identity, stance, emotion, taste, meaning or perspective. | Novelty, creativity, cosmetic variation alone. |
| **Authorship** | Causal and normative relation in which a participant meaningfully determines, endorses and bears responsibility for aspects of a work's form/meaning. | Legal ownership, raw labor share, button press. |
| **Customization** | Selection/modification within a pre-structured parameter or option space to adapt an artifact/identity/experience. | Full creation or creativity. |
| **Style** | Relatively stable, recognizable pattern/distribution of choices across comparable creative situations. | One skin, one genre label, one prompt adjective. |
| **Taste** | Evaluative discrimination/preference over forms that guides selection, critique, revision and rejection. | Objective quality scalar. |
| **Tool** | Capability surface that expands, compresses or transforms how intent can act on material/form. | Neutral conduit. |
| **Material** | The manipulable substrate and its affordances/resistances. | Passive content stock. |
| **Constraint** | Boundary/cost/rule that removes some possibilities and thereby can structure meaning among remaining ones. | Always-negative restriction. |
| **Grammar** | Rules/combinators that determine which constructions are valid, interpretable or causally meaningful. | UI syntax only. |
| **Artifact** | A created form treated as an inspectable, usable, revisable, performable or interpretable object/trace. | Necessarily permanent physical object. |
| **Attribution** | Assignment/belief about who contributed what and why the artifact has its present form. | Ownership or authorship by itself. |
| **Ownership** | Rights/claims/control/possession relation over an artifact, as already separated in R14. | Authorship or psychological identification. |
| **Curation** | Selection, ordering, framing and contextualization of existing/generated material. | Automatically low authorship. |
| **Co-creation** | Creative process in which multiple participants make independently consequential contributions to evolving form/intent/evaluation. | Mere tool use or outsourced execution. |
| **Generation** | Mechanism producing candidate content/form from inputs/context. | Creation, creativity or authorship by itself. |

The strongest compact separation is:

```text
Creation = making / organizing form
Creativity = novelty-with-value across process/product/context
Expression = making a perspective/identity legible
Authorship = meaningful causal responsibility for form/meaning
Customization = bounded modification/selection
Generation = candidate production mechanism
```

# 3. Creation — not every state change is making

Every game action changes state. If Creation meant merely `State → State'`, the category would become useless.

R20 therefore proposes:

```text
Creation =
deliberate organization or transformation
of material / state / signs / behavior
into a Form
whose internal organization is meaningfully contingent
on participant choices.
```

The key phrase is **internal organization**.

Compare:

```text
Press door button
→ door opens
```

versus:

```text
Arrange blocks
→ bridge with player-chosen geometry
```

Both change state. Only the second creates a form whose structure carries a nontrivial trace of participant decisions.

## 3.1 Minimal creation test

Ask:

> If the participant had made materially different meaningful choices, could the resulting form have been materially different in a way that remains inspectable, usable, interpretable or performable?

If not, the action may be transformation/activation rather than creation.

## 3.2 Creation need not be permanent

Creation can produce:

```text
persistent artifact
spatial construction
machine/configuration
story
song
performance
improvised tactic pattern
ritual
character appearance
shared arrangement
```

An ephemeral performance can still be created if it has organized form and attributable choice.

Therefore:

```text
Artifact
can mean persistent object
or structured performative / symbolic trace.
```

## 3.3 Creation need not be creative

A player can faithfully reproduce a blueprint.

```text
Creation ✓
Creativity maybe not
Authorship partial / derivative depending context
```

Likewise an automatic generator can produce a novel artifact with little player authorship.

# 4. Artifact, Form and Trace

## 4.1 Artifact

An Artifact is not defined by physical persistence alone.

For Game purposes:

```text
Artifact =
a produced Form that can become an object of
perception, use, revision, interpretation, memory or social reference.
```

Artifact types include:

```text
physical/spatial
mechanical/systemic
symbolic/textual
visual
musical
performative
social/institutional
procedural/rule-based
hybrid
```

## 4.2 Form

```text
Form =
the internally differentiated organization
of an artifact / performance / configuration.
```

The fact that a house exists is State.

Its:

```text
shape
layout
materials
entrances
style
function
symbolism
```

are aspects of Form.

## 4.3 Trace

A created form becomes historically/socially powerful when it leaves a trace:

```text
Creation
→ Artifact / Trace
→ later use / recognition / modification
→ History / Identity / Institution / Resource
```

This directly reconnects R13.

# 5. Creativity — more than novelty

## 5.1 Working definition

R20 uses a deliberately broad working definition:

```text
Creativity =
a process/capability/product relation in which
participants generate, discover, combine, explore or transform
possibilities in ways that are meaningfully novel
and valuable/appropriate relative to a context.
```

This definition is not intended as a final universal psychology.

Its purpose is to prevent three collapses:

```text
Creativity != randomness
Creativity != output volume
Creativity != creation itself
```

## 5.2 Novel relative to what?

Novelty needs a reference frame:

```text
new to this player
new to this run
new within this local community
new within the game's possibility space
new within a cultural/domain history
```

These are different.

A first-time player's discovery can have high personal creative value even if millions of players made the same structure before.

Therefore:

```text
PersonalNovelty
!= HistoricalNovelty
```

## 5.3 Appropriate / valuable relative to what?

Creative value can be:

```text
functional
expressive
aesthetic
surprising
socially meaningful
narratively fitting
strategically effective
personally satisfying
```

Not every creative game needs external expert validation.

## 5.4 Creativity can lie in process even when product is ordinary

A player may reach a common-looking artifact through:

```text
original reframing
interesting experimentation
learning
self-expression
social collaboration
```

So product novelty alone can miss player value.

# 6. Creative possibility spaces

R1 treated Game forms as multidimensional spaces. R16 treated topology as reachability. R20 now applies possibility-space language to creation.

```text
CreativePossibilitySpace =
Forms reachable from available
Materials × Tools × Grammar × Constraints × Skills × Time
```

This is not a literal Cartesian product in every implementation; it is a conceptual decomposition.

## 6.1 Combination

```text
known parts
→ unfamiliar arrangement
```

Examples:

```text
blocks → architecture
cards → deck
modules → machine
words → story
notes → melody
```

## 6.2 Exploration

```text
existing grammar
→ discover unusual/high-value regions
```

## 6.3 Transformation

```text
change the grammar / operators / constraints
→ create previously unreachable form families
```

Examples:

```text
modding
rule editing
custom scripting
new material systems
new instrument behavior
```

This distinction matters because:

```text
More samples inside same narrow space
!= wider creative agency
```

# 7. Construction — compositional making under structural semantics

## 7.1 Working definition

```text
Construction =
creation through assembling, arranging or configuring
components under structural relations and constraints.
```

Construction usually exposes:

```text
parts
connections
support/dependency
spatial relations
capacity
causal interactions
```

## 7.2 Construction can be instrumental or expressive

A bridge can be:

```text
pure optimization:
minimum cost that spans gap
```

or:

```text
creative construction:
functional + aesthetic + symbolic + personal form
```

The difference is not the medium. It is whether meaningful plural form choices survive after requirements are satisfied.

## 7.3 Crafting is not automatically construction

A recipe menu:

```text
2 wood + 1 stone
→ fixed axe
```

is production/transformation.

It becomes construction when the player meaningfully determines structure/configuration rather than merely invokes a recipe.

Therefore:

```text
CraftingSystem != CreativeSystem
```

## 7.4 Composition

Composition is broader than physical construction:

```text
Composition =
meaningful arrangement of parts and relations into form.
```

Stories, music, dialogue, decks, layouts and visual scenes can all be composed.

# 8. Expression — choice as communicative/identity-bearing form

## 8.1 Working definition

```text
Expression =
a choice or created form that makes some aspect of
identity, stance, emotion, taste, interpretation or meaning legible.
```

Expression does not require originality.

Wearing a common red uniform can express faction identity.

## 8.2 Meaningful expression requires semantic contrast

If all options are perceived as equivalent, choice count does not create expression.

```text
ExpressiveAgency
requires
meaningfully interpretable alternatives
+ attributable choice.
```

Therefore:

```text
1000 arbitrary sliders
can provide less expression
than 5 socially meaningful choices.
```

## 8.3 Expression can be direct or emergent

Direct:

```text
choose clothing
write dialogue
paint emblem
name settlement
```

Emergent:

```text
repeated playstyle
architecture habits
resource ethics
who the player protects
what the player refuses to automate
```

A player's identity can become visible through behavior without an explicit “personality editor.”

## 8.4 Expression may target self or audience

```text
Self-expression:
“This feels like me.”

Social expression:
“I want others to read this about me.”

Role expression:
“This expresses the character I am playing.”
```

These can conflict.

# 9. Customization — bounded personalization, not automatically authorship

## 9.1 Working definition

```text
Customization =
selection or modification inside a pre-structured option/parameter space
in order to adapt or personalize an artifact/identity/experience.
```

Examples:

```text
avatar appearance
loadout
house furnishing
UI layout
vehicle paint
voice/tone options
```

## 9.2 Customization can be expressive without being creative

Selecting a preset hairstyle can express identity even if the player invented none of its geometry.

Thus:

```text
Expression ✓
Customization ✓
Creation weak/partial
Creativity not required
```

## 9.3 Meaningful customization

Customization becomes valuable when differences affect:

```text
self-recognition
social recognition
future behavior
world response
role fantasy
aesthetic coherence
strategic function
```

If options are numerous but indistinguishable, customization depth is low.

## 9.4 Wide walls matter more than raw option count

A good customization system supports materially different identities or approaches rather than a combinatorial explosion of trivial parameter values.

```text
CustomizationCount != ExpressiveBreadth
```

# 10. Authorship — who meaningfully shaped the work?

Authorship is one of R20's hardest concepts because it mixes causal, psychological, social and legal language.

R20 intentionally isolates **game-relevant authorship** from legal doctrine.

## 10.1 Working definition

```text
Authorship =
a causal + normative relation in which a participant
meaningfully shapes and endorses aspects of an artifact's form/meaning
and can reasonably be treated as responsible for those aspects.
```

This includes two ideas:

```text
Causal contribution:
my decisions changed the work

Normative identification/responsibility:
I stand behind / claim / answer for those decisions
```

## 10.2 Authorial causality

A useful counterfactual test:

> If this participant had made meaningfully different decisions, would important properties of the artifact have been systematically different?

If yes, authorial causality is stronger.

If replacing the participant with a generic “accept default” policy yields approximately the same artifact distribution, player authorship is weak even if the player technically clicked every button.

## 10.3 Authorship is not proportional to keystrokes or pixels

One participant can define:

```text
concept
structure
constraints
selection criteria
critical revision
```

while another realizes surface details.

The first can still have major authorship despite producing fewer raw bytes.

Therefore:

```text
Authorship != LaborShare
Authorship != SurfaceGenerationShare
```

## 10.4 Authorship profile, not one number

A useful non-scalar profile is:

```text
AuthorshipProfile = {
  Framing / Intent,
  Constraint Setting,
  Structural Specification,
  Material Realization,
  Selection / Curation,
  Evaluation / Taste,
  Revision,
  Integration,
  Final Commitment / Publication
}
```

Different media distribute these roles differently.

## 10.5 Authorship can emerge through iteration

A player can start from an AI-generated seed and later gain substantial authorship by:

```text
reframing
rejecting
restructuring
injecting domain knowledge
combining
editing
committing
```

Likewise a human-originated seed can lose felt authorship if automated elaboration overwhelms the decisions the human cared about.

This is consistent with recent GenAI ownership evidence, but remains a hypothesis to test in Game contexts.

# 11. Attribution, ownership and provenance

## 11.1 Attribution

```text
Attribution =
who is believed/recorded to have contributed which creative decisions.
```

Attribution can be:

```text
accurate
partial
disputed
socially negotiated
institutionally recorded
```

## 11.2 Ownership

R14 already separated:

```text
Possession
Access
Control
Ownership/Rights
```

R20 adds:

```text
Authorship != Ownership
```

A player can author a sculpture but sell it.

A guild can own a structure built by one player.

An AI system can contribute to form without holding game-world property rights.

## 11.3 Psychological creative ownership

Players may also experience:

```text
“This is mine because I made it.”
```

This subjective ownership can matter to attachment, pride, persistence and willingness to share/defend the artifact even when formal game-world ownership differs.

## 11.4 Provenance

For collaborative/generative systems, artifact provenance can become functional state:

```text
which parts came from whom
which constraints were used
which source artifact was remixed
which model/tool transformed what
which revisions changed meaning
```

Provenance supports:

```text
credit
trust
repair
ownership disputes
learning
remix history
```

and reconnects R17 Information and R13 History.

# 12. Curation — selection can be creative

A common mistake is:

```text
“AI generated it, user only selected it, therefore user did nothing creative.”
```

Sometimes true. Sometimes false.

## 12.1 Working definition

```text
Curation =
selection + ordering + exclusion + framing + contextualization
of candidate material.
```

## 12.2 When selection has authorial weight

Selection matters when:

```text
candidate set is meaningfully diverse
criteria reflect participant taste/intent
rejection is consequential
ordering/context changes interpretation
selection constrains later creation
```

A film editor, museum curator, DJ or deckbuilder can exercise strong authorship through selection/composition without generating all source material.

## 12.3 Low-agency curation

If the system shows three near-identical outputs and the user accepts the first acceptable one:

```text
selection effort low
counterfactual influence low
intent expression low
```

Authorship may remain weak.

Therefore:

```text
Selection != automatically authorship
but
Selection != automatically trivial
```

# 13. Style — persistent choice structure

## 13.1 Working definition

```text
Style =
a relatively stable and recognizable distribution/pattern
of choices across comparable form-making situations.
```

Style can involve:

```text
shape
rhythm
composition
language
color
material
problem framing
preferred constraints
repeated motifs
interaction patterns
```

## 13.2 Style can be intentional or emergent

A player may deliberately develop a style, or other players may recognize one retrospectively.

```text
RepeatedChoice
→ Pattern
→ Recognition
→ Style / Identity
```

This connects R13 history to R18 identity.

## 13.3 Style is not one asset tag

Applying “cyberpunk” to a generated image is not equivalent to developing a personal style.

Style has stronger identity value when it reflects repeated decisions and constraints over time.

## 13.4 AI can imitate style without transferring authorship

Similarity of surface distribution does not establish causal authorship or identity continuity.

```text
StyleSimilarity
!= SameAuthor
!= SameIntent
```

# 14. Taste — evaluation is part of creation

Generation receives disproportionate attention because it produces visible output.

But creation also requires deciding:

```text
what is good enough
what fits
what to reject
what to preserve
what to simplify
what to revise
what matters
```

R20 calls this broad evaluative function **Taste**.

## 14.1 Working definition

```text
Taste =
contextual evaluative discrimination over forms,
used to select, reject, critique, combine and revise.
```

Taste can be:

```text
personal
social/domain learned
functional
ethical
aesthetic
role-specific
```

## 14.2 Taste is not a universal score

Two forms can be incomparable across plural values.

```text
beautiful but inefficient
funny but narratively incoherent
functional but identity-inappropriate
novel but ugly by creator's taste
```

R18's late-scalarization rule applies directly.

## 14.3 Creative quality often depends on rejection

```text
GenerateMore
!= CreateBetter
```

A powerful system may create value by helping the player:

```text
compare
inspect
undo
branch
critique
revisit
```

rather than merely increasing output volume.

# 15. Tool, material, affordance, resistance and grammar

## 15.1 Tool

A Tool is not a neutral pipe between intention and result.

```text
Tool =
a capability surface that maps participant action
into transformations of material/form.
```

Tools influence:

```text
resolution
speed
reversibility
precision
reachable forms
feedback
skill expression
error cost
```

## 15.2 Material

```text
Material =
manipulable substrate with affordances and resistances.
```

Examples:

```text
blocks
terrain
cards
words
notes
color fields
code
physical resources
social conventions
```

Good creative play can come from negotiating material resistance rather than removing it.

## 15.3 Grammar

```text
Grammar =
rules/combinators that make some transformations valid,
interpretable or causally meaningful.
```

Examples:

```text
snap-grid rules
music scale/rhythm
programming syntax
craft connection sockets
narrative continuity
physics joints
card-deck legality
```

## 15.4 Constraint can be generative

Constraint does two things simultaneously:

```text
removes possibilities
and
creates contrast/meaning among remaining possibilities
```

Without constraint:

```text
anything can happen
→ differences can become hard to interpret
→ commitment weakens
→ blank-page problem grows
```

Therefore:

```text
Freedom != CreativeAgency
```

R11's agency distinction survives again.

## 15.5 The creative-triad heuristic

A useful provisional structure is:

```text
Material gives possibility/resistance.
Grammar gives compositional meaning.
Tool gives actionable transformation.
```

Creative play emerges from the participant learning and exploiting their interaction.

# 16. Low floors, high ceilings and wide walls

Resnick's language is unusually compatible with our possibility-space model.

## Low floor

```text
How cheaply can meaningful making begin?
```

## High ceiling

```text
How far can skill/structure/complexity develop before the medium saturates?
```

## Wide walls

```text
How many meaningfully different directions/identities/forms
can be pursued within the medium?
```

This is superior to raw option count.

A strong creative game often wants:

```text
Low Entry Cost
+ High Mastery Ceiling
+ Broad Semantic Directions
```

not:

```text
Infinite undifferentiated parameters
```

# 17. The creative loop

R4's general game loop can now be specialized.

A useful R20 loop is:

```text
Impulse / Question / Intent
        ↕
Frame / Imagine
        ↓
Choose Material / Constraint / Tool
        ↓
Make / Compose / Transform
        ↓
Perceive Result
        ↓
Evaluate / Feel / Compare
        ↓
Revise / Branch / Reject / Keep
        ↓
Use / Perform / Share / Place in World
        ↓
New feedback / meaning / possibility
        ↺
```

The two-way arrow at the beginning matters:

```text
Intent shapes Artifact
Artifact feedback reshapes Intent
```

This is the creative co-evolution principle.

## 17.1 Tinkering

Creation can begin without a clear goal:

```text
Manipulate material
→ notice possibility
→ form intention
→ pursue / revise
```

This makes experimentation itself productive rather than “failure to specify prompt correctly.”

## 17.2 Closure

A creative loop still needs local closure:

```text
commit
publish
place
perform
ship
save version
```

Otherwise infinite revision can destroy satisfaction.

This reconnects R7/R8's local-closure principle.

# 18. Creative Agency

R11 defined Agency as selection among meaningfully different future trajectories.

R20 specializes it:

```text
CreativeAgency =
ability to intentionally shape meaningful properties
of a resulting Form among materially different alternatives.
```

A provisional heuristic:

```text
CreativeAgencyValue ≈
MeaningfulFormAlternatives
× CausalInfluence
× Feedback / Legibility
× Iterability
× Attribution
```

Do not treat this multiplication as a proven metric.

## 18.1 Counterfactual authorship test

Ask:

```text
Which important properties of the artifact
would differ if the player's decisions differed?
```

The answer reveals where creative agency actually resides.

## 18.2 Initiative is not the same as agency

An AI may take initiative by proposing ideas while the human retains authorial agency through:

```text
framing
selection
constraint-setting
revision
integration
final commitment
```

Conversely, a human can issue many prompts while having little agency if the model's outputs barely respond to the distinctions the user cares about.

# 19. Playable Creation and Playable Expression

R20 adds two useful members to the Playable-X family.

## 19.1 Playable Creation

```text
PlayableCreation =
a making process in which player choices causally shape
artifact/form properties, feedback supports a usable mental model,
and the resulting differences matter to later use, interpretation,
identity, world state or continued creation.
```

## 19.2 Playable Expression

```text
PlayableExpression =
expressive differences that the player can intentionally produce
and that remain legible to self, system or audience.
```

## 19.3 Why both are needed

A complex procedural generator can create impressive artifacts with low PlayableCreation.

A simple avatar badge system can have strong PlayableExpression despite low creativity.

Thus:

```text
TechnicalCreativePower
!= PlayerCreativeAgency
```

# 21. Cross-form falsification tests

## 21.1 Fixed crafting recipe

```text
inputs → predefined output
```

This is production/transformation, not necessarily creative construction.

It can still support strategic resource play from R14.

## 21.2 Modular weapon/build construction

If modules alter function, geometry and trade-offs in many viable combinations:

```text
Construction ✓
CreativeAgency possible
Expression possible
```

If one optimal configuration dominates, the system may collapse into optimization rather than open creation.

## 21.3 Minecraft-like building

Blocks provide:

```text
material
clear grammar
spatial constraints
high combinatorial ceiling
persistent world trace
```

Creative value can come from construction, exploration of form, social display and history without autonomous Agents.

This is a strong counterexample to any Game ontology centered on intelligent Subjects.

## 21.4 Factory / optimization builder

A factory layout can involve creation while primary player value comes from optimization/efficiency rather than expression.

```text
Creation ✓
Construction ✓
Creativity maybe
Expression optional
```

Do not label every builder game a creative-expression game.

## 21.5 Avatar creator

Preset selection can create strong expression but weak authorship of source assets.

```text
Customization ✓
Expression ✓
Authorship of assembled identity partial
Creativity optional
```

## 21.6 Deckbuilding

The player does not author the cards, but composition/curation can have strong authorship of the **deck as artifact**.

```text
SourceMaterial authored elsewhere
DeckComposition authored by player
```

This proves that authorship can occur at a higher compositional layer than material generation.

## 21.7 Level editor / UGC

A level editor often creates strong authorship because the player controls:

```text
framing
structure
placement
challenge grammar
iteration
playtesting
publication
```

The engine still performs most low-level realization.

This is a direct counterexample to “machine executes most bytes, therefore machine authored the level.”

## 21.8 Narrative roleplay

In tabletop-like or freeform narrative play, created artifacts may be:

```text
character identity
shared fiction
scene
relationship
story history
```

Creation can be social and ephemeral before becoming retained text/state.

## 21.9 SillyTavern-like interaction

A player may exercise authorship through:

```text
persona framing
world premise
scene direction
selection/rejection
relationship trajectory
revision
interpretation
```

while the model realizes most prose.

But if the user only presses “continue,” player authorship can become weak even if the output feels personalized.

Therefore:

```text
GeneratedPersonalization
!= PlayerAuthorship
```

## 21.10 Text-to-image generation

Prompt-only interaction can range from low to high authorship depending on:

```text
intent specificity
iteration
constraint control
selection
direct manipulation
composition
editing
integration
```

A one-shot generic prompt may be closer to commissioning/curation.

A tightly iterated workflow with sketches, masks, layout control and revision can create stronger authorial causality.

## 21.11 AI-assisted writing

AI can support:

```text
brainstorming
critique
variation
translation of intent into prose
revision
```

But interventions at different stages have different authorship effects. Writers may care most about owning planning, phrasing, or revision depending on the domain and identity of the work.

## 21.12 Co-op building

Shared construction introduces R19 again:

```text
creative cooperation
coordination
ownership conflict
credit
revision authority
```

The artifact becomes both creative object and strategic/social object.

# 23. Assistance, delegation, co-creation and autonomy

Do not treat all human–AI creative systems as one category.

## Assistance

```text
Human owns creative trajectory;
AI reduces local friction or supplies optional support.
```

## Delegated realization

```text
Human specifies important intent/constraints;
AI realizes substantial surface/detail work.
```

## Co-creation

```text
Human and AI both make independently consequential contributions
that modify the evolving artifact or creative direction.
```

## Autonomous generation

```text
System defines/chooses substantial goals, search and evaluation
without needing human creative decisions.
```

## Curation of autonomous generation

```text
AI generates independently;
human creates value through selection/framing/contextualization.
```

None is inherently superior.

The correct mode depends on which creative decisions are intended gameplay.

# 24. The automation boundary in creative games

A direct extension of earlier principles is:

```text
Automate realization friction.
Do not automatically automate the intended creative question.
```

Suppose the intended player value is architecture.

Good automation may handle:

```text

repetitive placement
structural preview
material count
lighting bake
```

Potentially harmful automation may decide:

```text

spatial hierarchy
signature silhouette
meaningful trade-offs
```

unless the game is about high-level direction/curation rather than direct architecture.

Therefore the automation boundary is experience-relative.

# 30. Major collapse / failure modes

## 30.1 Creation = StateChange collapse

Failure: every action becomes “creative.”

Result: the concept loses discriminative power.

## 30.2 Creation = Creativity collapse

Failure: anything built is called creative.

Result: cannot distinguish reproduction, optimization and novel form exploration.

## 30.3 Creativity = Novelty collapse

Failure: maximize surprise/randomness.

Result: incoherent or valueless output.

## 30.4 Creativity = OutputVolume collapse

Failure: more generated variants counted as more creativity.

Result: evaluation burden grows while authorship shrinks.

## 30.5 More options = more expression collapse

Failure: huge parameter count with weak semantic contrast.

Result: combinatorial noise, not identity.

## 30.6 Infinite canvas = maximum agency collapse

Failure: remove all constraints.

Result: blank-page paralysis and weak learnability.

## 30.7 Crafting = Creative construction collapse

Failure: recipe invocation called building creativity.

Result: confuses economy/production with form authorship.

## 30.8 Customization = Authorship collapse

Failure: choosing a preset means the player “created the asset.”

Result: loses material/source authorship distinction.

## 30.9 Prompt = Authorship collapse

Failure: any prompt automatically grants strong authorship.

Result: ignores decision specificity, iteration and causal influence.

## 30.10 AI pixels = AI authorship collapse

Failure: whoever realizes the most surface tokens owns all authorship.

Result: ignores framing, composition, curation and revision.

## 30.11 Selection = trivial collapse

Failure: all curation treated as non-creative.

Result: misses composition, editing and taste.

## 30.12 Selection = authorship collapse

Failure: accepting any generated option treated as strong authorship.

Result: ignores low counterfactual influence.

## 30.13 Tool = neutral conduit collapse

Failure: ignore how tool mapping changes reachable forms and decisions.

Result: cannot reason about automation boundaries.

## 30.14 Constraint = bad collapse

Failure: remove all resistance in name of freedom.

Result: destroys trade-offs, style, mastery and meaning.

## 30.15 Style = skin collapse

Failure: label/preset equated with creator style.

Result: ignores repeated choice structure.

## 30.16 Taste = score collapse

Failure: one objective aesthetic number.

Result: plural creative values disappear.

## 30.17 AI suggestion flood

Failure: generate faster than player can evaluate.

Result: curation overload and default acceptance.

## 30.18 Intent lock-in

Failure: system demands complete intent upfront.

Result: no tinkering/co-evolution/discovery.

## 30.19 Intent drift without awareness

Failure: AI outputs gradually redefine the project while user loses orientation.

Result: authorship weakens despite continued interaction.

## 30.20 No versioning / reversibility

Failure: experimentation has high irreversible cost.

Result: players avoid risk and converge early.

## 30.21 Generated world artifact without causality

Failure: impressive generated building is decorative only.

Result: world creation does not change future play.

## 30.22 UGC without audience/meaning

Failure: players can publish infinite content but nobody can discover, interpret or reuse it.

Result: production without social value.

## 30.23 Shared creation without authority semantics

Failure: anyone can overwrite anything.

Result: collaboration becomes conflict/noise.

## 30.24 Co-creation = equal initiative collapse

Failure: system forces AI to take initiative constantly.

Result: unnecessary interruption and loss of flow/control.

## 30.25 Generated personalization = expression collapse

Failure: model outputs personally flavored content without player-authored distinctions.

Result: personalization can mimic expression while bypassing agency.

## 30.26 Production tool = player creative game collapse

Failure: because Studio can generate assets, Game is assumed to have creative gameplay.

Result: internal production capability is confused with player-facing mechanics.

# 33. Direct answers to the R20 continuation questions

### What distinguishes creation from ordinary state transformation?

Creation organizes material/state/signs/behavior into an internally differentiated Form whose meaningful properties depend on participant choices and can become an object of perception, use, revision, performance or interpretation.

### When is construction optimization versus creative expression?

Construction is optimization-dominant when one metric/solution family largely determines success. It becomes creatively expressive when plural meaningful form choices remain after functional constraints and those choices can reflect intent/taste/identity.

### What makes an artifact attributable to the player when AI realizes most surface form?

The locus of meaningful decisions: framing, constraints, structural specification, selection, evaluation, revision, integration and final commitment. Raw token/pixel share is insufficient.

### How do constraints create rather than merely limit possibility?

They remove some futures while creating contrast, trade-offs, grammar, mastery and recognizable solutions among the remaining ones. Unlimited possibility can reduce legibility and commitment.

### How does self-expression differ from instrumental creation?

Instrumental creation primarily changes capability/function. Expression makes identity, stance, emotion, taste or interpretation legible. One artifact can do both.

### When is customization meaningful?

When bounded choices map to semantically or causally important differences recognized by the player/system/audience. Large option count alone is not enough.

### What distinguishes assistance, co-creation, delegation and autonomous generation?

Which participant controls framing, generation, evaluation, modification, selection and commitment. Co-creation requires independently consequential contributions from more than one participant; delegation preserves high-level authorship while transferring realization; autonomy transfers goal/search/evaluation initiative as well.

### How should taste/evaluation be represented?

As contextual and often plural evaluative discrimination used for rejection, selection and revision, not automatically one objective aesthetic score.

### How do persistent creations become World structure?

By changing later topology, resources, information, identity, institutions, social recognition or strategic possibility rather than remaining inert gallery objects.

### How should shared creation handle authority and ownership?

Separate authorship/provenance from possession/control/rights, and define explicit edit, revert, publish, fork/remix, attribution and completion authority where the experience depends on them.

### What are the minimum hard semantics for generative creative interaction?

Hard-state only what must remain causally stable: authoritative artifact state, adopted constraints, structural commitments, provenance/ownership when relevant, and world effects. Keep interpretation, suggestions, style nuance and many candidate variants soft/generated where possible.

# 35. R20 synthesis

The deepest compression of R20 is:

```text
Creation is not “content appeared.”

Creation occurs when a participant organizes possibility into Form,
and meaningful properties of that Form depend on participant choices.
```

Creativity is a separate question:

```text
Does the process/form discover, combine, explore or transform possibilities
in a meaningfully novel and contextually valuable way?
```

Expression is another:

```text
Does the resulting choice/form make identity, stance, taste or meaning legible?
```

Authorship is another:

```text
Which participant's decisions actually shaped and committed
which aspects of the resulting work?
```

This gives a compact creative stack:

```text
Motive / Question / Impulse
        ↕
Framing / Intent
        ↕
Creative Possibility Space
(Material + Tool + Grammar + Constraint)
        ↓
Make / Compose / Generate / Select / Transform
        ↓
Artifact / Form / Trace
        ↓
Perceive / Evaluate / Taste
        ↓
Revise / Branch / Reject / Commit
        ↓
Use / Share / Perform / Place in World
        ↓
History / Identity / Resource / Social Meaning / New Possibility
        ↺
```

And the strongest AI-era design discipline is:

```text
Track decision locus, not pixel/token share.
Preserve value-bearing authorial choices.
Use automation to expand reach or reduce unwanted friction.
Do not mistake generation for creativity or personalization for expression.
```

R20 adds:

```text
PlayableCreation
PlayableExpression
CreativeContributionTopology
AuthorialCausality
```

to the foundation vocabulary.

# 36. Unresolved questions left by R20

R20 exposes another foundation dimension that has been partially present since R3/R11 but never isolated.

Important unresolved questions include:

1. What is Embodiment in a game when the controlled entity may be an avatar, cursor, vehicle, camera, army, text persona or abstract policy?
2. How do Intent, Input, Control Mapping, Motor Skill, Action Resolution and Feedback differ?
3. What exactly is “Game Feel,” and which parts arise from control latency, audiovisual feedback, anticipation, inertia, animation, physics or consequence?
4. How does continuous sensorimotor skill differ from symbolic/strategic decision skill?
5. What makes an action feel like “I did that” rather than “the system did that for me”?
6. How do direct manipulation, command, delegation and autonomous execution distribute control and agency?
7. What is Affordance relative to capability, perceived action possibility and actual action legality?
8. How do animation, camera, sound, haptics and timing communicate causal ownership of action?
9. When does friction create mastery and materiality, and when is it merely bad interface cost?
10. How should AI assist motor/control tasks without consuming the skill or fantasy that constitutes the gameplay?
11. How does embodiment change identity, presence, risk and emotional attachment?
12. Which game forms need a body at all, and what counts as a body in text/generative/strategy forms?
