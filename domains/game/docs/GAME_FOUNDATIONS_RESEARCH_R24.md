# Ordivon Game Foundations Research — R24 Identity, Character, Role, Persona, Self, Status, Reputation, Continuity and Transformation

## 0. Status and boundary

R24 continues the foundation programme after R1–R23. It is a research record, not a product specification and not a G0–G8 promotion.

R23 asked:

> How are actions and consequences ordered through time?

R24 asks:

> What makes a Subject, character, object or collective count as the same entity through time, transformation, role change and social recognition — and what happens when self-belief, public presentation, institutional record and observer belief disagree?

# 1. External anchors and what they do — and do not — establish

R24 triangulates personal-identity philosophy, narrative identity, identity theory, social/collective identity, status and reputation research, player-avatar identification and current LLM role-playing/persona work. These sources are pressure tests. They do not impose one metaphysical theory on every game world.

## 1.1 Personal-identity philosophy — memory alone is too weak and branching is a hard test

The modern personal-identity literature distinguishes psychological-continuity approaches from bodily/biological and other accounts. Psychological continuity is usually broader than episodic memory: beliefs, intentions, preferences, desires and character can participate in overlapping causal chains.

A classic problem is branching/fission. If one source psychology continues equally into two later beings, qualitative and psychological continuity can hold to both, but numerical identity cannot straightforwardly be one-to-two.

Game lesson:

```text
MemorySimilarity != NumericalIdentity
PsychologicalSimilarity != UniqueIdentity
```

and cloning/forking is an especially strong falsifier for any naive “copy state = same entity” rule.

R24 therefore does **not** select psychological continuity as the universal criterion. A game's world can define biological, soul-based, institutional, causal-lineage, role-based or hybrid identity rules.

## 1.2 McAdams — narrative identity gives unity and purpose without solving numerical identity

McAdams' life-story model treats identity in part as an internalized and evolving narrative through which people organize life into unity and purpose.

Game lesson:

```text
NarrativeIdentity
can organize meaning across history,
while still being distinct from
which entity is numerically the same referent.
```

A character can misremember or reinterpret its own story without ceasing to be the same authoritative entity.

## 1.3 Identity theory — self meanings, role meanings and verification are relational processes

Burke/Stets identity theory distinguishes identity standards from perceived self-in-situation meanings and studies self-verification loops. It also distinguishes person, role, group and categorical bases of identity.

Game lesson:

```text
SelfIdentity is not merely stored biography.
```

It can be a control/interpretive process in which an actor tries to keep perceived self-meaning compatible with identity-relevant standards.

This supports R18's earlier observation:

```text
Identity becomes structural when it filters future action,
not when it is cosmetic prose.
```

## 1.4 Tajfel and social identity — identity can be group-relational

Social-identity research emphasizes that group/category membership can become part of self-conception and intergroup behavior.

Game lesson:

```text
IndividualIdentity
and
Group / SocialIdentity
are distinct but interacting structures.
```

Faction identity cannot be reduced to a private trait vector.

## 1.5 Collective identity is multidimensional

Ashmore, Deaux and McLaughlin-Volpe organize collective identity across dimensions such as self-categorization, evaluation, importance, attachment/interdependence, social embeddedness, behavioral involvement, content and meaning.

Game lesson:

```text
Membership flag != CollectiveIdentity
```

A player may formally belong to a guild without strongly identifying with it; conversely a dissolved faction can remain identity-relevant through memory, symbols and loyalty.

## 1.6 Status research — standing changes expectations but is not raw power

Status-characteristics / expectation-states research examines how socially recognized characteristics and standing can shape performance expectations and interaction.

Game lesson:

```text
Status != Capability
Status != Rank token
Status != Reputation belief alone
```

Status is relational and context-dependent; it can influence who is listened to, obeyed, deferred to or granted opportunity even when causal capability is unchanged.

## 1.7 Reputation can differ from identity and from self-view

Multi-rater personality work distinguishes underlying trait consensus, self-perception/identity and impressions/reputation held by others.

R19 already established:

```text
Reputation_i@j =
j's belief about i's likely type/policy/commitment behavior
```

R24 preserves:

```text
Reputation belongs primarily to observer belief state.
```

A disguise can alter reputation/recognition without changing underlying entity continuity.

## 1.8 Player–avatar identification is not body ownership or character identity

Empirical game research treats player-avatar identification as a cognitive/emotional relationship and measures dimensions such as absorption, attitudes and importance to self identity.

Game lesson:

```text
Player identifies with Avatar
```

is a player–representation relation, not proof that:

```text
Player == Character
Avatar == Body
Character == Agent
```

This preserves R21's embodiment separation.

## 1.9 Current LLM role-playing research — consistency should not mean static repetition

ACL 2026 work on Dynamic Persona Coherence explicitly separates longer-term identity-layer stability from history-dependent adaptive state, criticizing monolithic static personas that either repeat rigidly or drift catastrophically.

Game lesson:

```text
IdentityConsistency != BehavioralRigidity
```

A coherent character should be allowed to learn, grieve, reconcile, change goals and transform when history warrants it.

This is current empirical/system evidence, not a universal definition of identity.

# 2. First disambiguation — “identity” names several different questions

Before modeling Identity, ask which problem is meant.

## 2.1 Numerical / entity identity

```text
Is x at t1 the same authoritative entity as y at t2?
```

This is a sameness/persistence relation.

## 2.2 Qualitative identity / similarity

```text
How similar are x and y in properties, memory, style, appearance or policy?
```

Two clones can be qualitatively indistinguishable while numerically distinct.

## 2.3 Self-identity

```text
Who does the Subject take itself to be?
```

A self-model may be incomplete, mistaken or contested.

## 2.4 Role identity

```text
Which role meanings, duties and expectations are integrated into self-conception/action?
```

## 2.5 Social / group identity

```text
Which groups/categories are self-relevant and socially meaningful?
```

## 2.6 Public identity / presentation

```text
How is the entity named, signaled and recognized in interaction?
```

## 2.7 Institutional / legal identity

```text
Which authoritative record binds rights, obligations, ownership, office or liability to a referent?
```

## 2.8 Reputation

```text
What do observers believe about the target?
```

Compact rule:

```text
EntityIdentity
!= QualitativeSimilarity
!= SelfIdentity
!= RoleIdentity
!= SocialIdentity
!= PublicPresentation
!= InstitutionalIdentity
!= Reputation
```

# 3. Core term separation

| Term | Working meaning | Not equivalent to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Entity / Numerical Identity** | World/authority relation that treats states/stages as belonging to the same referent across change. | Similarity, same name. |
| **Continuity** | Causal/structural relations connecting stages through time; may support identity without being identical to it. | Memory alone. |
| **Identity Content** | Meanings, commitments, affiliations or self-descriptions treated as identity-relevant. | Entity sameness. |
| **Self** | Subject-relative first-person locus/self-model: the entity represented as “me” from that Subject's perspective. | Body, name, objective truth. |
| **Self-model** | Subject's representation of its own identity, capabilities, history, roles and commitments. | Authoritative world identity. |
| **Character** | A represented identity-bearing figure/entity in the game/fiction whose continuity, actions, perspective or portrayal can matter. | Subject or Agent necessarily. |
| **Persona** | Situated expressive/presentation configuration through which a character/Subject is encountered or performed: voice, style, stance, boundaries, selected identity claims. | Entire character/entity. |
| **Role** | Relational/institutional/narrative position carrying expectations, permissions, responsibilities, rights, capabilities and/or functions. | Class/loadout or occupant identity. |
| **Avatar** | Player-facing representation/control locus associated with a player/character. | Player, Character, Body necessarily. |
| **Body** | R21 self-relevant action–perception substrate. | Identity by itself. |
| **Identifier** | Symbol/token used to refer to an entity/account/role/record. | Entity itself. |
| **Name** | Human/interpretable identifier or address term. | Unique identity. |
| **Alias** | Alternate identifier/presentation linked or claimed to link to an entity. | New entity necessarily. |
| **Disguise** | Manipulation of observable identity evidence/presentation to induce a different recognition/belief. | Entity transformation necessarily. |
| **Recognition** | Observer/institution mapping from evidence to an identity/role/category hypothesis. | Authoritative identity truth. |
| **Status** | Social/institutional standing that structures expectations, deference, access or evaluation in a context. | Rank, power, reputation. |
| **Rank** | Explicit ordered position/category under a rule/institution. | Social status generally. |
| **Power** | Capability to alter relevant reachable futures. | Status. |
| **Reputation** | Observer-relative belief about target's likely qualities/policy/commitment behavior. | Target's identity/self-view. |
| **Transformation** | Change that preserves relevant identity/continuity under the governing identity rule. | Replacement. |
| **Replacement / Succession** | A new entity occupies a former entity's role/location/function without inheriting all identity. | Transformation. |
| **Clone / Copy** | New entity/state produced with high qualitative similarity/provenance from a source. | Same numerical identity. |
| **Fork** | One source lineage branches into multiple successors that share prior provenance but diverge. | One-to-many numerical identity by default. |
| **Collective Identity** | Self/social meaning attached to belonging/interdependence with a group/category/collective. | Organization authority or membership flag alone. |

# 4. Identity is a relation across stages, not a bag of traits

A trait list describes a state:

```text
brave
medic
red hair
loyal
```

It does not answer:

```text
Is this later entity the same one?
```

R24 therefore treats entity identity as a relation:

```text
SameEntity_A,purpose(x@t1, y@t2)
```

where `A` is the relevant authority/rule and `purpose` indicates which consequence domain is being adjudicated when necessary.

## 4.1 Why purpose can matter

A body swap can produce different answers for:

```text
who owns this injury?
who owes this debt?
who is married to whom?
who occupies the throne?
who controls this body?
```

A game may deliberately bind different consequences to different continuity relations.

## 4.2 Do not universalize one metaphysics

Possible world rules include:

```text
same organism
same brain/mind
same soul token
same causal lineage
same account/provenance identity
same institutional record
same narrative subject
hybrid / contested
```

The foundation requirement is explicit causal semantics, not one philosophical winner.

# 5. Continuity Basis, Evidence, Content, Recognition and Consequence

R24 introduces a five-way separation.

## 5.1 Continuity Basis

```text
What relation does authority use to treat stages as the same entity?
```

## 5.2 Identity Evidence

```text
What observations support an observer's belief that this is the same entity?
```

Examples:

```text
face
voice
memory test
credential
history
behavior
biometric/body
social witness
```

## 5.3 Identity Content

```text
What identity-relevant meanings/commitments/affiliations characterize the entity/self-model?
```

## 5.4 Recognition

```text
Who believes/accepts which identity claim?
```

## 5.5 Identity Consequence

```text
What rights, obligations, expectations, relationships or reactions follow if the claim is accepted?
```

Core rule:

```text
ContinuityBasis
!= IdentityEvidence
!= IdentityContent
!= Recognition
!= IdentityConsequence
```

This separation explains most disguise/amnesia/succession puzzles.

# 6. Continuity Profile — identity can be supported by several channels

R24 uses a profile rather than a universal scalar.

```text
ContinuityProfile = {
  CausalLineage,
  Substrate / Body,
  PsychologicalState,
  Memory,
  Values / Goals / Commitments,
  Relationships,
  InstitutionalRecord,
  SocialRecognition,
  NarrativeHistory,
  Provenance / ControlLineage
}
```

Not every game needs all fields; no field is universally necessary or sufficient.

## 6.1 Causal lineage

Later state derives through the authoritative transition/history of the earlier entity.

## 6.2 Body/substrate continuity

Same body/organism/object substrate remains.

## 6.3 Psychological continuity

Beliefs, dispositions, goals, intentions and character causally continue.

## 6.4 Memory continuity

Later entity can access/retain relevant past experiences.

Memory is useful evidence and functional continuity, but:

```text
Memory != Identity
```

## 6.5 Commitment/value continuity

Promises, values and adopted goals remain action-structuring.

## 6.6 Relationship continuity

Others remain bound by friendship, kinship, debt, rivalry, loyalty, trust or obligation.

## 6.7 Institutional continuity

Records, rights, roles or legal claims remain attached.

## 6.8 Social recognition

Community continues treating the entity as the same person/member/character.

## 6.9 Narrative continuity

Past and present are interpreted as chapters of one life/story.

## 6.10 Provenance/control lineage

Persistent identity can be anchored by authoritative lineage through migrations/reconstruction even when implementation changes.

# 7. Identity Authority — who decides sameness for which consequence?

R24 introduces:

```text
IdentityAuthority(domain) =
rules / institution / world authority that determines
which referent receives identity-linked consequences.
```

Examples:

```text
Body injury        → physical/body authority
Account inventory  → account/provenance authority
Debt/liability     → institutional identity authority
Promise            → Subject/commitment continuity
Office/throne      → institutional role/legitimacy
Recognition        → observer belief, not world truth
```

## 7.1 Different authorities can conflict

A pretender can be:

```text
recognized by the crowd
rejected by law
believed by one ally
known false by world truth
```

This is playable identity conflict.

## 7.2 Authority should be local to consequence

Avoid one global `identity=true/false` when different systems legitimately use different criteria.

# 8. Identifier, name and identity

```text
Identifier = reference token
Entity = referent
```

They must stay separate.

## 8.1 Names are not guaranteed unique

Two characters can share a name.
One character can change names.

## 8.2 IDs can persist while identity meaning transforms

Database continuity does not imply narrative/persona continuity.

## 8.3 New identifier need not mean new entity

Alias, title or marriage/name change can preserve entity continuity.

Core rule:

```text
IdentifierContinuity != IdentityContinuity
```

Identifiers are often evidence/handles, not ontology.

# 9. Self and self-model

R24 uses:

```text
Self =
Subject-relative first-person locus represented as “me”
```

and:

```text
SelfModel =
Subject's model of its own identity/history/roles/capabilities/commitments
```

## 9.1 Self-model can be false

Examples:

```text
amnesia
false implanted memory
secret adoption
mistaken lineage
role delusion
```

World truth need not change.

## 9.2 Self-model can lag transformation

A newly promoted leader may still act from an old subordinate self-conception.

## 9.3 Self-model can drive behavior

Burke/Stets-style identity verification suggests a useful game abstraction:

```text
IdentityStandard / SelfMeaning
→ compare perceived current self
→ discrepancy
→ action / reinterpretation / emotion
```

Only model this when player-facing identity behavior matters.

# 10. Character — represented identity-bearing figure, not cognition level

R10's continuum used “Character” informally. R24 refines:

```text
Character =
a represented identity-bearing figure/entity
whose portrayal, continuity, action, perspective or relation
matters within the game/fiction.
```

A Character can be:

```text
static portrait
scripted NPC
player-controlled figure
Subject
Agent
historical/dead figure present only through legacy
```

Therefore:

```text
Character != Subject
Character != Agent
Character != NPC necessarily
```

“Character” is primarily a representational/narrative/social role, not an autonomy class.

```text
Role =
relational / institutional / narrative position
```

```text

heir
judge
```

## 12.1 Role != Character Class

## 12.2 Role != Occupant

```text
KingRole
```

## 12.3 One entity can occupy multiple roles



# 14. Collective identity versus organization identity

```text
Organization = RepeatedCoordination + PersistentRoles + SharedResources
```

```text
CollectiveIdentity =
```

# 18. Identity Causality

R24 introduces:

```text
IdentityCausality =
value-bearing consequences / reachable futures
counterfactually depend on
which identity, role, membership or lineage
an action/state is attributed to or recognized as belonging to.
```

Test:

```text
same action
same physical state
same location

performed/recognized as identity A
vs identity B

→ materially different future?
```

Examples:

```text
same order from captain vs civilian
same face recognized as ally vs outlaw
same artifact owned by heir vs thief
same statement from trusted doctor vs stranger
```

If no consequence changes:

```text
identity distinction may be cosmetic at that layer.
```

Amnesia can remove:

```text
episodic memory
self-narrative
relationship recall
role knowledge
```

while preserving:

```text
body
causal lineage
institutional identity
others' recognition
rights/debts
skills/dispositions
```

Therefore:

```text
MemoryLoss != automatic IdentityBreak
```

But it may break:

```text
SelfIdentity continuity
Relationship functionality
Commitment accessibility
```

Identity consequences can split by layer.

Suppose A's psychological state moves to B's body while B's moves to A's body.

Possible questions:

```text
Who owns the body injury?
Who is spouse/friend?
Who owns property?
Who is recognized by face?
Who inherits old promises?
```

A game can answer differently.

R21 already established:

```text
Body != Identity
Embodiment != RoleIdentification
```

R24 adds:

```text
BodyContinuity and SubjectContinuity may diverge.
```

# 21. Clone / copy — similarity is not sameness

A copy can preserve:

```text
memory
appearance
voice
values
skills
persona
```

while producing a second distinct causal branch.

```text
Copy(source)
→ clone1
→ clone2
```

After branching:

```text
SharedPast != SharedNumericalIdentity
```

## 21.1 Branch-aware semantics

Useful structure:

```text
CommonProvenance
+ ForkPoint
+ DistinctPostForkHistories
```

rather than pretending both are one entity.

## 21.2 Rights/relationships become a design problem

Who owns:

```text
property
debt
marriage
rank
promise
crime liability
```

after duplication?

This requires institutional/world rules, not similarity heuristics.

# 22. Fork, merge and composite identity

## 22.1 Fork

One prior lineage creates several successors.

Do not infer one-to-many numerical identity automatically.

## 22.2 Merge

Several prior entities may combine memory/policy/body into one later entity.

Questions:

```text
which obligations persist?
which relationships transfer?
who is legally/strategically recognized?
```

## 22.3 Composite/hive identity

A collective Subject may have one policy/authority while members retain individual identity.

```text
CollectiveAgency != IndividualIdentity erasure
```

R24 leaves metaphysical rules to the Game but requires explicit consequence semantics.

# 23. Transformation versus replacement

Working test:

```text
Transformation =
meaningful change occurs
while relevant continuity authorities still bind future consequences
to the same referent.
```

```text
Replacement =
a new referent takes over role/function/location
without inheriting all identity-linked continuity.
```

Examples:

```text
promotion → transformation
new monarch succeeds old monarch → role succession / replacement
cybernetic upgrade → usually transformation if world says so
actor recast behind a fictional character → production replacement, character continuity may remain
```

Result: rights/relationships/history become incoherent.

## 51.4 Identity = body collapse

Failure: body swap/reincarnation impossible to model without contradiction.

## 51.5 Identity = prompt collapse

Failure: same system prompt means same character.

Result: independent histories/relationships disappear.

## 51.6 Character = Agent collapse

Failure: every meaningful character needs autonomous action selection.

Result: unnecessary cognition and reduced authored/player control.

## 51.7 Persona = Character collapse

Failure: public presentation is treated as full entity truth.

Result: disguise/private self/multiple personas impossible.

## 51.8 Role = class collapse

Failure: capability loadout stands in for social/institutional position.

Result: duty/authority/expectation/legitimacy lost.

## 51.9 Role = occupant collapse

Failure: new king becomes “same person” because office persists.

Result: succession semantics break.

## 51.10 Membership = collective identity collapse

Failure: Boolean guild member flag means loyalty/identification.

Result: alienation, dissent and layered belonging disappear.

## 51.11 Status = rank collapse

Failure: one numeric ladder represents all standing.

Result: context-specific prestige/deference erased.

## 51.12 Status = power collapse

Failure: socially respected means causally powerful.

Result: hidden power and symbolic authority cannot diverge.

## 51.13 Reputation = identity collapse

Failure: universal reputation score becomes objective identity fact.

Result: observer disagreement disappears.

## 51.14 Recognition = truth collapse

Failure: if observers believe disguise, world truth changes.

Result: deception impossible to reason about.

## 51.15 Similarity = numerical identity collapse

Failure: exact clone is “the same entity.”

Result: branching contradiction.

## 51.16 Provenance = identity collapse

Failure: any derivative/copy inherits full identity automatically.

Result: clone/successor rights become incoherent.

## 51.17 Transformation = replacement collapse

Failure: every major personality/body/status change creates new character.

Result: development loses continuity.

## 51.18 Replacement = transformation collapse

Failure: successor inherits all old relationships/debts/personhood.

Result: office/role succession becomes absurd.

## 51.19 Consistency = rigidity collapse

Failure: coherent Persona repeats same stance forever.

Result: history cannot matter.

## 51.20 Change = drift collapse

Failure: any value/behavior change considered persona failure.

Result: no development.

## 51.21 Drift = development collapse

Failure: stochastic inconsistency justified retroactively as “growth.”

Result: causal continuity disappears.

## 51.22 Backstory quantity = character depth collapse

Failure: more generated facts assumed deeper identity.

## 51.23 Global identity truth only

Failure: self, observers and institutions cannot disagree.

Failure: reduce identity to cosmetic lore.

## 51.30 One global identity authority

# 54. Direct answers to the R24 frontier questions

### What is Identity relative to memory, body, role and recognition?

Entity identity is an authority-governed sameness relation across stages. Memory, body, role and recognition are possible continuity channels/evidence/consequences, not universally identity itself.

### Character relative to Subject, Persona and Avatar?

Character is a represented identity-bearing figure. Subject is an actor with situated perspective/internal state. Persona is a situated expression/presentation surface. Avatar is player-facing representation/control locus. One object may occupy several of these roles, but the terms are not equivalent.

### What is Role?

A relational/institutional/narrative position carrying expectations, permissions, duties, rights, capabilities and/or function. Role continuity can survive occupant replacement.

### Persona relative to deeper continuity?

Persona is how an entity/character is presented/performed in a context. One entity can have several personas; a persona can be imitated. Persona similarity therefore does not establish entity continuity.

### What makes a Subject the same one through change?

The Game must define the relevant continuity authorities. Possible bases include causal lineage, body, psychology, commitments, relationships, institution and provenance. There is no foundation-wide universal criterion.

### Self identity vs observer identity?

Self identity is the Subject's self-model. Observer recognition is another Subject/institution's belief/claim. They may disagree with each other and with world authority.

### Status relative to Rank, Reputation and Power?

Status is contextual social standing; Rank is explicit ordered position; Reputation is observer belief; Power is causal capability. They may correlate but are not equivalent.

### Identity versus Reputation?

Identity concerns referent/continuity/self/social meaning; reputation is what an observer believes about a target's likely qualities or behavior.

### Development versus identity break?

Development changes identity-relevant content while preserving sufficient continuity under the world's governing rules and usually has causal/history support. Identity break/replacement occurs when the relevant authority no longer binds the future entity to the prior referent for the consequences at issue.

### How do aliases/disguises work?

They manipulate identifiers/presentation/evidence and therefore Recognition Topology. The underlying entity may remain unchanged while observer policy changes.

### How does identity constrain goals?

Identity content can act as a slow commitment/self-model that filters admissible goals, acceptable sacrifice, obligations and interpretation of reward.

### Player identification vs Avatar/Body ownership?

Player–character/avatar identification is a cognitive/emotional relation; body ownership is an embodiment phenomenon; character identity is diegetic continuity. They can vary independently.

### How does expression become identity?

Repeated attributed choices can become stable patterns recognized by self/others and incorporated into self/social identity. Surface imitation alone does not transfer identity.

### How should generative Personas maintain continuity without rigidity?

Separate slower identity/commitment structure from medium-term relationship/meaning state and fast affect/expression; allow change when history provides a causal bridge.

### What provenance is needed for Agent migration?

Enough authoritative lineage to bind the new runtime/model instance to retained identity state, commitments, relationships, rights and history. Model implementation alone should not determine identity.

### Collective identity vs organization?

Collective identity is self/social meaning of belonging and interdependence. Organization is a coordination/authority structure. Either can be strong while the other is weak.

The deepest compression of R24 is:

```text
Identity is not a trait list, name, memory or body.

Entity identity is a continuity/sameness relation
that binds stages and consequences to a referent
under explicit world/institutional rules.
```

A compact identity stack is:

```text
Authoritative Referent / Lineage
        ↓
Continuity Bases
        ↓
Identity Content / Self Model
        ↓
Roles / Memberships / Commitments
        ↓
Persona / Identifiers / Presentation
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Observer / Institutional Recognition
        ↓
Status / Reputation / Access / Obligation
        ↓
Action / Consequence
        ↓
History / Transformation
        ↺
```

The strongest sameness rule is:

```text
QualitativeSimilarity != NumericalIdentity.
```

```text
Memory != Identity.
```

```text
Reputation != Identity
and Recognition != WorldTruth.
```

```text
RoleContinuity != OccupantContinuity.
```

The strongest generative rule is:

```text
IdentityConsistency != BehavioralRigidity.
```

The strongest implementation rule is:

# 57. Unresolved questions left by R24

R18 and R19 repeatedly used relationships, trust, attachment, loyalty, intimacy, debt and obligation, but relationship itself has never been decomposed as a first-class persistent structure.

Important unresolved questions include:

1. What is a Relationship relative to repeated interaction, memory, reputation and current attitude?
2. What distinguishes acquaintance, affiliation, attachment, intimacy, trust, loyalty, dependence and obligation?
3. What is attachment relative to preference/value and identity?
4. What makes a relationship genuinely reciprocal rather than two independent attitudes?
5. How do communal and exchange relationships differ?
6. How do rivalry, enmity and dependency count as relationships without positive attachment?
7. What does relationship history need to retain after episodic memories are forgotten?
8. How do trust, reliability and vulnerability interact without becoming one meter?
9. How do relationships become resources, constraints, goals or identity structure?
10. What is intimacy relative to information access and vulnerability?
11. How do boundaries, consent, expectations and repair shape relationship dynamics?
12. How do relationships survive role/status/identity changes from R24?
13. How does social network topology differ from dyadic relationship state?
14. What makes AI/Persona relationship continuity real rather than generated flattery?
15. Which relational claims create shared-state/history debt in generative systems?
16. How can relationship progression be playable without becoming affection-point optimization?
