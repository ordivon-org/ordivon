## 1. Research question

The operative question is not whether "AI is a bubble" or whether headline U.S. equity indices must fall. The question is:

> While AI hyperscalers maintain unusually strong earnings, financing access and capital expenditure, are high oil prices, high real/nominal yields and rate volatility compressing the traditional rate-sensitive economy and the median listed company strongly enough to create a structurally unstable gap between cap-weighted equity indices, market breadth, credit, rates and the real economy? If so, does reconvergence occur through breadth recovery, time/earnings catch-up, leader valuation reset, or credit/funding transmission?

This document preserves the 2026-09-27 standing so later evidence can confirm, weaken or supersede it.

## 2. Ordivon LEGO decomposition

The system is separated into five non-collapsible layers:

1. **AI Technology / Monetization** — capability, adoption, AI revenue, economic productivity.
2. **AI Capital Formation** — hyperscaler capex, data-centre/power/compute buildout, leases, project finance, private credit and debt issuance.
3. **Macro Resource / Rate Transmission** — investment demand, real-resource scarcity, equilibrium real rates, oil/inflation pressure, mortgage and WACC transmission.
4. **Cross-sectional Market Structure** — cap-weighted indices, equal-weight indices, breadth, small caps, concentration and earnings breadth.
5. **Credit / Funding Plumbing** — IG/HY/CCC spreads, bank standards, repo/haircuts, refinancing, syndication, private-credit liquidity and forced deleveraging.

A finding in one layer does not mint authority in another. In particular:

- real AI technology progress does not prove current equity valuation;
- equity weakness does not prove technological failure;
- credit repricing does not prove a funding run;
- rate volatility does not prove a 2007/08-style systemic event.

## 3. Current standing

The current state is classified as:

**F3 — MARKET FRACTURE / CONCENTRATED RESILIENCE WITH BROAD INTERNAL TIGHTENING**

The classification is descriptive, not predictive.

### Confirmed or strongly supported observations

- Headline U.S. cap-weighted equity indices remain much stronger than the median constituent and equal-weight proxies.
- S&P 500 participation around late September is weak: independent breadth sources put fewer than half of constituents above their own 200-day moving averages, while the index itself remains well above its own long-term average.
- Index concentration is historically high; the largest ten S&P 500 constituents account for roughly the high-30% range of index weight in 2026.
- Treasury-rate volatility rose sharply in late September while equity-index implied volatility stayed comparatively subdued, producing a pronounced MOVE–VIX divergence.
- Long Treasury yields and household borrowing rates are restrictive; 30-year U.S. mortgage rates moved back to roughly 7% or above in late September.
- Fed district evidence shows a K-shaped real economy: residential and consumer-sensitive activity is pressured while data-centre, defence and selected manufacturing/non-residential construction remain strong.
- Broad U.S. investment-grade/high-yield credit remains orderly, but the weakest credit tiers show more deterioration than broad HY.
- AI infrastructure financing is increasingly debt- and commitment-heavy: public bonds, leases, project finance, SPVs and private credit are becoming material complements to internal cash flow.
- Current evidence supports an AI-related real-resource / saving-investment pressure channel on real rates more strongly than a simple "AI bond issuance mechanically crowds Treasuries out" story.

### Not confirmed

- Broad corporate credit crunch.
- System-wide repo/haircut spiral.
- Widespread refinancing shutdown.
- Forced deleveraging across AI infrastructure.
- 2007Q3-style funding run or 2008-style systemic crisis.
- A deterministic requirement that the S&P 500 must enter a bear market to restore equilibrium.

## 4. Historical analog decomposition

Current AI-market structure is not mapped to a single historical year. Different LEGO are at different historical stages:

- **Technology truth:** resembles the genuine IT/productivity revolution of the second half of the 1990s more than a purely narrative bubble.
- **Equity concentration / expectations:** contains 1999-like features.
- **Infrastructure competition:** is structurally closer to the 1999–2001 telecom/fibre buildout than to simple dot-com websites.
- **Debt financialisation:** has early/mid-2006-like features in the narrow sense that off-balance-sheet vehicles, project structures and non-bank credit links are becoming more important.
- **Funding fragility:** does **not** currently resemble August 2007; rollover refusal, haircuts and indiscriminate short-term funding runs are not established.
- **Systemic run:** does **not** resemble 2008.

An important correction to the earlier simplified analogy is preserved here: the 2000 telecom cycle was not "pure equity". Speculative telecom firms used substantial bond and bank financing, suffered extreme spread widening and bankruptcies. What remained much weaker than in 2008 was the system-wide money-like short-term funding / collateral / haircut / forced-sale chain.

## 6. Concentration positive-feedback hypothesis

A potentially self-reinforcing loop now deserves explicit monitoring:

```text
high rates
→ weak / leveraged / domestic firms underperform
→ mega-cap relative performance rises
→ mega-cap index weights rise
→ benchmark/passive exposure becomes more concentrated
→ relative financing advantage persists
→ AI capex remains strong
→ aggregate investment and selected demand stay resilient
→ rates can remain restrictive
→ weak firms continue to lag
```

Standing: **SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS, NOT YET CAUSALLY IDENTIFIED**.

The hypothesis must be falsified or strengthened with later cross-sectional earnings, investment, financing-cost and breadth evidence. It must not be treated as an automatic directional trading signal.

## 7. Wealth-effect / demand loop

A second loop may partially offset traditional-economy weakness:

```text
AI / mega-cap equity wealth ↑
→ high-asset-household consumption resilience
→ aggregate demand remains firmer than median-household conditions suggest
→ inflation / policy pressure can remain sticky
→ rate-sensitive sectors remain constrained
```

This is a plausible reinforcing mechanism; its quantitative contribution is unresolved.

## 8. AI financing and circularity seam

The financing graph now includes:

```text
hyperscaler operating cash flow
+ corporate bonds
+ private credit
+ project finance / SPVs
+ leases / capacity offtake
+ guarantees / purchase commitments
```

BIS has described some off-balance-sheet arrangements as economically debt-like "shadow borrowing" and highlighted new transmission channels through refinancing pressure, non-bank appetite and guarantees.

A separate research seam is **ecosystem-financed demand**:

```text
supplier / hyperscaler investment in AI lab or neocloud
→ funded customer purchases compute / cloud / chips
→ supplier records backlog / revenue
→ revenue supports more infrastructure investment
```

This is not automatically fictitious demand. The required distinction is between **organic external end-demand** and **ecosystem-financed or circular demand**. The latter can accelerate a real buildout while making terminal demand and credit quality harder to infer.

## 9. Crowding-out decomposition

### 9.1 Portfolio crowding-out

Claim: AI bond issuance directly forces investors to sell Treasuries or non-AI corporate bonds.

Standing: **NOT ESTABLISHED / WEAK EVIDENCE**.

Event-study work published by PIMCO in September 2026 found little statistically significant Treasury-yield, term-premium or swap-spread response to surprise AI debt deals. Non-AI high-quality corporate issuance has also continued to clear.

### 9.2 Saving-investment / real-resource crowding

Claim: unusually large AI investment absorbs savings and scarce real resources, raising equilibrium real-rate pressure unless saving expands, thereby crowding out other interest-sensitive investment.

Standing: **SUPPORTED**.

This mechanism is independent of whether capex is financed by retained earnings, equity or debt: real construction consumes the same power, labour, equipment, land and capital resources.

Dallas Fed has additionally highlighted the large amount of direct and synthetic duration supply associated with AI financing. This can affect the long end of rates markets without implying that single debt deals mechanically cause Treasury selloffs.

## 11. Reconvergence paths

Market fracture does not mechanically require a bear market. Four resolution paths are kept separate.

### R1 — Breadth catch-up

Oil/inflation pressure eases → rate volatility and yields decline → mortgage/WACC pressure eases → equal-weight, small-cap and rate-sensitive sectors recover while mega-cap leaders remain stable.

### R2 — Time correction

Headline indices move sideways while earnings and long-term moving averages catch up; valuation compresses without large nominal price loss.

### R3 — Leader valuation reset

Mega-cap / Nasdaq-led drawdown of roughly correction magnitude, while broad credit, funding, earnings and breadth stabilise. This can reduce concentration without creating systemic stress.

### F4 transition — Unhealthy credit transmission

Rates stay restrictive → ordinary earnings weaken → weakest-credit defaults/restructurings increase → HY spreads and bank/private-credit standards tighten → AI project finance also reprices → funding/earnings stress finally reaches leaders.

The health of a correction is therefore defined by **credit/funding/earnings behaviour**, not by an arbitrary equity drawdown percentage.

## 12. Healthy correction contract

A price correction is classified as primarily valuation-led only if most of the following remain true:

- broad IG/HY credit remains orderly;
- repo / financing haircuts do not materially rise;
- bank lending standards do not tighten sharply;
- earnings revisions remain broadly stable;
- MOVE peaks or declines rather than accelerates;
- market breadth stops deteriorating even if headline indices fall;
- AI project refinancing does not move into repeated failure.

If these fail together, the event is not treated as a "healthy reset" but as a possible F3 → F4 transition.

## 15. Transition contracts

### F3 → R1 breadth repair

Evidence package should include sustained improvement in RSP/SPY, 50DMA/200DMA breadth and new-high/new-low participation, preferably alongside lower/stable MOVE, yields and mortgage rates while broad credit remains orderly.

### F3 → R2 valuation reset

Headline leader drawdown / sideways valuation compression with stable earnings, stable broad credit, stable funding haircuts and no broad bank-standard tightening.

### F3 → F4 credit transmission

Do not trigger on equity weakness alone. Require a cluster such as:

- continued weakest-credit spread widening that propagates into broad HY/IG;
- material tightening of bank or private-credit availability;
- repeated syndication / refinancing failures;
- rising collateral haircuts or materially worse repo/funding terms;
- broad negative earnings revisions;
- AI project-finance stress spreading beyond isolated projects.

### F4 → F5 systemic

Require evidence of a self-reinforcing funding loop:

`asset loss → funding withdrawal → forced sale → collateral loss → further funding withdrawal`.

This transition is **not currently supported**.

## 20. Bottom line

The strongest current interpretation is not that "all assets are ignoring high rates". Rather, the firms with the strongest cash flows, cheapest financing, greatest AI exposure and largest index weights can currently withstand the restrictive-rate regime better than households, housing, weak-credit firms, smaller companies and other rate-sensitive segments. That creates simultaneous macro tightening and headline-equity resilience.

AI investment may itself reinforce the split by supporting aggregate demand, consuming scarce real resources and capital, and helping keep real rates elevated. Higher rates then enlarge the relative financing advantage of the same mega-cap firms, creating a possible concentration feedback loop.

The decisive next question is therefore not "when will the bear market start?" It is:

> **Does F3 heal through breadth/rate normalisation or valuation compression while credit remains orderly, or does the fracture migrate into broad credit, funding and earnings transmission (F4)?**

That transition — not a fixed index drawdown — is the durable decision-support object.
