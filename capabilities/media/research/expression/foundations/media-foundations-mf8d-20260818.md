# 0. Purpose

MF8-C established that a bearer need not contain one globally coherent preference/utility ordering. It may simultaneously host needs, wants, norms, goals, learned habits, institutional obligations and formal objectives that only partially align.

MF8-D therefore cannot define choice as:

```text
argmax one utility function
```

nor can it define decision as merely `the chosen action`.

This round reconstructs:

```text
Alternative
Selection
Choice
Decision
Recommendation
Authorization
Commitment
Policy
Strategy
Plan
Schedule
Intention
Execution
```

The central questions are:

1. What makes an alternative an alternative **for the bearer** rather than merely a physically possible successor?
2. What separates generic selection from agential choice?
3. Does genuine choice require indeterminism or metaphysical alternative possibilities?
4. What makes a decision more than a choice token?
5. What is a policy ontologically: representation, disposition, control rule, decision rule or all depending route?
6. What makes a plan different from policy, intention, schedule, prediction and trajectory?
7. How do bounded reasoning, satisficing, conflict and indecision fit?
8. How do delegation, recommendation and authorization change who chose or decided?
9. Can a deterministic agent genuinely choose?
10. What standing can an artificial system have when it generates a recommendation but another bearer authorizes it?

# 2. AlternativeStanding comes before ChoiceStanding

A key error is to count every physically possible next state as an option the bearer `could choose`.

MF8-D separates:

```text
PhysicalPossibility
DynamicalSuccessorPossibility
Control-Admissible Input
Feasible Action
Available Action
Policy-Admissible Action
Represented Alternative
Considered Alternative
Choice-Relevant Alternative
```

These are different standings.

Provisional primitive:

```text
ChoiceAlternativeStanding(a, B | A, C, Σ)
```

holds when candidate `a` belongs to a non-arbitrarily constituted action/commitment alternative domain `A` for bearer/decision-role `B` under context `C`, and is relevant to the bearer's practical selection organization rather than merely being an observer-enumerated physical possibility.

### D8-002
**Physical possibility ≠ ChoiceAlternativeStanding.**

### D8-003
**Feasibility ≠ Availability.** A physically feasible action may be inaccessible due to authority, information, resource, interface or timing constraints.

### D8-004
**Availability ≠ Representation.** A bearer can have a dispositionally available action without explicitly representing it as a symbolic option.

### D8-005
**Representation ≠ Consideration.** Many represented actions may never enter current deliberation.

### D8-006
Choice claims therefore require an alternative-domain standing route, not a raw count of world branches.

### D8-008

# 4. Generic SelectionStanding

Before agential choice, define the neutral event:

```text
SelectionStanding(s, A | M, Σ)
```

when a mechanism/process `M` resolves, emits, retains, suppresses or routes one or more members from alternative domain `A` according to some rule, dynamics, stochastic process or constraint.

SelectionStanding is intentionally broad enough for:

- multiplexer routing;
- thermostat mode switching;
- winner-take-all circuits;
- random sampling;
- scheduler dispatch;
- natural selection language under its biological standing route;
- database query selection;
- deterministic policy output.

### D8-009
**SelectionStanding ≠ ChoiceStanding.**

### D8-010
Generic branching/resolution does not establish agency, evaluation or authorship.

# 5. ChoiceStanding — provisional token core

MF8-D defines agential choice without metaphysical free will:

```text
ChoiceStanding(ch, B | A, Σ)
 = ChoiceAlternativeDomain
 + Selection/Resolution Episode
 + Bearer-relative ActionSourceStanding
 + Evaluative/Practical Guidance Standing
 + Choice Attribution Standing
 + Scope / Granularity / Time
 + Standing Route
```

A choice is thus a bearer-attributable practical selection/resolution among choice-relevant alternatives.

This definition does **not** universally require:

```text
conscious deliberation
explicit enumeration of all alternatives
randomness
indeterminism
perfect rationality
utility maximization
preference completeness
verbal report
post-choice execution
```

### D8-011
**Choice ≠ mere selection.**

### D8-012
**Choice ≠ action by identity.** One can choose/settle on an action before performing it; a choice can also concern a goal, plan, policy or omission.

### D8-013
**Choice ≠ decision by identity.** Some choice tokens are transient or local selections that do not create a stable commitment/settlement standing.

# 6. Determinism does not erase ChoiceStanding

This is a major MF8-D falsifier.

A deterministic policy can map a condition to one action with probability 1:

```text
π(s) = a
```

or more generally:

```text
π(a | s) ∈ {0,1}
```

while a stochastic policy assigns a distribution over actions.

The difference concerns **selection law**, not automatically agential status.

Suppose bearer B has:

- a grounded action-alternative domain;
- a persistent policy that is part of its action-source organization;
- evaluative/action-guidance standing;
- token-level attribution for the resulting selection.

Then determinism of the policy does not remove those standings.

### D8-014
**DeterministicSelection ≠ NonChoice by identity.**

### D8-015
**StochasticSelection ≠ Choice by identity.** A random-number generator sampling actions does not gain agency merely through nondeterminism.

### D8-016
ChoiceStanding therefore does not require metaphysical openness of the universe at the token instant.

### D8-017
What matters at this ontology level is whether alternatives have practical standing inside the bearer-relative selection organization and whether the resolution is source/guidance attributable.

# 7. Alternative possibility is itself typed

MF8-D distinguishes at least:

```text
Counterfactual Physical Alternative
  world could physically evolve differently under changed conditions

Control Alternative
  controller had multiple admissible inputs under declared conditions

Policy Alternative
  policy domain contains differentiated candidate actions across relevant states

Represented Alternative
  bearer/model explicitly represents candidate

Considered Alternative
  candidate enters current comparison/deliberation

Available Alternative
  bearer has current capability/authority/access to execute candidate

Normatively Eligible Alternative
  candidate is permitted/acceptable under relevant norm
```

### D8-018
A genuine choice need not require all seven kinds simultaneously.

### D8-019
`Could have done otherwise` is therefore under-specified until the alternative standing and counterfactual intervention are declared.

# 9. Bounded rationality and satisficing

Herbert Simon's bounded-rationality program attacks the idealized assumption that a chooser has complete knowledge, a stable fully ordered preference system and unlimited computational ability. His practical-reasoning tradition motivates search and aspiration-level/satisficing structures rather than universal global optimization.

MF8-D translates this into ontology discipline:

```text
ChoiceStanding
≠ OptimalChoice
≠ ExhaustiveSearch
≠ ExpectedUtilityMaximization
```

### D8-022
A bearer can genuinely choose from a locally generated/accessible candidate set without representing or evaluating every physically possible action.

### D8-023
Search procedure and stopping criterion are part of ChoiceProfile when they materially determine the option set and selection.

### D8-024
Satisficing is a legitimate decision/choice strategy, not failed ontology.

# 10. Indecision, deferral and abstention

A theory of choice must also admit unresolved cases.

```text
IndecisionStanding
```

holds when a practical selection process remains unresolved among alternatives beyond a relevant decision point/horizon.

```text
DeferralStanding
```

holds when resolution is intentionally or procedurally postponed.

```text
AbstentionStanding
```

holds when withholding selection/authorization is itself selected under a declared option structure.

### D8-025
**NoChoice ≠ NoAgency.** An agent can fail to choose, defer or remain conflicted.

### D8-026
Abstention can itself be a choice when `do not select/authorize now` has ChoiceAlternativeStanding.

This parallels MF8-B's omission distinction.

# 11. RecommendationStanding

Recommendation is frequently confused with decision in human-AI systems.

Provisional:

```text
RecommendationStanding(r, S, D | A, K, Σ)
```

holds when source S produces an evaluatively/practically ranked or endorsed candidate `r` for a decision bearer/role D, under alternative set A and criterion K, **without the recommendation itself necessarily settling or authorizing the surrounding practical process**.

### D8-027
**Recommendation ≠ Choice of the recipient.**

### D8-028
**Recommendation ≠ Decision.**

### D8-029
A recommendation engine can have formal/local ChoiceStanding inside its own process while the surrounding human/institution retains final DecisionStanding.

# 13. DecisionStanding

MF8-D treats decision as a stronger settlement relation than bare choice.

Provisional:

```text
DecisionStanding(d, B | A, Σ)
 = Choice/Resolution Standing
 + Practical Settlement / Commitment Effect
 + Declared Decision Domain
 + Attribution / Authority Route
 + Temporal Validity
 + Revision/Reconsideration Conditions
```

A decision occurs when a bearer/role settles a practical question sufficiently to alter the standing of alternatives for subsequent action, planning, authorization or reasoning.

This can include:

- adopting an action;
- rejecting alternatives;
- setting a goal;
- selecting a plan;
- committing to a policy;
- approving/denying a proposal;
- deliberately deferring under a decision rule.

### D8-033
**Decision ≠ internal computation.** Evaluation may occur without settling anything.

### D8-034
**Decision ≠ Choice token universally.** A local action selection may not create a persisting settlement; a decision generally changes practical standing beyond the instantaneous comparison.

### D8-035
**Decision ≠ Execution.** A decision can remain unexecuted, fail, be revoked or be superseded.


# 14. Settlement does not require irrevocability

A decision need not be permanent.

The relevant contrast is:

```text
Open Practical Question
        ↓ decision
Provisionally Settled Practical Standing
        ↓ possible new evidence / failure / deadline / conflict
Reconsideration / Revision / Revocation
```

### D8-036
**Commitment ≠ Irrevocability.**

### D8-037
A revisable decision is still a decision if it changes subsequent reasoning/action standing until a declared reconsideration condition is met.

### D8-038
Constantly recomputing the same question from scratch is a different architecture from retaining a decision/commitment as a practical state.

This distinction prepares MF8-E's treatment of intention.

# 15. CommitmentStanding

Provisional:

```text
CommitmentStanding(c, B, X | Σ)
```

holds when bearer/role B adopts, accepts or is validly bound to X in a way that changes future practical reasoning/action: X gains persistence, default continuation, resource reservation, consistency pressure, means-end consequences, or special reconsideration conditions.

Commitment routes include:

```text
Personal / Practical
Delegated / Contractual
Institutional / Role
Formal System
Plan/Policy Adoption
Normative / Legal
```

### D8-039
Commitment ≠ preference strength.

### D8-040
Commitment ≠ desire. A bearer can be committed to an action it no longer strongly wants.

### D8-041
Commitment ≠ execution guarantee.

Cohen & Levesque's formal work explicitly analyzes intention through choice plus commitment and conditions for dropping goals; MF8-D treats this as a strong formal/intention route without reducing all decision or all agency to that particular theory.

# 16. Decision, commitment and intention

Keep:

```text
Choice
  = attributable practical selection/resolution token

Decision
  = practical settlement that changes subsequent standing

Commitment
  = persistence/binding organization attached to an adopted practical standing

Intention
  = richer action-directed practical commitment profile
    (full reconstruction deferred to MF8-E)
```

Therefore:

```text
Choice ≠ Decision
Decision ≠ Commitment
Commitment ≠ Intention
Decision ≠ Intention by identity
```

### D8-042
A decision can create an intention, but not every decision is an intention—for example a decision about classification, authorization or delegation may settle a practical question without itself being an intention to personally execute the action.

### D8-043
A local policy choice can occur without forming a durable intention.

# 17. PolicyStanding

MF8-A provisionally described policy as a mapping from relevant conditions/information to action selections/distributions. MF8-D now reconstructs its standing.

```text
PolicyStanding(π, B | I, A, Σ)
```

holds when rule/kernel/disposition/procedure `π` has grounded standing for bearer/system B as governing **conditional action selection or action-selection probabilities/priorities** from action domain A as a function of information/state/context domain I.

Policy routes include:

```text
Formal Deterministic Policy
Formal Stochastic Policy
Learned Policy
Biological/Behavioral Policy
Habitual Policy
Institutional Decision Rule
Designed Control Policy
Delegated Operational Policy
Meta-Policy
```

### D8-044
**Policy ≠ Choice token.** A policy is type-level/standing organization; a choice is one practical selection episode.

### D8-045
**Policy ≠ Action.** A policy can exist unused; an action can violate, override or bypass a policy.

### D8-046
**Policy ≠ Goal.** Policy specifies how selection is organized conditional on information; goal gives evaluative target standing.

### D8-047
**Policy ≠ Preference.** A policy may encode habit, constraint, exploration, rule-following or approximation rather than reveal a stable preference ordering.

### D8-048
**Policy ≠ Decision.** A decision can adopt/change a policy; a policy can generate many later choices without a new high-level decision each time.

### D8-049
A neural network can represent a policy, approximate a policy, participate in a larger policy, or merely predict actions; architecture syntax alone does not decide which.

### D8-050
A policy extracted by an analyst from behavior remains a represented/inferred policy until independently grounded in the target organization.

# 20. StrategyStanding

`Strategy` is another overloaded term.

MF8-D uses provisionally:

```text
StrategyStanding(S, B | Interaction, Horizon, Σ)
```

for a policy/plan family whose action guidance is explicitly organized with respect to temporally extended interaction, contingencies, opponents/other agents, or higher-level courses of action.

In game-theoretic formal routes, a strategy may specify actions across information sets/histories. In ordinary planning, `strategy` may instead denote a coarse plan or policy family.

### D8-053
**Strategy ≠ Policy universally.** Some formal strategies are policies over histories/information sets, but ordinary-language strategy can include goals, sequencing and resource commitments beyond a simple state→action mapping.

The exact strategy ontology can be specialized later in Game/Social foundations rather than forced into one MF8 primitive.

# 21. PlanStanding

A plan is not a predicted future trajectory.

Provisional primitive:

```text
PlanStanding(P, B | G, H, Σ)
```

holds when structured practical organization `P` has grounded standing for bearer/role B as a **temporally extended, typically partial and revisable organization of commitments, subgoals, actions, constraints, ordering relations, contingencies, resource assumptions and/or decision points** toward one or more goals/obligations G over horizon H.

A plan may contain:

```text
committed steps
open slots
subgoals
ordering/dependency relations
conditional branches
resource reservations
policy references
reconsideration conditions
fallbacks
termination conditions
coordination commitments
```

### D8-054
**Plan ≠ Complete Future Script.**

### D8-055
**Plan ≠ Trajectory.** A realized history can diverge from the plan while the plan remains identifiable and revisable.

### D8-056
**Plan ≠ Prediction.** A plan is practical/normative organization of what is to be done; a prediction represents what is expected to occur.

### D8-057
**Plan ≠ Schedule.** A schedule supplies temporal allocation/order; a plan may contain a schedule, but can exist with underspecified timing.

### D8-058
**Plan ≠ Policy.** A plan structures an extended project/commitment network; a policy conditionally governs selections, potentially indefinitely and across many projects.

# 22. Partiality is a feature, not a defect

Bratman's planning theory treats future-directed intentions as elements of partial plans that structure later practical reasoning. Bratman, Israel & Pollack likewise emphasize plans as devices for resource-bounded practical reasoning that constrain how much further deliberation is needed.

MF8-D therefore rejects:

```text
PlanStanding requires every future step to be precomputed.
```

### D8-059
A plan can intentionally leave details open for later information.

### D8-060
A plan can constrain later choice without eliminating later agency.

### D8-061
Plan adoption can reduce the future alternative set deliberately, which is a computational/practical resource-management function rather than evidence of lost agency.

# 23. Plan versus intention

Intentions and plans overlap but are not identical.

A bearer can intend one action without possessing an articulated multi-step plan. A plan can also be externally authored and merely recommended rather than adopted by the bearer.

MF8-D therefore distinguishes:

```text
PlanRepresentation
PlanProposal
AdoptedPlanStanding
PlanCommitmentStanding
PlanExecution
```

### D8-062
**External PlanProposal ≠ bearer's AdoptedPlanStanding.**

### D8-063
Adoption is a practical-standing transition: the plan begins to constrain subsequent choice/reasoning for the declared bearer/role.

Full IntentionStanding is deferred to MF8-E.

# 26. Delegation and decision authority

Delegation changes standing rather than merely moving computation.

Provisional:

```text
DecisionAuthorityStanding(B, D | Σ)
```

holds when bearer/role B is authorized under the relevant organizational/institutional/operational structure to settle decision domain D with recognized downstream effect.

And:

```text
DelegationStanding(P, A, D | Σ)
```

holds when principal P validly grants agent/subsystem A some authority/capability to choose, decide, authorize or execute within decision domain D and constraints.

### D8-069
**Delegation ≠ transfer of all goals/preferences/values.**

### D8-070
**Delegation ≠ abdication of all higher-level responsibility.** Responsibility is deferred to later MF8.

### D8-071
A delegated subsystem can possess genuine local DecisionStanding while its goals remain externally assigned and its decision domain bounded.

# 27. Deterministic delegated decision is still a decision candidate



### D8-072
**DecisionStanding can be institutional/formal/operational.**

### D8-073
Whether the executing software also has stronger autonomous AgencyStanding remains a separate claim.

# 31. Deterministic habit versus deterministic deliberative choice

Two systems can produce the same deterministic action token through very different practical organizations:

```text
Habitual/default route:
  cue → learned policy → action

Reflective route:
  candidate generation → conflict/evaluation → settlement → action
```

Both may instantiate choice standing at some route/scale, but their profiles differ in:

```text
consideration-set breadth
current evaluative sensitivity
deliberation
reconsiderability
reason responsiveness
goal sensitivity
habit strength
```

### D8-084
Determinism alone cannot distinguish habit from deliberative choice.

### D8-085
Choice richness should therefore be profiled through organization/evidence, not entropy of the output distribution.

# 34. Single-option cases

Ordinary language sometimes says `I had no choice` when only one viable option remains.

MF8-D distinguishes:

```text
Singleton Feasible Set
Plural Represented Set with one feasible member
Plural Practical Set with extreme cost asymmetry
Externally forced execution with no bearer governance
```

### D8-092
A minimal choice episode normally requires at least two differentiated ChoiceAlternativeStanding candidates at the relevant practical level.

### D8-093
A bearer can still **decide/endorse/accept** a forced or unique course, but this is not the same as selecting among live alternatives.

This prevents `decision` and `choice` from being forced into identity.

# 35. Policy adoption is itself a higher-level decision

A bearer can decide:

```text
Use policy π for domain D until condition R.
```

Subsequent selections can then occur automatically.

### D8-094
**Policy adoption can be one high-level decision that delegates many low-level choices to the adopted policy.**

### D8-095
This creates temporal compression of practical reasoning and explains how an agent can remain the higher-level author of a structured course without deliberating every micro-action.

This parallels the resource-bounded role of plans.

# 36. Plan adoption narrows future choices without eliminating them

Suppose a bearer adopts:

```text

```

The plan may settle the destination while leaving open:

```text
transport mode
route
meal timing
contingency handling
exact departure time
```

### D8-096
**Planning is selective closure of practical questions, not universal closure of agency.**

### D8-097

Triggers may include:

```text

failure

constraint violation
external revocation
authority change
conflict with higher-order commitment
```

SelectionStanding is clear; no practical/evaluative bearer attribution follows. **PASS:** selection ≠ choice.

## HC-D4 — Random action generator
Stochastic selection exists without evaluative/source authorship. **PASS:** randomness ≠ choice.

## HC-D5 — Human picks bitter medicine over sweets
Need/goal/norm can outweigh immediate liking/wanting. Choice exists without one scalar evaluative route. **PASS.**

## HC-D6 — Habitual route to work
A learned policy can select a route with little current deliberation. This may retain practical choice at one scale while having low current deliberative richness. **PASS.**

## HC-D7 — Simon-style satisficing
A bearer searches a bounded candidate set and selects the first option meeting an aspiration/acceptability threshold. **PASS:** genuine choice need not maximize a global utility over all physical alternatives.

## HC-D8 — Coerced decision
Threat changes costs and autonomy; practical alternatives may remain. **PASS:** coercion ≠ no choice universally.

## HC-D9 — Arm physically forced to press button
Physical outcome occurs while bearer-level alternative resolution is bypassed. **PASS:** effect ≠ choice.

## HC-D10 — Model recommends, human approves
Model has RecommendationStanding and possibly local formal ChoiceStanding; human/institution has final Authorization/DecisionStanding. **PASS.**

## HC-D11 — Automated approval rule with delegated authority
Deterministic software can instantiate institutional DecisionStanding if the institution validly configures the rule's output to settle the domain. This does not prove intrinsic machine goals. **PASS.**

## HC-D12 — Plan generated by planner but rejected
Planning/PlanProposalStanding exists; AdoptedPlanStanding does not. **PASS:** generated plan ≠ adopted plan.

## HC-D13 — Partial plan with future open slots
PlanStanding survives despite unspecified future choices. **PASS:** plan ≠ complete script.

## HC-D14 — Prediction exactly matches later history
PredictionStanding may be perfect while PlanStanding is absent. **PASS:** plan ≠ prediction/trajectory.

## HC-D15 — Plan fails in execution
Plan/Decision/Commitment standing can remain historically valid despite failed realization. **PASS:** plan/decision ≠ success.

## HC-D16 — Institution delegates bounded decisions to agent
Local DecisionAuthorityStanding can belong to delegate; principal retains higher-level delegation/governance standing. **PASS:** decision attribution is multi-route.

# 50. Evidence anchors

Primary/authoritative anchors used in MF8-D:

1. **Herbert A. Simon (1955), `A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice`, Quarterly Journal of Economics 69(1), 99–118, DOI `10.2307/1884852`.** Used to attack unlimited-information/unlimited-computation assumptions and support bounded candidate search/satisficing as legitimate practical reasoning rather than failed choice.
2. **Michael E. Bratman (1987), `Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason`, Harvard University Press; reissued by CSLI.** Planning theory of intention; intentions as elements of partial plans that organize practical reasoning across time.
3. **Michael E. Bratman, David J. Israel & Martha E. Pollack (1988), `Plans and Resource-Bounded Practical Reasoning`, Computational Intelligence 4(3), 349–355, DOI `10.1111/j.1467-8640.1988.tb00284.x`.** Plans constrain further practical reasoning under resource bounds; used against `plan = fully precomputed script`.
4. **Philip R. Cohen & Hector J. Levesque (1990), `Intention is Choice with Commitment`, Artificial Intelligence 42(2–3), 213–261, DOI `10.1016/0004-3702(90)90055-5`.** Formalizes relations among choice, commitment, goals and intentions; used to separate transient choice from persistent commitment/intention.
5. **Richard S. Sutton & Andrew G. Barto, `Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction`, 2nd ed.** Formal policy/action/value framework; used as evidence that deterministic and stochastic policy are selection-law distinctions within formal RL rather than agency ontology by themselves.
6. **Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman (1981), `The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice`, Science 211.** Retained from MF8-C as evidence that decision/choice organization cannot assume one context-free stable preference ordering.
7. **Sarah Lichtenstein & Paul Slovic (1971), `Reversals of preference between bids and choices in gambling decisions`, Journal of Experimental Psychology 89.** Retained from MF8-C to motivate elicitation-route dependence and separation of preference from choice.

Competing philosophical theories of free will, reasons, intention and rationality are not adjudicated here. MF8-D only requires that the ontology not smuggle one of them into minimal ChoiceStanding.

# 51. MF8-D verdict

The deepest reconstruction is:

```text
SELECTION
 = mechanism resolves/routes among alternatives

CHOICE
 = bearer-attributable practical selection among
   ChoiceAlternativeStanding candidates
 + source/guidance/attribution

DECISION
 = choice/resolution that settles a practical question
   and changes subsequent standing until revision/reconsideration

POLICY
 = conditional action-selection organization
   across information/state/context

PLAN
 = temporally extended, partial, revisable organization
   of commitments/subgoals/actions/constraints/contingencies
```

The decisive consequences are:

> **Choice does not require randomness. Determinism describes how a selection is resolved, not whether the alternative domain and resolution belong to an agential practical organization. Conversely, randomness does not create agency.**

and:

> **A decision is not whoever computed the highest score. It is the bearer/role whose resolution has standing to settle the practical question. Recommendation, choice, authorization and execution can therefore belong to different components.**

and:

> **A plan is not a future trajectory. It is a partial practical commitment structure that deliberately closes some questions while leaving others open, thereby reducing repeated reasoning under resource bounds.**

This gives MF8 a clean architecture for humans, organisms, institutions and artificial systems without forcing them into one utility-maximizing or consciously deliberative template.
