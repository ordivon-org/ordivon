# 2. Working definitions for destructive testing

## 2.1 Materialization

```text
MaterializationStanding(D,B,t | Σ)
```

means that distinctions `D` are actually instantiated/realized in a bearer `B` at time/scope `t,Σ` such that the bearer has a non-arbitrary physical/computational/representational configuration corresponding to those distinctions.

Materialization does **not** imply long persistence.

A transient electrical, optical, neural or computational state may be materialized for milliseconds.

## 2.2 Inscription

Working candidate:

> a transformation that establishes or updates a bearer configuration so that distinctions remain available beyond the immediate producing micro-event under some persistence/readout criterion.

This is deliberately weaker than permanent storage.

## 2.3 Capture / Recording

Working candidate:

> a source/event/process-relative transformation that produces a new token/trace whose distinctions are grounded as standing for, measuring, replaying or otherwise preserving selected aspects of the source occurrence.

Thus recording normally adds a provenance/grounding relation from a source occurrence to a later token.

## 2.4 Fixation

`Fixation` is **regime-relative**.

In U.S. copyright law it is a legal status requiring an authorized embodiment in a copy/phonorecord sufficiently permanent or stable for more than transitory duration; transmitted sounds/images can qualify where fixation is made simultaneously with transmission.

This legal standing must not be promoted into a universal Media primitive.

## 2.5 Persistence / Durability

Persistence is continuation through time under a declared identity criterion.

Durability is a profile of expected/supported persistence under environmental, maintenance and readout conditions.

## 2.6 Fixity

In digital-preservation practice, PREMIS `fixity` is evidence used to determine whether a file/bitstream has changed, typically through a message digest.

Thus:

```text
Fixation ≠ Fixity
```

## 2.7 Preservation / Archive

Preservation adds policies, representation information, stewardship, monitoring, migration and future-access obligations.

Therefore:

```text
Persistence ≠ Preservation
```

# 72. Research anchors used

Representative authoritative/comparison anchors:

- 17 U.S.C. §101 and U.S. Copyright Office Title 17 materials — legal `fixation` is an authorized embodiment in a copy/phonorecord sufficiently stable for more than transitory duration; live transmitted sounds/images can count as fixed when fixation is made simultaneously.
- U.S. Copyright Act legislative material associated with §102 — explicitly distinguishes purely evanescent/transient reproductions from sufficiently permanent embodiments and discusses simultaneous live recording/transmission.
- Library of Congress PREMIS Data Dictionary v3.0 — preservation metadata data model for Objects, Events, Rights and Agents; file/bitstream `fixity` is represented through message-digest information and is only one part of preservation metadata.
- CCSDS 650.0-M-3, *Reference Model for an Open Archival Information System (OAIS)*, Issue 3, December 2024 — current OAIS reference model; archival standing concerns an organization accepting responsibility to preserve information and make it available to a Designated Community, not mere bit persistence.
- MF1/MF3/MF4/MF6/MF7 frozen Ordivon foundations plus Round B-E/B-G results.

These are falsification/comparison anchors rather than external authority over Ordivon's ontology.
