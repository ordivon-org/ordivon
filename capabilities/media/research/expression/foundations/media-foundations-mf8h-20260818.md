# 2. Plurality, collection and aggregate

```text
PluralityStanding({B_i} | Σ)
```

means multiple distinguishable bearer candidates/items are present under scope.

```text
CollectionStanding(C, {B_i} | Rule, Σ)
```

means a set/multiset/classification C collects members under a declared inclusion rule.

Examples include all people in a room, all cars on a road, all current market participants or all servers carrying one tag.

### H8-002
**Plurality ≠ CollectiveBearer.**

### H8-003
**CollectionStanding ≠ CollectiveAgency.**

### H8-004
Analyst-defined membership is insufficient for target group bearer standing.

A collection can also have an aggregate property:

```text
AggregateStanding(A, C | F, Σ)
```

where A is constituted/computed from member properties through F—for example mean income, vote count, center of mass, average velocity or total inventory.

### H8-005
**Aggregate property ≠ group-level attitude/state by identity.**

### H8-006
A majority statistic is not automatically a group's decision unless an organization/constitution makes that aggregation authoritative.

```text
CoordinationStanding(E, {B_i} | K, Σ)
```

```text
SynchronizationStanding(E, {B_i} | V, Σ)
```

### H8-007
**Coordination ≠ CollectiveAgency.**

### H8-008

Traffic, flock alignment and clock synchronization can exist without a group bearer that chooses the resulting pattern.

Social-insect self-organization is a hard falsifier: local interactions among simple individuals can generate highly structured collective behavior and powerful problem solving without centralized control.

### H8-009
**Macroscopic organization does not prove group-level evaluative orientation or authorship.**

### H8-010
Emergence can establish collective capability while remaining neutral on CollectiveAgencyStanding.

```text
 + grounded shared/joint activity organization
 + participant-sensitive coordination/commitment/support as route requires
 + joint-action attribution at declared scale
 + scope/provenance
```

### H8-011
**JointActionStanding ≠ simultaneous action.**

### H8-012
**JointActionStanding ≠ merely externally coordinated movement.**

### H8-013
**JointActionStanding ≠ CollectiveAgencyStanding.** Two people can jointly carry a table without constituting a third persistent agent.

# 5. Rival theories of joint action remain live

MF8-H deliberately preserves competing routes.

- **Bratman route:** interlocking intentions, mutual responsiveness and commitment to mutual support.
- **Cohen–Levesque route:** persistent joint goals/intentions and commitments distinguish teamwork from coincident coordinated activity.
- **Searle route:** primitive `we-intention` rather than reduction to separate I-intentions plus beliefs.
- **Gilbert route:** joint commitment/plural-subject standing explains normative relations that merely matching private goals may not capture.
- **Grosz–Kraus SharedPlans route:** partial shared plans, commitment, partial knowledge and contracted subactivities organize collaboration without requiring one irreducible group-mind primitive.

### H8-014
These are rival joint-action standing routes, not universal proofs of persistent collective agency.

### H8-015
Their common lesson is that joint action requires more practical integration than coincident coordination.

```text
CollectiveBearerStanding(G | Σ)

 + Typed Internal Organization / Role Structure
 + Group-Level State/Standing Domain
```

### H8-022
**CollectiveBearerStanding ≠ CollectiveAgencyStanding.**

### H8-023

Potential candidates include committees, corporations, sports teams, military units, formal software organizations and some organismal colonies.

# 11. Aggregation versus collective decision

```text
AggregationStanding(Y, {x_i} | F, Σ)
```

A group decision requires:

```text
CollectiveDecisionStanding(d, G | D, Σ)
 = valid CollectiveBearer G
 + constituted decision domain D
 + recognized decision procedure/authority
 + member/input aggregation or deliberation route as applicable
 + group-level practical settlement effect
 + validity/revision rules
 + group attribution
```

### H8-036
**Vote count ≠ CollectiveDecision automatically.**

### H8-037
The same 6–4 tally can be a binding decision inside a constituted committee and merely a poll result in a crowd.

### H8-038
CollectiveDecisionStanding depends on constitution/authority, not numerical aggregation alone.

List–Pettit judgment aggregation provides a decisive anti-reduction case: coherent individual judgment sets can fail to aggregate into a coherent group set under plausible issue-wise rules, motivating explicit collective attitude/decision organization.

### H8-039
**Collective attitude ≠ issue-wise majority attitude by identity.**

### H8-040
A constituted group judgment may diverge from every individual's complete judgment set while remaining traceable to group procedure.

### H8-041
This yields group-level partial autonomy without mysterious collective consciousness.

# 17. Corporation, committee and team hard cases

### H8-064
Corporations are strong InstitutionalAgencyStanding candidates where the relevant organization is actually present.

### H8-065

### H8-066

### H8-067

### H8-069

### H8-070

# 26. Evidence batteries

## CollectiveBearer evidence

1. **Boundary/membership** — non-arbitrary inclusion/exclusion rules?
2. **Persistence** — continuation through member/state changes?
3. **Role/organization** — durable internal organization?
4. **Group-level state** — standings beyond snapshot aggregates?
5. **Termination/fission/fusion** — identifiable dissolution/split/merge criteria?

### H8-113
Failure here weakens CollectiveBearerStanding before agency is considered.

## CollectiveAgency evidence

1. Group evaluative orientation.
2. Group-level practical alternative domain.
3. Binding group decision settlement.
4. Collective action-source organization.
5. Group-level action attribution.
6. Persistence through member turnover.
7. Intervention on constitution/policy/decision procedure changes group behavior.
8. Dissent test: valid group position survives some disagreement.
9. Analyst/representation firewall: group goal is organizationally grounded rather than grammatical summary.

### H8-114
Collective agency requires convergent evidence across bearer/source/evaluation/decision/action standing.

# 32. Evidence anchors

4. **Margaret Gilbert (1990), `Walking Together: A Paradigmatic Social Phenomenon`, Midwest Studies in Philosophy 15, DOI `10.1111/j.1475-4975.1990.tb00202.x`.** Plural-subject/joint-commitment route and normative structure of joint action.
5. **Barbara J. Grosz & Sarit Kraus (1996), `Collaborative Plans for Complex Group Action`, Artificial Intelligence 86(2), DOI `10.1016/0004-3702(95)00103-4`.** SharedPlans with partial knowledge/plans, commitment and collaborative decomposition.
6. **Milind Tambe (1997), `Towards Flexible Teamwork`, Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research 7, DOI `10.1613/JAIR.433`.** Implemented STEAM teamwork with joint intentions, monitoring, communication and reorganization.

# 33. MF8-H verdict

```text
COLLECTION
 = members grouped under a rule

COORDINATION
 = member states/actions organized/interdependent

JOINT ACTION
 = multiple agents' actions practically integrated
   as contributions to one joint activity

COLLECTIVE BEARER
 = constituted group with boundary/organization/state/persistence
   beyond an analyst-defined member snapshot

COLLECTIVE AGENCY
 = CollectiveBearer
 + group-level evaluative orientation
 + group-level choice/decision organization
 + persistent collective action source
 + group action repertoire/attribution
 + autonomy/delegation profile
```

Consequences:

> **Two agents can act jointly without creating a third persistent agent.**

> **A group can possess valid goals, judgments, policies and decisions while some members disagree, if those standings are constituted through genuine group organization rather than grammatical shorthand.**

> **Member turnover is compatible with collective identity when roles, constitution, memory, policy and decision procedures preserve the bearer.**

> **Swarm intelligence, crowd dynamics and markets demonstrate that impressive emergent coordination/capability do not themselves establish a group agent.**

> **Corporations, committees and sufficiently organized artificial teams are stronger candidates because they can possess persistent bearer identity, group-level decisions, policies, memory, authority and attributable action.**

> **Collective agency does not erase individual agency or responsibility; both can be nested across member, team and institutional scales.**
