# 1. Problem statement

MF4-E and MF4-F established independent temporal and spatial organization. Yet many media wholes are heterogeneous:

- audiovisual speech;
- film/video;
- diagram + caption + legend;
- map + labels;
- speech + gesture;
- visual + haptic object exploration;
- sensor arrays;
- multimodal interfaces;
- tactile sensory substitution;
- text–image documents;
- multimodal AI systems.

The naive model is:

`multiple modalities present -> align them -> fuse them -> one multimodal representation`.

This fails in several ways:

1. modalities can co-occur without belonging together;
2. cues can correspond without sharing one cause;
3. a system can integrate decisions while preserving modality-specific states;
4. reliability weighting is not equivalent to semantic fusion;
5. contradictory modalities may be kept separate rather than averaged;
6. complementary modalities can add different information rather than redundant estimates of one variable;
7. a standing cross-medium artifact can be compositionally unified without any current perceptual fusion;
8. sensory substitution shows function can cross carrier/modality boundaries without making carriers identical;
9. engineered multimodal architectures can coordinate loosely coupled modality components through explicit interfaces rather than one shared latent state.

MF4-G therefore attacks:

`Modality = Channel = Carrier = Source = Representation Mode`;

and:

`Co-presence = Alignment = Correspondence = Common Cause = Integration = Fusion`.

# 2. Modality must be typed

`Modality` can refer to different things:

- sensory modality: vision, audition, touch, proprioception, etc.;
- interaction modality: speech, handwriting, gesture, keyboard, pointing;
- representational mode: text, image, diagram, audio, animation;
- physical carrier/channel: photons, pressure waves, electrical signals, tactile actuators;
- computational stream/component.

### Result

**CG-01 — `Modality` is not one natural universal category; claims must specify sensory, interaction, representational, carrier or computational sense.**

---

# 3. Modality ≠ Channel

One sensory modality can have multiple channels/streams.

Examples:

- two microphones are two channels within audition;
- RGB channels are not three sensory modalities;
- left/right audio channels are one auditory modality with multiple signal channels.

### Result

**CG-02 — Modality and signal/channel multiplicity are distinct.**

---

# 4. Channel ≠ Source

One source can project into many channels, and one channel can contain mixtures of many sources.

### Result

**CG-03 — Signal channel identity and world-source identity must remain distinct.**

---

# 5. Carrier ≠ Modality function

A visual scene can be encoded into tactile stimulation in sensory substitution.

### Result

**CG-04 — Physical carrier/receptor pathway and perceptual/informational function are distinct.**

---

# 6. Representational mode ≠ Sensory modality

Written text and diagrams are visually sensed but different representational modes.

### Result

**CG-05 — Sensory input modality does not uniquely determine representational mode/content.**

# 7. One representational mode can cross sensory modalities

# 19. McGurk gives a strong integration falsifier

**CG-18 — Visual speech information can change auditory speech perception; speech perception cannot be treated as purely auditory under multimodal viewing conditions.**

**CG-23 — Under suitable common-variable conditions, multisensory estimates can be combined with weights related to cue reliability rather than fixed modality priority.**

**CG-24 — Cue-combination rule and internal representational architecture are distinct.**

# 26. Reliability is variable-specific

# 27. Reliability is context-dependent

**CG-26 — Multimodal weighting can change dynamically as evidence quality changes.**

# 28. Alais–Burr falsify fixed visual capture

**CG-27 — Ventriloquism-like spatial integration is better characterized as reliability-sensitive cue combination than universal visual dominance.**

# 153. Multimodal error taxonomy

## Missing modality

Expected stream unavailable.

## Spurious modality/stream

Unrelated stream included.

## Misalignment

Timing/spatial alignment outside intended profile.

## Misbinding

Correct streams assigned to wrong source/role.

## Common-cause error

Independent causes fused or one cause incorrectly split.

## Reliability-weighting error

Evidence weights inappropriate to uncertainty/calibration.

## Fixed-dominance error

One modality privileged regardless of reliability/task.

## Over-fusion

Conflict/provenance/modality-specific distinctions erased too early.

## Under-integration

Complementary/synergistic cues fail to interact when useful.

## Cross-modal contradiction

Content disagreement inconsistent with intended relation.

## False redundancy

Derived/correlated streams double-counted as independent evidence.

## Mapping/code error

Cross-modal transformation incorrectly preserves/assigns distinctions.

## Synchronization error

Lag/drift exceeds intended tolerance.

## Source identity switch

Cross-modal token assignments swap.

## Provenance collapse

Per-modality/derivation histories lost.

## Accessibility loss

Transformation omits task-critical distinctions.

## Shared-latent overclaim

Common vector space treated as semantic fusion without grounding/use evidence.

## Modality ablation fragility

Whole fails unexpectedly when a nominally redundant stream is absent.

## Unintended dominance

A high-salience/precise modality suppresses task-critical information from another.

### Result

**CG-152 — Multimodal failure is a typed relational family, not one fusion accuracy score.**

# 158. Provisional multimodal composition profiles

## M0 — Co-present multimodality

Multiple modality/media streams share context but need not interact strongly.

## M1 — Correspondence-linked multimodality

Typed mappings relate units across modes.

## M2 — Common-source/target cue integration

Streams jointly constrain one source/property under causal/reliability assumptions.

## M3 — Complementary role composition

Different modalities contribute different role/filler/content structure.

## M4 — Transform/substitution composition

Information is converted across carriers/modalities while selected functions/content survive.

## M5 — Coordinated multimodal interaction

Separate modality components are orchestrated through interfaces/action/dialog state.

## M6 — Fused/latent multimodal state

A system builds a shared integrated state/representation, if independently evidenced.

## M7 — Adaptive/conflict-aware multimodal composition

The system maintains source uncertainty, recalibrates, preserves disagreement/provenance and changes integration policy dynamically.

These overlap and are not a mandatory ladder.

# 160. Provisional axioms CG-01→CG-153 — compressed core

**CG-01–08** Modality, channel, carrier, source, sensory modality, representational mode, multisensory and multimedia are distinct typed categories.

**CG-09–17** Co-presence, alignment, correspondence, common cause, integration and fusion form increasingly stronger but non-equivalent relation classes; fusion must be level-typed.

**CG-28–36** Common target variable, common source and cue reliability differ; Körding-style causal inference makes integration conditional/probabilistic and conflict informative.

**CG-37–47** Redundancy, complementarity, synergy, binding cues, temporal/spatial correspondence and semantic relations are distinct; no one cue defines multimodal composition.

**CG-131–145** Accessibility transformations, content invariants, asymmetric recoverability, whole persistence, synchronization metadata and specified/inferred correspondence expand cross-medium composition beyond sensory fusion.

**CG-146–153** Multiple consumers, typed uncertainty, availability, rich error taxonomy and evidence profiles complete the provisional ontology.

# 162. Primary/original/official literature anchors

- McGurk, H. & MacDonald, J. (1976), `Hearing lips and seeing voices`, *Nature* 264, 746–748. DOI: 10.1038/264746a0. Demonstrates visual articulation can qualitatively alter speech percepts from incongruent auditory input.
- Shams, L., Kamitani, Y. & Shimojo, S. (2000), `What you see is what you hear`, *Nature* 408, 788. DOI: 10.1038/35048669. Auditory beeps can alter perceived visual flash count, falsifying fixed visual-dominance assumptions.
- Ernst, M. O. & Banks, M. S. (2002), `Humans integrate visual and haptic information in a statistically optimal fashion`, *Nature* 415, 429–433. DOI: 10.1038/415429a. Reliability/variance-sensitive visual–haptic cue combination for a common property.
- Alais, D. & Burr, D. (2004), `The Ventriloquist Effect Results from Near-Optimal Bimodal Integration`, *Current Biology* 14(3), 257–262. DOI: 10.1016/j.cub.2004.01.029. Audiovisual localization influence shifts with visual reliability; bimodal precision can improve beyond either modality alone.
- Körding, K. P., Beierholm, U., Ma, W. J., Quartz, S., Tenenbaum, J. B. & Shams, L. (2007), `Causal Inference in Multisensory Perception`, *PLoS ONE* 2(9):e943. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0000943. Models audiovisual localization as probabilistic inference over common versus separate causes plus source location, providing a hard falsifier of mandatory cue fusion.
- Bach-y-Rita, P., Collins, C. C., Saunders, F. A., White, B. & Scadden, L. (1969), `Vision Substitution by Tactile Image Projection`, *Nature* 221, 963–964. DOI: 10.1038/221963a0. Camera information transformed into tactile stimulation provides a canonical carrier/function separation case.
- Sumby, W. H. & Pollack, I. (1954), `Visual Contribution to Speech Intelligibility in Noise`, *Journal of the Acoustical Society of America* 26(2), 212–215. DOI: 10.1121/1.1907309. Visual observation of speaker movements improves speech intelligibility, especially where auditory opportunity for improvement is high.
- W3C Multimodal Interaction Working Group (2012), `Multimodal Architecture and Interfaces`, W3C Recommendation 25 October 2012. Defines a loosely coupled, potentially distributed multimodal UI architecture with modality components, interaction manager and well-defined lifecycle/interfaces; strong engineered counterexample to shared-state/co-location necessity.

# 164. Deepest MF4-G conclusion

The strongest surviving candidate is:

> **A multimodal or cross-medium composition exists when units, streams or representations carried through different sensory, interaction, representational or computational modes participate in typed cross-mode relations—such as correspondence, common-source/target evidence, complementarity, redundancy, substitution, synchronization, labeling, control or coordinated action—that are counterfactually relevant to the identity, content, perception, operation or evaluation of a whole under a declared scope.**

And:

> **Integration is conditional organization, not synonymous with fusion: a multimodal whole may preserve modality-specific states, disagreement, provenance and separate implementations while still coordinating them through causal inference, reliability weighting, role bindings, transformations or interfaces.**

Compactly:

`MultimodalComposition = Modality-Specific Units + Typed Cross-Modal Relations + Integration/Segregation Policy + Whole/Scope`.
