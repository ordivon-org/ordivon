# 0. Purpose

```text

awareness
phenomenology
subjectivity
access
report
wakefulness
arousal
```

These terms are routinely used as if they named one phenomenon. They do not.

MF9-A does **not** select Global Neuronal Workspace, recurrent processing, higher-order theory, Integrated Information Theory, predictive processing, attention schema or any other theory as the explanation of consciousness.

The first task is lower-level:

> **What distinct standings must exist before rival theories can even disagree clearly about experience?**

MF9-A therefore performs term separation, builds epistemic firewalls and identifies the minimal open problem that later rounds must falsify.

# 2. `Consciousness` is not accepted as one primitive

Block's phenomenal/access distinction is sufficient to demonstrate a fundamental term-separation problem: `phenomenal consciousness` concerns experience or what-it-is-like character, while `access consciousness` concerns availability for reasoning and rational control of speech/action.

MF9-A therefore rejects the bare primitive:

```text
Consciousness(X)
```

unless the intended standing is typed.

### E9-002
**Consciousness is an overloaded family label, not a frozen primitive.**

At minimum, claims must distinguish:

```text
Experiential / Phenomenal Standing
Conscious Access Standing
Creature/Global Conscious-State Standing
Content Awareness Standing
Wakefulness/Arousal Standing
Metacognitive/Self Awareness Standing
Report Standing
```

### E9-003
`conscious` as an adjective must never silently move between these standings.

# 3. ExperienceStanding — provisional target primitive

MF9-A introduces the deliberately phenomenology-facing claim form:

```text
ExperienceStanding(E, B | Σ)
```

where:

- `B` = candidate experiencing bearer/subject;
- `E` = experiential episode/state/content under discussion;
- `Σ` = temporal, modal, evidential and epistemic scope.

Provisional meaning:

> **There is something it is like for bearer B in/through E under the declared scope.**

This is intentionally **not yet a reductive computational definition**.

Why?

If MF9-A defined experience as report, access, attention, representation, integration, self-modeling or recurrent processing, it would simply encode one contested theory into the ontology before falsification.

### E9-004
`ExperienceStanding` is therefore an **explanandum standing** at MF9-A, not yet an implementation criterion.

### E9-005
Evidence for ExperienceStanding and the constitutive basis of ExperienceStanding must remain separate.

# 4. ExperienceClaim and evidence route

Because experience is not directly third-person observable in the same way as a voltage or emitted token, every scientific attribution needs an explicit evidence route.

```text
ExperienceClaim = <
  Candidate Bearer,
  Candidate Experience,
  Experiential Domain/Content,
  First-Person Evidence?,
  Behavioral Evidence?,
  Physiological/Neural Evidence?,
  Causal/Intervention Evidence?,
  Comparative/Structural Evidence?,
  Theory-Dependent Inference?,
  Alternative Explanations,
  Uncertainty,
  Scope
>
```

### E9-006
**EvidenceOfExperience ≠ Experience by identity.**

### E9-007
**AbsenceOfReport ≠ AbsenceOfExperience.**

No-report paradigms are especially important here because report requirements introduce additional perceptual, decisional, working-memory and motor/report processes that can contaminate attempts to identify the neural correlates of experience.

### E9-008
**PresenceOfReport ≠ proof that the reported phenomenal content occurred exactly as described.** Report itself is another action/representation standing with reliability limits.

# 6. ExperientialContentStanding

MF9-A provisionally separates occurrence from content:

```text
ExperientialContentStanding(C, E, B | Route, Σ)
```

means C is what experience E presents/is experienced as being about or containing under a declared experiential-content route.

This definition remains neutral between:

```text
representationalist theories

enactive/sensorimotor routes
higher-order routes
IIT-like intrinsic-structure routes
```

### E9-012
**Experience occurrence ≠ particular content.**

### E9-013
**RepresentationStanding ≠ ExperientialContentStanding.**

A system can contain unconscious representations; an experience claim requires additional standing.

### E9-014
Later rounds must determine whether all phenomenal character is representational content, partly representational, or not exhaustively representational.

# 7. SubjectStanding / SubjectivityStanding

Experience is bearer-relative in a way ordinary information processing need not be.

MF9-A introduces:

```text
SubjectOfExperienceStanding(B, E | Σ)
```

when E is attributed as experience **for B**.

And:

```text
SubjectivityStanding(E, B | Σ)
```

captures the first-personal/for-the-bearer character of an experience claim.

### E9-015
**SubjectOfExperienceStanding ≠ explicit SelfModelStanding.**

An experience can be for a bearer without the bearer representing itself as `me`, naming itself, possessing autobiographical memory or reflecting on its own existence.

### E9-016
**Subjectivity ≠ introspective reportability.**

### E9-017
**Subjectivity ≠ agency.**

MF8 agency remains frozen independently.

# 8. Creature/Global state versus local/content experience

`B is conscious` can mean a global organism/system state; `B is conscious of X` concerns a content relation.

MF9-A separates:

```text
ExperientialStateStanding(B | H, Σ)
```

for whether bearer B is in a state/horizon supporting one or more experiences,

from:

```text
ExperienceStanding(E, B)
ExperientialContentStanding(C, E, B)
```

for particular episodes/content.

### E9-018
**Global conscious-state standing ≠ every possible content being experienced.**

### E9-019
**Lack of one content experience ≠ globally unconscious bearer.**

This state/content distinction is required before discussing sleep, anesthesia, disorders of consciousness, masking or inattentional phenomena.

### E9-021

### E9-022


The exact architecture remains theory-dependent.

### E9-025
**ConsciousAccessStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding by definition.**

### E9-026
**GlobalAvailability ≠ Report by identity.**

### E9-027
GNW is retained as a major rival model of conscious access/processing, not frozen as the definition of experience.

# 19. SenseOfAgencyStanding

MF8 froze:


```text
SenseOfAgencyStanding(SOA, B, A/O | Σ)
```

means B has an experienced/subjective standing of initiating, controlling or authoring action A and/or outcome O.

# 46. Evidence anchors

1. **Ned Block (1995), `On a Confusion about a Function of Consciousness`, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 18(2), 227–247, DOI `10.1017/S0140525X00038188`.** Canonical phenomenal-consciousness versus access-consciousness distinction and warning against treating consciousness as one concept.
2. **Stanislas Dehaene & Lionel Naccache (2001), `Towards a cognitive neuroscience of consciousness: basic evidence and a workspace framework`, Cognition 79, 1–37, DOI `10.1016/S0010-0277(00)00123-2`.** Major global-workspace/access rival model; explicitly distinguishes extensive unconscious processing from specific conscious-access functions.
3. **Stanislas Dehaene & Jean-Pierre Changeux (2011), `Experimental and theoretical approaches to conscious processing`, Neuron 70(2), 200–227, DOI `10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018`.** Experimental/theoretical GNW synthesis retained as a rival model rather than ontology definition.
4. **Victor A. F. Lamme (2006), `Towards a true neural stance on consciousness`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 10(11), 494–501, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2006.09.001`.** Recurrent-processing/neural criterion rival that challenges behavioral/report-centered definitions.
5. **Naotsugu Tsuchiya, Melanie Wilke, Stefan Frässle & Victor A. F. Lamme (2015), `No-Report Paradigms: Extracting the True Neural Correlates of Consciousness`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 19(12), 757–770, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2015.10.002`.** Report-confound and no-report evidence firewall.
6. **Christof Koch & Naotsugu Tsuchiya (2007), `Attention and consciousness: two distinct brain processes`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 11(1), 16–22, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2006.10.012`.** Psychophysical dissociations supporting term separation of attention and consciousness.
7. **Francesca Siclari et al. (2017), `The neural correlates of dreaming`, Nature Neuroscience 20, 872–878, DOI `10.1038/nn.4545`.** Sleep/dream evidence forcing wakefulness and experience apart and supporting state/content distinctions.
8. **Patrick Haggard (2017), `Sense of agency in the human brain`, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 18, 196–207, DOI `10.1038/nrn.2017.14`.** Sense of agency as subjective experience of action/outcome control, distinct from objective MF8 agency/authorship.
9. **Matthew Botvinick & Jonathan Cohen (1998), `Rubber hands 'feel' touch that eyes see`, Nature 391, 756, DOI `10.1038/35784`.** Multisensory body-ownership illusion supporting separation of physical body membership and experienced ownership.
10. **Stephen M. Fleming & Hakwan C. Lau (2014), `How to measure metacognition`, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 8:443, DOI `10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443`.** Distinguishes metacognitive bias, sensitivity and efficiency and supplies measurement discipline for self-monitoring claims.

MF9-A uses these anchors to separate targets, not to select a winner among consciousness theories.

# 48. MF9-A verdict

The first major result of MF9 is negative but foundational:

> **There is no defensible single primitive called `consciousness` that can safely stand for experience, access, awareness, reportability, wakefulness, attention, metacognition and self-awareness at once.**

The provisional center is instead:

```text
EXPERIENCE
 = bearer-relative phenomenal occurrence/explanandum

PHENOMENAL CHARACTER
 = qualitative/structural manner of that experience

EXPERIENTIAL CONTENT
 = what/how something is presented within the experience

CONSCIOUS ACCESS
 = broad downstream functional availability

REPORT
 = an action/communication about a state/content

WAKEFULNESS / AROUSAL
 = global state dimensions

METACOGNITION
 = monitoring/evaluation of first-order processes

SENSE OF AGENCY / OWNERSHIP
 = specific experiential self/action/body profiles
```

and every experience claim requires an explicit evidence route.

MF9-A therefore establishes the ontology needed to ask the real question in later rounds:

> **What structure does experience itself have, and what—if anything—constitutes or realizes that structure?**
