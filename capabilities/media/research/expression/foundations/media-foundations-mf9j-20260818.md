# 0. Purpose

MF9-A→I repeatedly used phrases such as:

```text
consciousness supports flexible behavior
consciousness supports learning
consciousness supports integration
pain motivates avoidance
experience grounds value
awareness improves attention control
```

Those claims are dangerously ambiguous.

They can mean at least:

```text
A. a neural/functional mechanism correlated with experience performs function F;
B. ExperienceStanding itself currently contributes causally to F;
C. ExperienceStanding was historically selected because it produced F;
D. the underlying mechanism was selected for F and experience came along as a consequence;
E. experience initially evolved for F but is now used for G;
F. experience has no selected function and is a by-product/spandrel;
G. classic phenomenality is unreal, while quasi-phenomenal/introspective machinery performs F.
```

MF9-J asks:

> **What exactly could it mean for experience to have a function, what evidence would distinguish function of phenomenality from function of its mechanisms, and what does comparative/artificial evidence say about evolutionary origin and causal role?**


```text

  AttributedPurpose
}
```

### J9-001
**`The function of consciousness is F` is invalid unless `function` is typed.**

### J9-002
Historical selection and current causal contribution can coincide, diverge or be unrelated.

# 2. Selected effect is etiological

Introduce:

```text
SelectedEffectStanding(
  Trait,
  Effect |
  HeritableVariation,
  DifferentialFitness,
  SelectionHistory,
  CounterfactualAlternatives,
  Σ
)
```

The selected-effect claim is:

```text
Trait T exists / was shaped in part
because ancestors bearing T produced effect E
which contributed to differential reproduction/survival.
```

### J9-003
**Current usefulness ≠ evidence that the trait originally evolved for that use.**

Feathers can currently serve insulation/display/flight while their historical selected effects changed across lineages.

### J9-004
The same discipline applies to consciousness.

### J9-006

# 5. Evolution does not by itself refute epiphenomenalism

A common argument says:

```text
consciousness evolved
therefore consciousness must affect behavior
therefore epiphenomenalism is impossible
```

This is too fast.

If:

```text
adaptive neural/functional organization M
→ selected because M improves fitness

and

ExperienceStanding E
→ necessarily accompanies/supervenes on M
```

then E can persist across evolutionary history even if E has no additional causal role beyond M.

### J9-009
**Evolutionary persistence of ExperienceStanding does not by itself prove ExperienceStanding was a selected effect.**

### J9-010
Epiphenomenalism would block **selection for phenomenality itself**, but not selection of a substrate that reliably generates phenomenality as a correlated by-product.

# 7. Function of the basis versus function of experience

Reuse the 2022 conceptual distinction:

```text

  Which function does experience itself contribute?
```

### J9-012
A theory may answer the basis question while remaining silent about contribution.

Examples:

```text
GNW:

but
workspace broadcast's adaptive function
≠ proven function of phenomenality itself
```

### J9-013
**FunctionalBasis ≠ FunctionalContribution.**

# 8. Phenomenality-specific function requires a contrast class

Introduce:

```text
PhenomenalitySpecificFunctionClaim(
  E,
  F |
  NonPhenomenalMatchedContrast,
  MechanismControl,
  Theory,
  Σ
)
```

To show phenomenality itself adds function F, ideally compare:

```text
System A:
mechanism M + Experience E

System B:
mechanism M - Experience E

and test:
A performs F differently from B.
```

### J9-014
In many theories this contrast is impossible in principle because E is identical to, constituted by or necessarily realized by M.

### J9-015
If no matched nonphenomenal counterpart is possible, asking for an **independent extra function of experience** may be ill-posed under that theory.

# 9. Identity/realization theories change the causal question

Suppose:

```text
Experience E
= organized physical/functional process M
```

Then:

```text
M causes action A
```

and:

```text
E causes action A
```

may be two descriptions of the same causal process rather than two competing causes.

### J9-016
**No extra physical force is required for a higher-level realized state to be causally explanatory.**

A heart's pumping causes circulation without `pumping` being an extra force beyond molecular motion.

### J9-017
But realization/supervenience alone does not prove every phenomenal property is causally relevant to every downstream effect.

Property-level causal relevance still requires a suitable contrast/intervention/explanatory relation.

# 13. Flexibility is the most common candidate—and too broad in raw form

A widespread proposal is:

```text
consciousness
→ flexible, context-sensitive, nonroutine behavior
```

This has intuitive force because conscious processing is often associated with:

```text
novel situations

error correction
longer-range integration
replanning
```

### J9-023
But **behavioral flexibility alone is not consciousness-specific.**

# 14. Unconscious flexibility is experimentally real

Van Gaal et al. showed masked stimuli can flexibly influence behavior and prefrontal activity depending on a trial-by-trial changing task context.

Other masked-priming studies show unconscious processing can be modulated by active task sets.

### J9-024
Therefore:

```text
FlexibleBehavior
≠ ConsciousnessStanding
```

### J9-025
A viable flexibility theory must specify what **range, horizon, novelty, integration width or policy reconfiguration** requires consciousness rather than generic contextual control.

### J9-026

```text
Integration
```

### J9-027


# 16. Conscious processing may extend integration range rather than create integration from zero

The review literature suggests unconscious integration often has narrower temporal/semantic/sensory windows and may depend more heavily on previously learned structures.

### J9-028
This motivates:

```text
ConsciousnessContribution
= expansion / coordination / reconfiguration
of already-existing unconscious capacities
```

rather than:

```text
Consciousness creates every integrative operation.
```

### J9-029
This is a **quantitative/organizational function hypothesis**, not a binary-function hypothesis.

# 17. Action selection is a major basal-function candidate

Klein & Barron's 2025 Phenomenal Interface Theory proposes that mobile goal-directed animals must combine:

```text
sensory state
internal state
stored value information
multiple action options
```

into a common framework for multi-objective action selection.

They propose this common framework as a `phenomenal interface`.

### J9-030
This is a serious function proposal because it targets a concrete computational problem:

```text
heterogeneous value-bearing information
→ commensurable option selection
```

### J9-031
But action selection itself is far broader than experience.

# 19. Common currency is promising but not sufficient

### J9-035

### J9-036
This proposal targets phenomenality more directly than generic integration/flexibility.

### J9-037

# 22. Reward optimization is not yet subjective value

### J9-047

### J9-048

### J9-049

This is exactly why selected-effect versus current causal-role separation matters.

# 28. Homeostatic/affective function remains plausible but not exclusive

Damasio/Solms-like routes propose experience as a way of representing organismic need/value and guiding adaptive regulation.

Recent evolutionary-function work likewise emphasizes interoceptive feelings as grounding protective responses.

### J9-050
The biological plausibility is high for affective states because valence tightly couples to survival-relevant choice.

### J9-051
But:

```text
homeostatic control
reward prediction
nociception

```

can all occur without demonstrated experience.

Therefore the functional question remains **what felt valence adds**.

# 29. Pain is the sharpest causal-function test

Pain appears paradigmatically causal because:

```text
it hurts
→ we avoid it
```

But neural-causation analysis allows multiple interpretations:

```text
A. experienced pain itself causally guides choice;
B. the same neural state both realizes pain and causes choice;
C. neural decision circuitry causes choice while pain is epiphenomenal;
D. pain is a higher-level ground/information-bearing state but not an independent mechanistic cause.
```

### J9-052
Behavior alone cannot decide among these ontologies.

---

### J9-053
Relief preference supports experienced-valence attribution but does not isolate a causal contribution unique to phenomenality over its neural realization.

This is a general identification problem.

# 31. Phenomenality-specific causal experiments may be impossible under some theories

If phenomenal character supervenes necessarily on exact physical organization, then we cannot experimentally hold every physical fact fixed while changing only phenomenality.

### J9-054
The zombie-control experiment:

```text
same full physical mechanism
same behavior
no experience
```

is unavailable if physicalism is true.

### J9-055
Therefore absence of a phenomenality-only intervention does not automatically imply epiphenomenalism; it may reflect constitutive identity.

If two systems are functionally equivalent but a theory assigns experience to only one because of substrate/intrinsic-causal differences, then:

```text

```

### J9-056
IIT-like and biological-naturalist positions therefore naturally allow:

```text
FunctionalRoleDuplicate
```

### J9-057
For such theories, phenomenality cannot be inferred from function alone.

# 35. Unconscious human cognition provides a stronger within-organism contrast

# 36. Conscious initiation versus unconscious execution may matter

### J9-065

# 41. Deep homology and convergent realization are both plausible

Vertebrates, arthropods and cephalopods share very ancient cellular/signalling ancestry but possess strikingly different nervous-system architectures.

Complex brains/cognition clearly evolved along partly independent trajectories, especially vertebrate versus cephalopod lineages.

### J9-070
If phenomenality depends on a very abstract functional organization, multiple convergent origins are plausible.

### J9-071
If it depends on a deeply homologous cellular/biophysical property, a much older common origin with many transformations/losses becomes plausible.

### J9-072
Current comparative evidence does not choose between these at the phenomenality level.

---

# 42. Insect–vertebrate analogies are a convergence stress test

Barron & Klein argued insect central-complex-like organization and vertebrate midbrain systems may solve analogous egocentric action-control problems despite different anatomy.

### J9-073
This supports:

```text

```

### J9-074

# 43. Cephalopods weaken single social/intelligence narratives

### J9-076
Cephalopods are a major falsifier for overly vertebrate-specific evolutionary stories.

```text
one historical origin route
```

but not as an unqualified universal function without phylogenetic support.

# 48. Selected effects require heritable fitness consequences

To establish that phenomenality was selected for, one would need evidence linking:

```text
variation in the consciousness-relevant trait
→ variation in behavior/physiology
→ differential fitness
→ heritable substrate
→ historical selection
```

### J9-083
This chain is rarely available for phenomenal properties themselves.

### J9-084
Most evolutionary consciousness proposals currently identify plausible functions of candidate mechanisms rather than directly demonstrating historical selection on phenomenality.

1. **Fitch, Allen & Roskies (2025), `The evolutionary functions of consciousness`, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B 380:20240299, DOI `10.1098/rstb.2024.0299`.** Current theme-issue overview; emphasizes comparative/evolutionary function and selected-effect versus current causal-role distinctions.
2. **Mason et al. (2022), `Functions of consciousness: conceptual clarification`, Neuroscience of Consciousness 2022:niac006, DOI `10.1093/nc/niac006`.** Separates functional basis, functional contribution, selected effect and causal role; explicitly preserves epiphenomenal functional-basis possibility.

5. **Mudrik et al. (2011), `Integration without awareness: expanding the limits of unconscious processing`, Psychological Science 22:764–770, DOI `10.1177/0956797611408736`.** Object/background semantic-context integration without awareness.

(2014), `Information integration without awareness`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 18:488–496, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2014.04.009`.** Unconscious integration exists but tends to be narrower/less flexible, motivating scope rather than zero/one hypotheses.
7. **Klein & Barron (2025), `Phenomenal interface theory: a model for basal consciousness`, Philosophical Transactions B 380:20240301, DOI `10.1098/rstb.2024.0301`.** Phenomenal-interface/action-selection proposal using common framework and multi-objective values, with insects as basal model.
8. **Barron & Klein (2016), `What insects can tell us about the origins of consciousness`, PNAS 113:4900–4908, DOI `10.1073/pnas.1520084113`.** Analogous vertebrate/insect egocentric action-control architectures and Cambrian-origin proposal.
9. **Cleeremans & Tallon-Baudry (2022), `Consciousness matters: phenomenal experience has functional value`, Neuroscience of Consciousness 2022:niac007, DOI `10.1093/nc/niac007`.** Phenomenal/intrinsic subjective value as function proposal.

The question:


```text
Which experience type?
Which bearer?

Which historical selected effect?
Which underlying mechanism?

Which ecological/developmental context?
```

The strongest established results are:

```text

2. flexibility/integration/learning/action selection/social coordination are too broad to be consciousness-specific by themselves;

```

The key ontology result is:

> **Experience-associated function, phenomenality-specific causal contribution and historical selection for phenomenality are three different questions. Current science has strong answers to the first, partial/theory-dependent answers to the second, and weak historical evidence for the third.**
