# 1. Why MF3-I cannot simply summarize MF3-A→G

MF3-I is an adversarial reconstruction round.

The task is not to preserve earlier wording. It is to find assumptions that survived because earlier rounds shared the same hidden bias.

The main attack surfaces are:

1. **External/public representation:** can a book, sign, map, database row or file represent while nobody is currently using it?
2. **Vehicle–target distinction:** must target and vehicle be physically distinct entities?
3. **Proxy recruitment:** is actual current consumer use constitutive, or can representational standing persist offline?
4. **Grounding:** can content be fixed through design, convention, institutional practice, production history or public language without direct sensorimotor coupling?
5. **Evaluation:** is correctness/error too narrow for directives, probabilities, fiction, models and public symbols?
6. **Format:** are symbol, model, neural state and public artifact variants of one relation or separate kinds?
7. **Evidence vs ontology:** did MF3-F/G accidentally turn experimental evidence requirements into constitutive representation conditions?
8. **Gauge:** can representation survive coordinate/basis/material transformation?
9. **Self/recursive representation:** can representations target themselves, their own artifacts or other representations?
10. **Inactive persistence:** what survives copying, serialization, storage, medium migration, quotation and versioning?
11. **Misrepresentation:** can false, fictional, forged or systematically biased artifacts still represent?
12. **Nonrepresentation:** does the theory still exclude mere correlations, signals, causal intermediaries and analyst-decodable states?

The final theory must survive all twelve.

# 2. H-bridge falsifier: the unread book

Suppose a book is placed in an archive for ten years and nobody reads it.

If representation requires **current actual consumer recruitment**, then every sentence stops representing while the book is unread and starts representing again when someone opens it.

That is implausible for public/external representation.

Zhang & Norman's distributed-representation framework explicitly treats external structures as representations participating with internal representations in distributed cognitive tasks. Clark & Chalmers' notebook case similarly makes stored external information functionally available across time rather than becoming meaningful only at the instant of inner rehearsal.

### Reconstruction

Separate:

## Representational standing

A vehicle/token/type has an established grounded role under which its distinctions are available/authorized to stand for target distinctions even when not currently consumed.

Grounding may be maintained by:

- public convention;
- producer/design intention;
- institutional schema;
- production history;
- established system role;
- stable code/protocol;
- previous/current systemic learning history.

## Active representation episode

A standing representation is currently read, decoded, manipulated, simulated, queried or otherwise recruited by a consumer/system.

### Result

**RI-01 — Actual current consumer recruitment is not constitutive of all representation. Representation has a standing/activation distinction.**

This is the largest revision to MF3-A.

---

# 3. Standing representation does not collapse into arbitrary decodability

The unread-book result could tempt an overcorrection:

> if no current use is needed, anything that an observer can decode can represent anything.

Reject.

A random stone pattern that can be mapped by an analyst to yesterday's stock prices does not therefore have public representational standing.

Standing requires a grounding relation that predates or constrains the arbitrary decoder:

- design;
- convention;
- historical use;
- producer intention;
- institutional rule;
- endogenous learned consumer role;
- causal/indexical production relation plus an established interpretive practice;
- another non-arbitrary grounding route.

### Result

**RI-02 — Inactive standing removes the need for current use, not the need for grounded content-determining relations. External decodability remains insufficient.**

---

# 4. Consumer requirement reconstructed

Earlier MF3 often spoke of `consumer/use` as if one actual downstream consumer were always required.

The public cases force a distinction:

## Active systemic representation

Actual consumer recruitment is central evidence and part of the episode.

## Standing public/designed representation

A token can retain representational status through a socially/technically established **consumer disposition/practice**, even if no one currently consumes it.

## One-off intentional representation

A creator can draw a private map for future use and die before anybody reads it. The production intention/design relation can establish standing despite zero later uptake.

## Later-adopted accidental mark

A naturally/accidentally produced mark need not initially represent, but a community/system may subsequently recruit it as a conventional sign, thereby creating representational standing.

### Result

**RI-03 — `Consumer` is a role in grounding/use, not a requirement that a currently active interpreter exist at every instant.**

---

# 5. Producer intention is neither universally necessary nor sufficient

## Not necessary

Public words, road signs, machine schemas and copied documents can retain content after the original producer is gone or unknown.

A mature convention stabilizes token use beyond a single producer's occurrent intention.

Millikan's work on linguistic conventions is useful here: linguistic forms can persist as reproduced patterns linked to cooperative producer/consumer responses rather than depending on one explicit rule or one current intention.

## Not sufficient

A producer can intend a random noise trace to represent a detailed world state without establishing any stable mapping/content condition that others or the system can use.

### Result

**RI-04 — Producer intention is one grounding route, not the universal source of representation.**

---

# 6. Public convention is powerful but not universal

Public-language and institutional artifacts show that convention can stabilize content across users and generations.

But a private diagram, a learned neural state or a world-model latent can represent without public convention.

### Result

**RI-05 — Convention/public practice is a strong grounding route for external representation, not a universal constitutive condition.**

---

# 7. Grounding becomes typed and plural

MF3-B/G already moved toward plural grounding. MF3-I freezes that move.

A representation may be grounded by one or more of:

## G1 — Systemic/endogenous grounding

The system itself learns/recruits the vehicle as a proxy in its computation/action.

## G2 — Designed/intentional grounding

A designer/producer establishes a content-bearing role through a code, diagram, schema, field or artifact.

## G3 — Conventional/public grounding

A stable social/linguistic/institutional practice fixes token/type/use relations.

## G4 — Causal/indexical production grounding

The vehicle is produced by the target/relevant process in a way that an established system/practice recruits representationally, as in measurement traces or photographs.

## G5 — Structural/keyed grounding

A model/map/diagram uses an explicit or stabilized key to map selected vehicle structure to target structure.

## G6 — Hybrid grounding

Most mature representations combine several routes.

### Result

**RI-06 — Representation Foundations v1 rejects one universal grounding mechanism; grounding is plural but constrained and provenance-typed.**

---

# 8. Pure causal covariance still fails

Smoke covaries causally with fire.

Tree rings covary with growth conditions.

A photodiode voltage covaries with light intensity.

These can be signals/evidence without already being representations.

They become representations when a biological/artificial/public practice recruits their distinctions under a content-determining standing/use relation.

### Result

**RI-07 — Natural information/causal covariance is neither sufficient nor universally necessary for representation.**

Conventional symbols can represent absent/nonexistent targets without current covariance.

---

# 9. Vehicle and target need not be distinct physical entities

Earlier formulations sometimes required a `distinct target/domain`.

Counterexamples:

- metadata represents properties of the same file/object containing the metadata;
- a checksum field represents the bytes of the containing record except/including defined regions;
- `you are here` marker participates in a map representing a location that includes the map itself;
- a self-description can represent properties of the describing system;
- quotation can represent another token of the same type;
- an interpretability model represents another representation.

### Reconstruction

What must be distinct are **roles**, not necessarily entities.

`Vehicle-role` and `Target-role` must be analytically distinguishable in the representational relation, but the underlying physical/computational object sets can overlap.

### Result

**RI-08 — Representation requires role distinction, not physical vehicle–target disjointness. Self- and recursive representation are admissible.**

---

# 10. Target need not be an actual existing object

MF3-B survives the attack.

Representations can concern:

- fictional entities;
- future events;
- counterfactual worlds;
- impossible states;
- empty database references;
- hypothetical variables;
- mathematical structures.

Therefore:

`Target domain/content condition ≠ actual referent`.

### Result

**RI-09 — Actual referent existence is not constitutive of representation.**

---

# 11. Content is not one object label

MF3-E/F/G strongly support keeping content richer than reference.

A representation may contribute:

- individual reference;
- property;
- relation;
- probability/distribution;
- directive;
- model state;
- structural constraint;
- role/binding;
- function/operator;
- predicted possibility.

### Result

**RI-10 — Representation content is typed structure/condition, not universally an object referent.**

---

# 12. Mode survives falsification

The same domain condition can occur under different representational modes:

- descriptive;
- directive;
- interrogative/query-like;
- probabilistic;
- fictional/simulative;
- normative/institutional;
- approximate/model-relative.

Thus binary truth cannot be the sole evaluation relation.

### Result

**RI-11 — Representational mode/direction-of-fit is first-class and must remain distinct from content condition.**

---

# 13. Evaluability survives, but is weakened from `truth condition`

A stop sign is not primarily true/false.

A probability forecast is not simply true/false per token.

A fictional map may be accurate relative to a fictional domain.

A low-resolution scientific model can be adequate despite idealization.

The surviving commonality is not binary truth but **typed evaluability**:

- reference success/misreference;
- truth/falsity;
- fidelity/distortion;
- satisfaction/non-satisfaction;
- calibration/scoring;
- structural adequacy;
- compliance/achievement;
- applicability within scope.

### Result

**RI-12 — Representation requires a non-arbitrary typed evaluation/fit profile, not universal binary truth conditions.**

This keeps misrepresentation meaningful without forcing proposition-like semantics onto all representations.

---

# 14. Is evaluability genuinely constitutive?

Attack case: a proper name token `Paris` seems representational even when used without making a claim.

Response: the token still has reference conditions and can misrefer/be unresolved.

Attack case: an artistic depiction.

Response: if it genuinely depicts/represents a subject, there are at least applicability/reference/fidelity relations under the relevant representational mode, even if artistic value is not reducible to accuracy.

Attack case: a variable placeholder.

Response: its representational contribution is evaluated under binding/assignment rules rather than standalone truth.

### Result

**RI-13 — Typed evaluability survives as a minimal anti-arbitrariness/error condition when understood broadly enough to include reference, binding and satisfaction.**

---

# 15. Misrepresentation remains a crucial discriminator

A theory that defines content as whatever currently causes/correlates with a vehicle cannot distinguish error from content drift.

Cases surviving across domains:

- miscalibrated thermometer;
- wrong map;
- false memory;
- hallucinated object;
- wrong model transition;
- incorrect database field;
- neural location estimate error;
- artificial model's false world state.

### Result

**RI-14 — Stable enough content to permit typed error/misrepresentation remains a central criterion for strong representation attribution.**

---

# 16. But noise is not automatically misrepresentation

A corrupted bitstream may no longer instantiate a valid symbol/token.

A neural spike may be noise without forming a representational state.

A malformed JSON payload may be syntactically invalid rather than semantically false.

### Result

**RI-15 — Invalid vehicle/noise, representation failure, misrepresentation and consumer misread remain distinct failure types.**

---

# 17. Public artifact persistence

A representation can remain standing across time if its grounding relations remain recoverable/stable enough.

Examples:

- an archived book;
- a road map in storage;
- a database backup;
- a schema definition;
- a photograph;
- a source file.

No active signal flow is required during storage.

### Result

**RI-16 — Representation can persist dispositionally across inactive storage; signal/perception activation is a separate episode.**

This cleanly separates MF1 Signal from MF3 Representation.

---

# 18. Copying falsifies token identity as content identity

Copying a text file produces a new physical token but can preserve:

- symbol types;
- content;
- structure;
- public interpretation.

Therefore:

`same representational content ≠ same physical token`.

### Result

**RI-17 — Token continuity, type continuity, content continuity and provenance continuity are distinct.**

---

# 19. Serialization/encoding migration falsifies byte identity

The same text can migrate:

- UTF-8 → UTF-16;
- JSON → CBOR under a schema-preserving transformation;
- printed page → scanned text;
- compressed → decompressed file;
- image format A → format B.

If the relevant code/key and content relations are preserved, representation can persist despite different bytes/vehicles.

### Result

**RI-18 — Representation identity is not byte/material identity; format/code transforms can preserve content under typed equivalence.**

This inherits MF3-C.

---

# 20. Lossy migration may preserve some content and destroy other content

OCR can preserve lexical content while destroying typography.

Image compression can preserve recognizable depiction while destroying fine metric data.

Map simplification can preserve topology while removing street-level detail.

### Result

**RI-19 — Representation preservation under transformation is content/scope/equivalence-relative, not all-or-nothing.**

---

# 21. Provenance is not identical to semantic content

Two byte-identical documents can differ in provenance:

- authentic original;
- authorized copy;
- forged copy;
- synthetic duplicate.

They may express the same propositional content but differ in:

- authorship;
- evidential status;
- institutional authority;
- indexical/history-dependent reference.

### Result

**RI-20 — Semantic/content identity and provenance/authenticity identity are separate dimensions, though provenance can itself be part of content for some representation types.**

---

# 22. Forgery does not imply nonrepresentation

A forged passport still contains symbols that represent a name, nationality and date of birth; what fails is its provenance/authority and possibly factual accuracy.

A fabricated photograph can depict a nonexistent event.

### Result

**RI-21 — Forged/fabricated artifacts can remain representations while failing authenticity, authority or truth conditions.**

---

# 23. Quotation is representation-of-representation

Quotation can use one token/expression to mention/represent another token/type rather than its ordinary referent.

This supports recursive representation without requiring new metaphysical primitives.

### Result

**RI-22 — Representations can target other representations, their forms, contents or provenance. Recursive representational layering is ordinary.**

---

# 24. Database record as hard falsifier

Consider:

```text
user_id = 42
country = "SG"
balance = 100
```

Its semantics depend on:

- schema;
- namespace;
- field definitions;
- unit conventions;
- entity identity rules;
- null/error semantics;
- version.

The bytes alone do not fix the meaning.

But once the schema/protocol establishes these relations, the record can represent while no process is reading it.

### Result

**RI-23 — Public/computational representation demonstrates that schema/protocol context can ground content dispositionally without current consumption.**

---

# 25. API payload as active representation episode

When the same record is transmitted through an API and parsed by a consumer, standing representation becomes active representation.

The active episode adds:

- signal transmission;
- parsing;
- current consumer use;
- potential action effects.

### Result

**RI-24 — Standing representation and active signal-mediated use are composable but non-identical layers.**

---

# 26. Schema can itself represent

A schema represents constraints/types/roles that payload instances are expected to satisfy.

It may also function directive/normatively by specifying what messages are valid.

### Result

**RI-25 — Representation role multiplicity is real: the same artifact can descriptively represent structure and normatively constrain future tokens.**

---

# 27. Source code is not one representational kind

Source code can simultaneously:

- be a formal symbolic artifact;
- specify/instruct computation;
- represent an algorithm or data structure;
- serve as documentation to humans;
- be executable after interpretation/compilation.

### Result

**RI-26 — Representation, instruction and execution roles can coexist; code should not be forced into one mode.**

---

# 28. External memory does not force extended-mind metaphysics

Zhang–Norman show cognition can depend on internal + external representational structures, and Clark–Chalmers argue under certain coupling conditions that external resources can become parts of cognitive processes.

MF3 does not need to settle whether the mind literally extends.

It only needs the weaker surviving point:

> external artifacts can possess stable representational roles and participate causally in cognition.

### Result

**RI-27 — External representation is compatible with, but does not require, a commitment to the Extended Mind thesis.**

---

# 29. Epistemic action confirms external representation's operational role

Kirsh & Maglio's Tetris work shows agents can manipulate external structures to reduce internal computational burden.

This supports an important model-level role of external representations:

> representational vehicles can be physically transformed so that the world performs part of a cognitive computation.

### Result

**RI-28 — External representation can support surrogate/epistemic operations without being internalized into a neural state first.**

---

# 30. Medium and representation reconnect, but do not collapse

MF0 defined medium as a relational role in structured mediation.

MF3 now defines representation as a grounded standing-in/proxy relation.

A representation token necessarily needs some vehicle/medium through which distinctions are instantiated or maintained.

But:

- a medium can mediate without representing;
- a representation can migrate across media;
- the same physical substrate can have medium and representational roles simultaneously;
- medium concerns mediation topology/transformability;
- representation concerns target/content/proxy/evaluation.

### Result

**RI-29 — Representation is a possible role within structured mediation, but Medium ≠ Representation.**

---

# 31. Signal and representation reconnect, but do not collapse

A stored representation need not currently be a signal.

When read/transmitted, vehicle variation becomes an active signal recruited by a consumer.

Thus:

`standing representation → activation/transduction → signal episode → perceptual/computational uptake`.

But a signal can exist without representation.

### Result

**RI-30 — Signal is active discriminative variation; representation can have persistent standing across inactive periods. Signal ≠ Representation.**

---

# 32. Perception and representation remain non-identical

MF2 perception can organize discriminability under uncertainty without requiring explicit internal stand-ins of every discriminated property.

Some perceptual states may become representations when recruited as proxies for distal/body/world possibilities.

### Result

**RI-31 — Perception does not universally require representation, while perceptual systems can instantiate representations.**

This preserves implementation neutrality.

---

# 33. Model remains a strengthened representation profile

MF3-D survives.

A model is not merely any representation; it organizes reusable surrogate relations/operations over a target/question space.

### Result

**RI-32 — Model is a representation capability profile involving structured surrogate operations, scope and query families; not every representation is a model.**

---

# 34. Symbol remains a strengthened vehicle/format profile

MF3-E survives.

Symbolhood adds re-identifiable types/roles and type-governed operations.

Semantic symbolhood adds grounding.

Compositional symbolic organization adds reusable constituents/binding/composition.

### Result

**RI-33 — Symbol ≠ Representation; symbolic representation is a typed representational format/capability.**

# 35. Neural/artificial evidence rounds do not define ontology

MF3-F/G produced rich evidence profiles.

Attack:

If causal intervention were constitutive, archived public artifacts would fail to represent.

If probe decodability were constitutive, any externally decodable correlation would represent.

If cross-task reuse were constitutive, narrow but genuine representations would be excluded.

### Reconstruction

Separate two layers:

## Representation ontology

What relation makes a vehicle stand for a target/content.

## Representation attribution epistemology

What evidence licenses us to claim that relation in a hard-to-interpret system.

### Result

**RI-34 — Probe, tuning, causal intervention, reuse and decoding are evidence dimensions, not universal constitutive conditions.**

# 36. Attribution evidence still matters enormously

For neural/AI systems, grounding/use/content are not publicly specified like JSON schemas.

Therefore evidence profiles remain indispensable:

- association;
- information;
- endogenous use;
- causal recruitment;
- grounding/target determination;
- error/evaluability;
- robustness/scope;
- decoupling;
- gauge/identifiability;
- provenance;
- mechanistic integration.

### Result

**RI-35 — Representation Foundations v1 freezes an ontology/evidence separation while retaining domain-specific evidence profiles as attribution discipline.**

# 37. Consumer-relativity vs observer-arbitrariness

MF3 repeatedly called content consumer-relative.

Attack:

Could an arbitrary analyst declare any content relative to themselves?

No.

### Reconstruction

Content can be relative to:

- actual system consumer;
- public practice;
- design/schema;
- production convention;
- model key;
- task context.

But arbitrary external decoding unsupported by those relations remains analyst-ascribed only.

### Result

**RI-36 — Consumer/practice relativity is constrained systemic relativity, not arbitrary observer relativism.**

---

# 38. Multi-level content survives

A token may have different legitimate content at different grounded levels.

Example: API field `country="SG"` can be:

- a string token at protocol level;
- a country-code value at schema level;
- a country identity at application level;
- a jurisdictional constraint at policy level.

These are not arbitrary if distinct consumers/practices actually recruit them.

### Result

**RI-37 — Layered content is admissible when each layer has independent grounding/use relations.**

---

# 39. Representation can be dispositional without becoming purely hypothetical

Standing public representation is dispositional in the sense of potential authorized use.

But the vehicle must actually instantiate or belong to an established representational token/type relation.

A nonexistent hypothetical book does not currently constitute a representation token simply because one could write it.

### Result

**RI-38 — Standing representation is an actual grounded role of an existing vehicle/type specification, not mere logical possibility of future interpretation.**

---

# 40. Type-level representation

A protocol can reserve a codepoint with specified meaning even before that codepoint occurs in any message.

Thus symbol/representation **types** can acquire standing through a code/schema specification before token instantiation.

Token-level representation then instantiates that type under context.

### Result

**RI-39 — Representation can exist at type/specification level and token/episode level; token existence is not required for type-level assigned content.**

# 41. Accidental exact copies

Suppose random noise produces byte-for-byte an existing valid JPEG.

Does it represent the pictured person?

MF3-I distinguishes:

- the byte pattern instantiates a publicly interpretable image type/format;
- whether this particular token inherits the ordinary image's provenance/indexical relation is separate;
- it may depict under public image conventions while lacking the original photograph's causal provenance.

### Result

**RI-40 — Token content can partly inherit from type/convention while provenance/indexical content remains history-dependent.**

This prevents all-or-nothing answers to accidental-copy cases.

# 53. Minimal representation must not reduce to structural correspondence

# 55. Gauge challenge survives and is scoped

# 59. Institutional representations

Money, credentials, passports, legal records and status fields often acquire force through institutional practices rather than physical similarity.

Searle's social-ontology work is a useful strong case that some status functions depend on collectively sustained linguistic/institutional structures.

MF3 does not adopt all of Searle's social ontology, but the falsifier is decisive:

> representational/normative force can be institutionally grounded rather than naturally intrinsic to the vehicle.

### Result

**RI-58 — Institutional/public grounding is a real representational route and can change normative consequences without changing physical vehicle properties.**

# 66. Representation and prediction

A prediction is a representational act/output about an unknown/future condition.

A representation need not predict.

### Result

**RI-65 — Prediction is one use/mode of representation, not its definition.**

---

# 67. Representation and generation

A generative model can emit structured outputs that depict/describe possibilities, but generative capacity alone does not determine whether internal states represent those possibilities.

### Result

**RI-66 — Generation is an operation/output capability, not sufficient internal representational evidence.**

---

# 68. Representation and realism

Realistic-looking outputs can be false; sparse schematic models can be highly adequate.

### Result

**RI-67 — Realism/plausibility ≠ fidelity ≠ representation.**

---

# 69. Representation and abstraction

Representations can intentionally collapse distinctions outside scope.

### Result

**RI-68 — Abstraction is selective representational omission/equivalence, not automatically error.**

---

# 70. Representation and compression

Lossy compression can preserve task-relevant representational distinctions while discarding others.

### Result

**RI-69 — Representation can be selectively lossy; completeness is never a universal criterion.**

---

# 71. Representation and future optionality

Two representations with equal current task sufficiency can differ dramatically in what future queries they support.

### Result

**RI-70 — Task sufficiency and future optionality remain distinct representational/resource properties.**

---

# 72. Representation and interpretation

An external interpreter can discover a standing public representation.

An arbitrary interpreter can also impose a novel mapping that was never part of the token's grounding.

### Result

**RI-71 — Interpretation can reveal, activate, extend or newly create representational relations; it does not retroactively prove every decoder was always intrinsic content.**

---

# 73. Reconstructing the minimal core

After all attacks, the strongest minimal relation is no longer:

`Vehicle + Target + Actual Consumer Use + Correctness`.

It becomes:

```text
Representational Standing
=
Vehicle/Type
+ Grounded Standing-In Relation
+ Target Domain / Content Profile
+ Mode / Context / Scope
+ Typed Evaluability
```

An **active representation episode** adds current recruitment/use.

---

# 74. Frozen definition — Representation Foundations v1

> **A representation is a grounded standing-in relation in which distinctions or structure of a vehicle/type are established—through systemic use, design, convention, production history, keyed structure or other non-arbitrary grounding—as standing for, specifying or proxying distinctions, conditions or possibilities in a target domain under a context/mode/scope that supports typed evaluation such as reference, fit, fidelity, satisfaction, calibration or error.**

Important clarifications:

- vehicle-role and target-role must be distinguishable, but the underlying entities may overlap;
- actual referent need not exist;
- current consumer use is not required for standing representation;
- active recruitment is a separate representation episode;
- grounding can be systemic, designed, conventional, causal/indexical, structural or hybrid;
- content can be descriptive, directive, probabilistic, structural, fictional, model-relative or other typed forms;
- evaluation need not be binary truth;
- representation can be internal or external, transient or persistent, symbolic or nonsymbolic, local or distributed.

---

# 75. Frozen compact form


a) Standing representation:

`RepStanding(V, Θ, D, Φ, M, G, K, Σ, H)`

where:

- `V` = vehicle/token/material realization;
- `Θ` = optional vehicle/type/code identity;
- `D` = target domain;
- `Φ` = content condition/structure/contribution;
- `M` = representational mode/direction-of-fit;
- `G` = grounding/key/provenance relation;
- `K` = typed evaluation/fit profile;
- `Σ` = context/scope/reference frame/granularity;
- `H` = relevant history/practice.

b) Active representation episode:

`RepActive = RepStanding + U_t + C_t`

where:

- `U_t` = current consumer/system recruitment/operation;
- `C_t` = current causal/signal/computational context.

# 76. Frozen representation roles

Representation Foundations v1 recognizes at least these profiles without claiming they are exhaustive natural kinds:

## R0 — Indicative/descriptive representation

Stands for a state/condition/domain.

## R1 — Directive representation

Specifies desired/required action/state.

## R2 — Structural/model representation

Preserves keyed relations and supports surrogate operations/inference.

## R3 — Probabilistic representation

Represents uncertainty/distribution under a scoring/calibration semantics.

## R4 — Symbolic representation

Uses re-identifiable types/roles and type-governed operations.

## R5 — Compositional symbolic representation

Adds reusable constituent/role/binding/composition relations.

## R6 — Memory/predictive/simulative representation

Supports absent/past/future/hypothetical targets.

## R7 — Public/external representation

Standing is stabilized by design/convention/institution/history and persists without current active use.

These profiles can overlap.

# 77. Frozen grounding profile

**GF1 Systemic/endogenous** — actual learned/functional consumer role inside a system.

**GF2 Designed/intentional** — producer/designer establishes proxy/code relation.

**GF3 Conventional/public/institutional** — social/practice-level type/use relations stabilize content.

**GF4 Causal/indexical production** — target/process produces vehicle under an established representational practice.

**GF5 Structural/keyed** — selected structure/operations are explicitly keyed to target structure.

**GF6 Hybrid** — several routes jointly constrain content.

No single GF route is universally necessary.

---

# 78. Frozen activation distinction

**Standing** answers:

> Does this vehicle/type currently have a grounded representational role?

**Active** answers:

> Is that standing representation currently being read/recruited/operated on?

### Core rule

`Inactive ≠ Nonrepresentational`.

---

# 79. Frozen identity distinctions

Do not collapse:

`Vehicle Token ≠ Vehicle Type ≠ Code ≠ Format ≠ Geometry ≠ Content ≠ Referent ≠ Provenance ≠ Authority ≠ Consumer Use.`

And:

`Physical Identity ≠ Information Identity ≠ Functional Identity ≠ Content Identity ≠ Provenance Identity.`

---

# 80. Frozen signal/perception/representation chain

`Reality ≠ Signal ≠ Perception ≠ Representation ≠ Meaning ≠ Knowledge.`

Possible relation:

```text
Reality/world/body
   ↓ structured causal variation
Signal episode
   ↓ perceptual/computational organization
Perceptual/system state
   ↓ if grounded as proxy/standing-in
Representation
   ↓ if embedded in social/linguistic/pragmatic practice
Meaning structures
   ↓ if true/justified/etc.
Knowledge (possible)
```

None of these arrows is universally obligatory.

---

# 81. Frozen model distinction

`Representation ≠ Model ≠ Simulation ≠ Prediction`.

A model is a structured representation supporting surrogate operations over a query/scope space.

Simulation is execution of a model.

Prediction is a target-directed output/claim.

---

# 82. Frozen symbol distinction

`Representation ≠ Symbol`.

Formal symbolhood = typed/re-identifiable token/role manipulability.

Semantic symbolhood = formal symbolhood + grounded content/reference.

Compositional symbolic organization = reusable constituents + roles/binding/composition.

---

# 83. Frozen geometry distinction

`Content ≠ Geometry ≠ Coordinates`.

Geometry can make distinctions operationally accessible but is not semantics by itself.

Representation equivalence must state the preserved relation and transformation class.

---

# 84. Frozen attribution discipline

For opaque biological/artificial systems, evidence should be reported separately from ontology.

Suggested dimensions:

- Association/selectivity;
- Information/recoverability;
- Endogenous use;
- Causal recruitment;
- Grounding/target determination;
- Evaluability/error;
- Robustness/scope;
- Decoupling/model use;
- Gauge/identifiability;
- Provenance;
- Mechanistic integration.

### Core rule

No one dimension universally establishes representation.

---

# 85. Frozen content discipline

For any strong representation claim, ask:

1. What is the vehicle/type?
2. What target domain is proposed?
3. What is the content condition/structure?
4. What mode/direction-of-fit applies?
5. What grounding route fixes the target/content?
6. What context/scope/frame/granularity applies?
7. What evaluation/error relation exists?
8. Is the representation standing or currently active?
9. Which consumer/practice, if any, recruits it?
10. What plausible alternative content attributions have been falsified?

---

# 86. Frozen public-representation discipline

For external artifacts additionally ask:

- token vs type;
- producer/design history;
- public convention/institution;
- schema/protocol/version;
- current vs historical standing;
- provenance/authenticity;
- copy/migration history;
- actual vs potential consumer;
- authority/status-function;
- whether interpretation merely activates an existing standing or creates a new one.

---

# 87. Frozen neural/artificial evidence wording

Prefer evidence-typed claims:

- `is tuned/associated with X`;
- `X is decodable from V`;
- `downstream consumer uses V for X-related computation`;
- `intervention on V causes X-specific effects`;
- `convergent grounding/use/error evidence supports representation of X`.

Avoid jumping directly from probe/activation examples to semantic labels.

# 92. Freeze decision

MF3-A→G contained two constitutive overstatements that failed falsification:

1. **Actual consumer recruitment** was too strong for inactive public/external representation.
2. **Distinct vehicle and target entities** was too strong for self-/recursive representations.

Both have been repaired:

- current recruitment → `standing vs active` distinction;
- entity distinction → `vehicle-role vs target-role` distinction.

A third methodological issue was repaired:

3. **Evidence conditions vs ontology** were conflated in neural/artificial rounds.

These are now explicitly separated.

After these revisions, no remaining falsifier forces abandonment of the grounded standing-in + typed evaluability core.

### Decision

# **Representation Foundations v1 — FROZEN**

# 93. Frozen one-paragraph synthesis

> **Representation is a relational standing-in role, not a natural object class. A vehicle or type represents when its distinctions/structure are non-arbitrarily grounded—through systemic use, design, convention, production history, structural key or hybrid routes—as standing for, specifying or proxying distinctions, conditions or possibilities in a target domain under a typed content/mode/context/scope and evaluation profile. Representation may remain standing while inactive; current consumer recruitment defines an active episode rather than representation's existence. Vehicle and target roles must be distinguishable but may occupy overlapping entities. Representation does not require internality, consciousness, actual referents, resemblance, compositionality, decoupling or complete fidelity, and it is distinct from signal, perception, symbol, model, computation, meaning and knowledge.**
