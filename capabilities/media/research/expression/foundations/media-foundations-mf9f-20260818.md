# 0. Purpose

MF9-A introduced `ExperientialStateStanding` but did not yet reconstruct it. MF9-B–E mostly focused on episode/content structure, access/report/metacognition, and constitutive theories.

MF9-F asks a different question:

> **What does it mean for a bearer to be in a global state that permits, contains, disconnects, suppresses, or obscures experience across time—and how can we infer such states when behavior, report, wakefulness, and cognition dissociate?**

Primary stress domains:

```text
wakefulness
sleep
REM / NREM
dreaming / no-dream report
sedation
general anesthesia
connected consciousness
disconnected consciousness
coma
unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (UWS / vegetative state)
minimally conscious state (MCS)
cognitive motor dissociation (CMD)
locked-in syndrome (LIS)
perturbational complexity
covert command-following
```

The key methodological target is the common collapse:

```text
responsive = conscious
unresponsive = unconscious
awake = conscious
asleep = unconscious
```

MF9-F rejects these identities.

# 1. Global experiential state is not a scalar by default

Introduce:

```text
GlobalExperientialStateStanding(G,B|H,Σ)
```

as a bearer-level profile over a horizon `H`, containing at least:

```text
CurrentExperienceOccurrence
ExperientialCapacity
Wakefulness
Arousal
EnvironmentalConnectedness
EndogenousExperience
SensoryAccess
GlobalAvailability
CommandComprehension
VolitionalControl
MotorResponsiveness
CommunicationCapacity
Reportability
MemoryEncoding/Recall
StateStability
TransitionProfile
Brain/causal evidence
Uncertainty
```

### F9-001
**Global experiential state ≠ one scalar `level of consciousness` by definition.**

### F9-002
A scalar may be constructed for a declared clinical or experimental purpose, but it is a projection from a multidimensional state profile.

Bayne, Hohwy & Owen's multidimensional critique of `levels of consciousness` is therefore adopted as a methodological constraint, not as a complete empirical theory.

---

# 2. Experiential capacity versus current experience

Introduce:

```text
ExperientialCapacityStanding(B|H,Route,Σ)
```

meaning bearer B retains the organization/capacity to instantiate ExperienceStanding tokens over horizon H under declared endogenous/exogenous conditions.

Separate:

```text
CurrentExperienceStanding(E,B,t|Σ)
```

### F9-003
**ExperientialCapacityStanding ≠ CurrentExperienceStanding.**

A bearer can retain a capacity for experience while not currently having a detected experience token.

### F9-004
Conversely, evidence for a current experience establishes at least transient capacity but does not determine the full future state capacity profile.

This distinction is central to dreamless-sleep claims and disorders of consciousness.

---

# 3. Wakefulness

MF9-A introduced `WakefulnessStanding`.

Refine:

```text
WakefulnessStanding(W,B|Physio/BehavioralRoute,Σ)
```

as a state involving wake-like arousal/activation organization and often eye opening/behavioral readiness, but not itself phenomenal standing.

### F9-005
**Wakefulness ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### F9-006
**Wakefulness ≠ environmental connectedness.**

### F9-007
**Wakefulness ≠ behavioral responsiveness.**

UWS is the decisive hard case: wakefulness/eye-opening cycles can occur without ordinary behavioral evidence of awareness.

---

# 4. Arousal

```text
ArousalStanding(A,B|Route,Σ)
```

tracks activation/readiness dimensions relevant to wakefulness and responsiveness.

### F9-008
**Arousal ≠ Experience.**

### F9-009
**Arousal ≠ Wakefulness by identity.**

### F9-010
**Arousal ≠ cognitive access.**

Arousal can be physiologically high while cognition is disorganized, and some conscious states occur with low motor responsiveness.

# 5. Responsiveness

```text
BehavioralResponsivenessStanding(R,B|Stimulus,Effector,Criterion,Σ)
```

and:

```text
CommandFollowingStanding(CF,B|Command,Channel,Effector,Σ)
```

### F9-011
**BehavioralResponsiveness ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### F9-012
**No behavioral response ≠ no command comprehension.**

### F9-013
**No command-following ≠ no experience.**

Motor, language, sensory, attentional, memory, motivational and effector failures can all block observable behavior.

# 6. Connectedness

Anesthesia and sleep require a new dimension:

```text
EnvironmentalConnectednessStanding(C,B|ExternalDomain,Route,Σ)
```

meaning ongoing experience is appropriately coupled to current external environmental events.

Separate:

```text
DisconnectedExperienceStanding(E,B|Σ)
```

for experience dominated by internally generated content while ordinary environmental connectedness is absent or strongly reduced.

### F9-014
**Experience ≠ ConnectedExperience.**

### F9-015
**Disconnected consciousness ≠ unconsciousness.**

Dreaming and ketamine anesthesia are major examples.

---

# 7. Connected consciousness

Define:

```text
ConnectedExperienceStanding(E,B,World|Route,Σ)
```

requiring:

```text
ExperienceStanding(E,B)
+
content/organization causally linked to current environment
+
adequate evidence of that linkage
```

### F9-016
ConnectedExperienceStanding is stronger than generic sensory processing; unconscious sensory responses do not suffice.

### F9-017
ConnectedExperienceStanding does not require later episodic memory by definition.

This matters in anesthesia, where later amnesia can mask intraoperative connectedness.

---

# 8. Memory/recall firewall

Introduce:

```text
ExperienceRecallStanding(R,B,E|Delay,Probe,Σ)
```

### F9-018
**NoRecall ≠ NoExperience.**

### F9-019
**DelayedReport ≠ direct real-time readout.**

### F9-020
Dream/anesthesia retrospective reports are evidence about prior experience but are filtered through memory encoding, retention and retrieval.

Therefore:

```text
No dream report
```

cannot be equated with:

```text
proven absence of all prior experience
```

without additional evidence.

# 9. Sleep stage is not consciousness state

Introduce:

```text
SleepStageStanding(S,B,t|PSGRoute,Σ)
```

for polysomnographic stage classification.

### F9-021
**REM ≠ Dreaming by identity.**

### F9-022
**NREM ≠ NoExperience.**

Siclari et al. found dream reports in both REM and NREM and contrasted experience versus no-experience reports within sleep stages; posterior low-frequency/high-frequency activity differentiated these reports more directly than sleep-stage label alone.

### F9-023
Sleep stage is a physiological state descriptor, not a phenomenal-state label.

# 10. Dreaming

Introduce:

```text
DreamExperienceStanding(E,B|SleepState,RecallRoute,Σ)
```

for experiences occurring during sleep that are largely disconnected from current environmental sensory control.

### F9-024
**Dreaming = ExperienceStanding compatible with BehavioralUnresponsivenessStanding.**

### F9-025
Dreaming demonstrates that the bearer can remain experientially active while ordinary external command following is absent.

### F9-026
Dreaming also falsifies a universal equation:

```text
EnvironmentalConnectedness = Consciousness
```

---

# 11. Dream report categories

Sleep studies often need at least:

```text
DE   = dream experience with reportable content
DEWR = dream experience claimed but content not recalled
NE   = report of no experience
```

### F9-027
**DEWR ≠ NE.**

### F9-028
**NE report ≠ metaphysical proof of no experience.**

`NE` is best represented as:

```text
NoExperienceReportStanding
```

with uncertainty about false-negative recall.

---

# 12. Posterior dream evidence

Siclari et al. (2017) found that across NREM and REM sleep, reported dream experience versus no-experience reports was associated with reduced low-frequency activity in a posterior cortical hot zone; higher-frequency activity tracked certain dream contents.

### F9-029
This shows that global wakefulness and REM status are not sufficient state descriptors for current ExperienceStanding.

### F9-030
It provides a content/state neural correlate, not a direct proof that posterior activity alone is constitutively sufficient.

This is compatible with MF9-D's posterior/RPT evidence while preserving theory neutrality.

---

# 13. Dreamless sleep remains epistemically difficult

Three different claims must be separated:

```text
A. No remembered dream content.
B. Subject reports no experience.
C. Literally no ExperienceStanding occurred.
```

### F9-031
A ≠ B ≠ C.

### F9-032
Current sleep evidence supports intervals with no-experience reports, but literal total absence of experience is harder to prove because retrospective access is itself state-dependent.

### F9-033
MF9-B's MinimalExperienceStanding remains relevant: sparse bodily/presence-like experience could be missed by ordinary dream-content probes.

# 15. Unresponsiveness under anesthesia

Sanders et al.'s isolated-forearm work provides direct evidence that behavioral unresponsiveness/paralysis at the whole-body level can coexist with volitional command following through an isolated limb.

In a 2017 multicenter cohort, 12 of 260 patients (4.6%) responded after intubation; some responders also indicated pain.

### F9-036
**WholeBodyUnresponsiveness ≠ NoVolitionalResponse.**

### F9-037
**Neuromuscular paralysis ≠ Experience suppression.**

### F9-038
**Standard anesthetic state labels do not guarantee absence of connected consciousness.**

---

# 16. Connected consciousness under anesthesia

The isolated forearm technique tests a particularly strong form of state evidence:

```text
under intended anesthesia
+
under paralysis outside isolated limb
+
correct response to verbal command
```

This supports:

```text
AuditoryAccess
CommandComprehension
WorkingMaintenance
VolitionalControl
MotorExecution via spared effector
```

### F9-039
IFT response is therefore strong evidence of `ConnectedExperienceStanding`/conscious cognitive access, not merely sensory activation.

### F9-040
Failure to respond on IFT is not symmetric evidence of unconsciousness because command comprehension, attention, task persistence and effector use can fail.

---

# 17. Consciousness versus connectedness under anesthesia

A useful state decomposition is:

```text
Connected Consciousness
Disconnected Consciousness
No detected consciousness
```

### F9-041
Anesthetic drugs can reduce environmental connectedness before—or differently from—abolishing endogenous experience.

### F9-042
This makes `connectedness` a genuine dimension of global experiential state rather than a synonym for consciousness.

---

# 18. Ketamine hard case

Sarasso et al. (2015) compared propofol, xenon and ketamine during behavioral unresponsiveness.

They found:

```text
propofol / xenon:
  low TMS-evoked cortical complexity
  no post-emergence reports in that sample

ketamine:
  wake-like/high cortical response complexity
  vivid dream reports after emergence
```

### F9-043
**BehavioralUnresponsiveness does not fix experiential status across anesthetic agents.**

### F9-044
Anesthesia must be modeled agent-by-agent and dimension-by-dimension rather than as an `off` switch.

---

# 19. Propofol/xenon no-report evidence must remain cautious

Sarasso et al.'s absence of post-anesthesia experience reports under propofol/xenon is evidence compatible with unconsciousness in that experiment.

### F9-045
But:

```text
NoDelayedReport
≠ logical proof of NoExperience
```

because anesthetics also affect memory.

### F9-046
The combination of low perturbational complexity + absent report is stronger than either alone but remains an evidence profile, not an ontological identity.

# 20. Perturbational complexity

Introduce:

```text
PerturbationalComplexityEvidenceStanding(P,B|Protocol,Metric,Threshold,Σ)
```

Casali et al. (2013) defined PCI by perturbing cortex with TMS and compressing the distributed spatiotemporal EEG response.

### F9-047
**PCI ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### F9-048
**PCI ≠ IIT Φ.**

### F9-049
PCI is a theory-informed empirical marker of the brain's capacity for complex causal responses.

---

# 21. PCI and state capacity

Casali et al. tested PCI across wakefulness, sleep, dreaming, anesthesia and brain injury.

Casarotto et al. (2016) then derived a benchmark cutoff from communicative/conscious versus report-absent reference states and applied it to unresponsive patients.

### F9-050
A high PCI in an unresponsive patient is best interpreted as evidence for preserved **capacity for consciousness / complex experiential-supporting dynamics**, not as direct access to a specific current content.

### F9-051
This distinction between `capacity evidence` and `current content evidence` is fundamental.

---

# 22. PCI high in behaviorally UWS patients

Casarotto et al. found high PCI values in a subset of behaviorally diagnosed VS/UWS patients, overlapping the conscious benchmark range.

### F9-052
**Behavioral UWS diagnosis ≠ proof of absent experiential capacity.**

### F9-053
Brain-based state markers can expose capacity not expressed through behavior.

But:

### F9-054
High PCI does not by itself specify whether the patient is currently experiencing anything at the exact measurement moment, what that experience contains, or whether environmental connectedness is present.

# 24. Coma

For MF9-F:

```text
ComaStanding(C,B|ClinicalRoute,Σ)
```

is a clinical syndrome involving absence of wakefulness/eye-opening and absence of purposeful responsiveness over the relevant interval.

### F9-056
**ComaStanding ≠ proven NoExperienceStanding.**

Clinically, coma is operationally diagnosed behaviorally/neurologically; covert experience is difficult to assess directly.

### F9-057
Coma differs from UWS because wakefulness/eye-opening cycles are absent.

# 25. Unresponsive Wakefulness Syndrome

Laureys et al. proposed `unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (UWS)` as a descriptive replacement for `vegetative state`.

Define:

```text
UWSStanding(U,B|ClinicalRoute,Σ)
```

as:

```text
wakefulness / eye-opening cycles present
+
no reproducible behavioral evidence of awareness/command following under standard assessment
```

### F9-058
**UWS = wakefulness without demonstrated behavioral awareness, not metaphysical proof of no experience.**

This distinction is now empirically mandatory because covert command-following and high-complexity subgroups exist.

---

# 26. Minimally Conscious State

Giacino et al. define MCS by inconsistent but definite/reproducible behavioral evidence of self or environmental awareness.

Introduce:

```text
MCSStanding(M,B|CRSRoute,Σ)
```

### F9-059
**MCS is behaviorally defined evidence of consciousness, not a claim that the person's phenomenality itself is `minimal` in richness.**

A patient can show only minimal behavioral output while potentially having much richer experience than behavior reveals.

### F9-060
Therefore:

```text
MinimallyConsciousState
≠ MinimalExperienceStanding
```

This terminology firewall is mandatory.

---

# 27. MCS-minus and MCS-plus

Later clinical usage distinguishes:

```text
MCS−:
  lower-level behavioral signs such as visual pursuit/localization
  without command following / intelligible language

MCS+:
  higher-level signs including command following or intelligible verbal behavior
```

### F9-061
These are behavioral/cognitive stratifications, not direct phenomenal-rank categories.

### F9-062
They should not be mapped onto `less experience` / `more experience` without independent evidence.

---

# 28. Emergence from MCS

Classic criteria include reliable functional communication and/or functional object use.

### F9-063
**EmergenceFromMCSStanding ≠ onset of ExperienceStanding.**

It marks a behavioral functional boundary, not the moment consciousness begins.

This prevents a serious clinical/ontological category error.

---

# 29. Locked-in syndrome

Introduce:

```text
LockedInStanding(L,B|Motor/CommunicationRoute,Σ)
```

for severe motor output impairment with preserved consciousness/cognition, often with limited eye movement/blinking communication routes.

### F9-064
**MotorOutputCapacity ≠ ExperienceCapacity.**

### F9-065
**CommunicationBandwidth ≠ cognitive/phenomenal richness.**

LIS is the clearest classical case showing that a highly conscious bearer can look almost entirely behaviorally unresponsive if the output channel is destroyed.

---

# 30. Cognitive Motor Dissociation

Introduce:

```text
CognitiveMotorDissociationStanding(CMD,B|Task,NeuralRoute,BehavioralRoute,Σ)
```

when:

```text
no observable command-following behavior
+
reproducible task-contingent brain response to commands
```

that requires sustained cognitive operations.

### F9-066
CMD is not simply `hidden movement`; it is a dissociation between behaviorally expressed motor response and neurally detected command performance.

---

# 31. CMD evidence strength

Owen et al. (2006) first showed a behaviorally vegetative-state patient generating task-specific imagery responses to command.

Monti et al. (2010) found willful fMRI modulation in 5 of 54 DoC patients and established yes/no fMRI communication in one patient despite absent bedside communication.

### F9-067
Task-specific command-following provides strong evidence of:

```text
language comprehension
instruction maintenance
selective task set
volitional initiation
sustained imagery/cognitive control
```

### F9-068
It therefore constitutes much stronger evidence of conscious cognitive access than passive sensory activation alone.

---

# 32. Large-scale CMD evidence

Bodien et al. (2024) studied 353 adults across six international centers.

Among 241 participants with no observable verbal command response, 60 (25%) showed task-based fMRI/EEG command responses and met the operational definition of CMD.

### F9-069
**ObservableCommandFollowing ≠ CommandFollowingCapacity.**

### F9-070
Behavior alone can substantially underestimate preserved cognition in severe brain injury.

This is one of MF9-F's strongest falsifiers of `Responsiveness = Consciousness`.

---

# 33. CMD false-negative asymmetry

In the same 2024 cohort, task-based fMRI/EEG responses were detected in only 43 of 112 participants who *did* show observable command responses behaviorally.

### F9-071
**No neural task response ≠ no command capacity.**

These tasks have high cognitive and technical demands and low sensitivity in some known-conscious/behaviorally responsive participants.

### F9-072
Positive CMD evidence is much stronger than negative CMD evidence.

This asymmetry must be encoded in evidence interpretation.

---

# 34. Covert consciousness is overloaded

Distinguish:

```text
CovertExperienceClaim
CovertCommandFollowingClaim
CovertCognitiveCapacityClaim
HighComplexityCapacityClaim
```

### F9-073
**CMD ≠ generic proof of every kind of conscious experience.**

It strongly establishes a complex conscious-access/cognitive route under the task, but not the total phenomenological repertoire of the patient.

### F9-074
High PCI without command following is a different evidence class from CMD.

# 40. State transition is not instantaneous binary switching

```text
ExperientialStateTransitionStanding(G1→G2,B|Trajectory,Σ)
```

Anesthetic induction/emergence, sleep onset/awakening and DoC recovery can change dimensions at different times.

### F9-086

### F9-089

# 44. Sleep versus anesthesia

### F9-094
**Sleep ≠ Anesthesia.**

### F9-095

# 46. UWS versus MCS

```text
BehaviorallyUnresponsive
```

and even:

```text
behaviorally classified coma/UWS/MCS−
```

cannot be treated as synonymous with:

```text

```

### F9-100
Behavioral UWS is now explicitly an **observable-response phenotype**, not a final negative inference about all covert cognition.

# 48. CMD is not LIS

Locked-in syndrome has known/preserved consciousness with severe motor output loss and often a residual communication route.

CMD is detected when behaviorally overt command following is absent but task-contingent neural command following is present.

### F9-101
**CMD ≠ classical LIS.**

### F9-102
But CMD demonstrates a broader principle shared with LIS:

```text
MotorInterfaceFailure
≠ Cognitive/ExperientialFailure
```

---

# 49. Evidence hierarchy is not a simple ranking

Different evidence can support different claims:

```text
Immediate verbal report
  strong content/report evidence, but requires output channel

Delayed report
  experience evidence with memory filter

Overt command following
  strong access/volition evidence

Covert neural command following
  strong access/volition evidence without motor output

No-report perceptual proxy
  lower report confound, indirect experience inference

PCI/high complexity
  state-capacity evidence, content-poor

Passive sensory activation
  processing evidence, weak consciousness specificity
```

### F9-103
There is no one evidence source that dominates every question.

### F9-104
Evidence must be matched to the target claim.

---

# 50. Positive versus negative evidence asymmetry

For hidden experience/cognition:

```text
specific positive command-following pattern
```

can strongly establish preserved capability.

But:

```text
failure to detect it
```

can arise from:

```text
fatigue
sensory/language impairment
attention failure
technical noise
poor task choice
state fluctuation
insufficient sensitivity
```

### F9-105
**Positive covert-command evidence and negative covert-command evidence are not epistemically symmetric.**

This is supported directly by the 2024 cohort, where many behaviorally command-following patients failed the imaging/EEG paradigms.

ExperientialStateStabilityStanding(S,B|H,Σ)

# 66. Evidence anchors

1. **Bayne, Hohwy & Owen (2016), `Are There Levels of Consciousness?`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 20:405–413, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2016.03.009`.** Argues that global states are multidimensional rather than adequately represented by a single conscious-level ordering.
2. **Siclari et al. (2017), `The neural correlates of dreaming`, Nature Neuroscience 20:872–878, DOI `10.1038/nn.4545`.** Dreaming occurs in REM and NREM; reported experience versus no-experience reports associated with posterior low-frequency/high-frequency activity differences.
3. **Sanders et al. (2012), `Unresponsiveness ≠ unconsciousness`, Anesthesiology 116:946–959, DOI `10.1097/ALN.0b013e318249d0a7`.** Conceptual/empirical synthesis separating responsiveness, connectedness and consciousness under anesthesia.
4. **Sanders et al. (2017), `Incidence of Connected Consciousness after Tracheal Intubation`, Anesthesiology 126:214–222, DOI `10.1097/ALN.0000000000001479`.** Multicenter isolated-forearm cohort; 12/260 responded after intubation, with pain reported by some responders.
5. **Gaskell et al. (2017), `Frontal alpha-delta EEG does not preclude volitional response during anaesthesia`, British Journal of Anaesthesia 119:664–673, DOI `10.1093/bja/aex170`.** Volitional response can occur despite frontal EEG patterns conventionally associated with general anesthesia.
6. **Sarasso et al. (2015), `Consciousness and Complexity during Unresponsiveness Induced by Propofol, Xenon, and Ketamine`, Current Biology 25:3099–3105, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2015.10.014`.** Ketamine unresponsiveness with vivid delayed dream reports/high perturbational complexity versus low complexity/no reports in propofol/xenon sample.
7. **Casali et al. (2013), `A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior`, Science Translational Medicine 5:198ra105, DOI `10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294`.** Introduces PCI across wakefulness, dreaming, NREM sleep, anesthesia and DoC.
8. **Casarotto et al. (2016), `Stratification of unresponsive patients by an independently validated index of brain complexity`, Annals of Neurology 80:718–729, DOI `10.1002/ana.24779`.** PCI benchmark and high-PCI subgroup among behaviorally VS/UWS patients.
9. **Giacino et al. (2002), `The minimally conscious state: definition and diagnostic criteria`, Neurology 58:349–353, DOI `10.1212/WNL.58.3.349`.** Consensus behavioral definition of MCS and emergence criteria; explicitly notes sensory/motor impairments can underestimate cognition.
10. **Laureys et al. (2010), `Unresponsive wakefulness syndrome: a new name for the vegetative state or apallic syndrome`, BMC Medicine 8:68, DOI `10.1186/1741-7015-8-68`.** Descriptive UWS terminology emphasizing wakefulness with unresponsiveness.
11. **Owen et al. (2006), `Detecting awareness in the vegetative state`, Science 313:1402, DOI `10.1126/science.1130197`.** Task-specific fMRI imagery response to command in a behaviorally diagnosed vegetative-state patient.
12. **Monti et al. (2010), `Willful modulation of brain activity in disorders of consciousness`, New England Journal of Medicine 362:579–589, DOI `10.1056/NEJMoa0905370`.** Willful fMRI modulation in a subset of DoC patients; one patient communicated yes/no despite lack of bedside communication.
13. **Claassen et al. (2019), `Detection of Brain Activation in Unresponsive Patients with Acute Brain Injury`, New England Journal of Medicine 380:2497–2505, DOI `10.1056/NEJMoa1812757`.** EEG command-following/cognitive-motor dissociation in acutely unresponsive brain-injured patients.
14. **Bodien et al. (2024), `Cognitive Motor Dissociation in Disorders of Consciousness`, New England Journal of Medicine 391:598–608, DOI `10.1056/NEJMoa2400645`.** Six-center prospective cohort; 60/241 (25%) participants without observable command responses showed task-based fMRI/EEG command responses; neural tasks also produced many false negatives in behaviorally responsive patients.
15. **AAN/ACRM/NIDILRR Practice Guideline Update (2018), `Disorders of consciousness`, Neurology, DOI `10.1212/WNL.0000000000005926`.** Recommends treating confounds, optimizing arousal and serial standardized behavioral assessment for diagnostic accuracy.
