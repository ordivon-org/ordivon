# 0. Purpose

MF5-A→D deliberately built a general spatial ontology without assuming that an observer experiences physical geometry veridically.

MF5-E asks the embodied falsifier:

> **What spatial structure is actually available to a perceiving/acting system, and how do retinal/image, perceptual, body-centered, gravitational, action-linked and consciously experienced spatial organizations relate to physical space?**

The dangerous collapses are:

```text
Retinal/Image Space = Perceptual Space = Physical Space
Perceived Distance = Physical Distance
Perceptual Boundary = Material Boundary
Visual Field = Experienced World Space
Egocentric = Allocentric
Body-Centered Space = Physical Near Space
Peripersonal = Reachable
Subjective Vertical = Gravity Vector
Perceptual Upright = Subjective Visual Vertical
Spatial Perception = Conscious Spatial Experience
One Spatial Task = One Internal Metric
One Neural Spatial Code = One Canonical Internal Map
```

MF5-E keeps phenomenon, behavioral geometry, neural evidence and implementation separate.

# 1. Image/retinal space is not perceived world space

An optical image/retina supplies a viewpoint-dependent projection. Lateral image relations may be directly available while scene depth remains underdetermined from one view and must be constrained by disparity, occlusion, motion, perspective, shading, learned/world structure and other cues.

Koenderink & van Doorn explicitly distinguish the relatively well-defined lateral visual field/image structure from the ambiguous depth organization of visual space.

### SE-01

**Retinal/image geometry ≠ perceived 3D spatial geometry.**

### SE-02

**Depth organization is an inference/organization problem rather than a copied third image coordinate.**

---

# 2. Perceptual depth can be structurally meaningful while underdetermined

Ambiguous depth does not imply no spatial organization. A perceiver can maintain ordinal depth, surface layout, occlusion order or approximate distance while exact metric depth remains uncertain.

### SE-03

**Spatial standing can be partial/profile-specific under perceptual uncertainty.**

### SE-04

`DepthOrder ≠ MetricDepth ≠ SurfaceGeometry ≠ ObjectIdentity`.

---

# 3. Binocular information improves depth without guaranteeing physical-metric identity

Empirical depth studies show binocular viewing can markedly improve depth discrimination/estimation relative to monocular viewing, including at multi-meter distances, while estimates can still remain compressed/nonveridical.

### SE-05

**More sensory constraint can improve spatial accuracy/precision without collapsing perceptual geometry into physical geometry.**

### SE-06

**Cue sufficiency is task/range dependent; no single depth cue is frozen as universal spatial foundation.**

---

# 4. Perceived visual space can be geometrically distorted

Loomis, da Silva, Fujita & Fukusima found that observers required larger physical depth intervals than frontal intervals for them to appear equal, with the disparity increasing across tested egocentric distances.

### SE-07

**Equal physical lengths need not have equal perceived lengths across spatial directions.**

### SE-08

**Perceptual spatial geometry can be anisotropic/nonveridical relative to Euclidean physical geometry.**

---

# 5. One observer can expose different spatial geometries through different tasks

In the same Loomis et al. study, visually judged depth-vs-width interval matching was distorted, while open-loop walking and pointing to previewed target locations were relatively accurate.

### SE-09

**Explicit spatial-appearance geometry ≠ visually guided action/location geometry.**

### SE-10

**One behavioral task cannot be assumed to reveal one complete internal spatial metric.**

This is one of MF5-E's strongest falsifiers of `Perceptual Space = one metric map`.

---

# 6. Verbal, manual and locomotor measures are not interchangeable readouts

Foley's distance experiments found large differences between verbal and manual distance indices, even when the two were related by a transform, and strong dependence on available distance cues.

### SE-11

**Spatial report format/task can change the observed response geometry.**

### SE-12

**Behavioral readout geometry ≠ perceptual ontology by default.**

A report may include response mapping, memory, motor calibration and decision components.

---

# 7. Visual-space geometry is therefore operationally plural

MF5-E provisionally separates:

```text
AppearanceGeometry
LocalizationGeometry
ActionGuidanceGeometry
RecognitionGeometry

ReportGeometry
```

### SE-13

**These profiles may share information while showing different distortions/invariants.**

### SE-14

**Do not infer one hidden canonical metric merely because several tasks correlate.**

---

# 8. `Perceptual space` must remain weaker than a literal internal map claim

A system can discriminate spatial relations and guide behavior without storing a single explicit 3D Cartesian scene model.

### SE-15

**Perceptual spatial standing ≠ explicit coordinate-map implementation.**

### SE-16

**MF5-E remains implementation-neutral: spatial organization can be distributed, relational, frame-specific, task-specific, dynamical or model-based.**

---

# 9. Visual field is not the same as perceived world extent

The optical/retinal field has a sensor geometry and field of view. Experienced/world-relative space includes inferred continuation, occluded objects, body relation and remembered/expected layout beyond current retinal stimulation.

### SE-17

**Current visual field ≠ currently represented/perceived spatial world.**

### SE-18

**Visible extent ≠ world extent ≠ spatial standing.**

This strengthens MF5-D `Visibility ≠ Occupancy`.

---

# 10. Occluded space can remain perceptually structured

An object disappearing behind an occluder can retain tracked/persistent spatial relation despite no current image support for its hidden surface.

### SE-19

**Perceptual spatial persistence can extend beyond currently visible regions.**

### SE-20

This does not mean hidden detail is perceptually evidenced; identity/location continuity and unseen surface/detail certainty remain separate.

# 11. Perceptual boundary is not material boundary

MF2/MF4 already showed border ownership, figure-ground and completion. MF5-D added boundary-standing routes.

MF5-E now states:

### SE-21

**Perceptual boundary standing ≠ material boundary standing.**

A contour may be due to occlusion, illumination, texture, grouping or inferred surface separation.

### SE-22

**Perceptually organized region identity can survive missing/occluded physical image boundaries.**

---

# 12. Egocentric and allocentric relations are different spatial predicates

Egocentric organization expresses spatial relations relative to the observer/body/sensor.
Allocentric organization expresses relations relative to environment/world/landmark/configuration structure.

### SE-23

`Egocentric ≠ Allocentric`.

### SE-24

**One location can have stable allocentric relations while its egocentric coordinates continuously change during self-motion.**

---

# 13. Retinocentric, head/body-centered and object-centered frames also remain distinct

Fogassi et al. found many F4 visual receptive fields near the monkey that remained in similar positions relative to the body despite eye-position changes, while a minority were retinocentric.

### SE-25

**Retinocentric ≠ somato/body-centered spatial coding.**

### SE-26

**Multiple frame types can coexist within one nervous system and support different effectors/tasks.**

The authors' own conclusion that space coding differed with the computational needs of controlled effectors is retained as a model-consistent empirical interpretation, not a universal neural law.

---

# 14. Peripersonal space is body-linked, not simply metric-near space

Graziano/Gross and Fogassi et al. reported bimodal visual-somatosensory receptive fields around body parts, often responding to approaching stimuli in nearby space.

### SE-27

**Peripersonal organization can be body-part-centered/somatosensory-linked rather than a fixed world-centered radius.**

### SE-28

**Physical metric nearness is insufficient to define peripersonal space universally.**

---

# 15. Peripersonal space is not identical to reachable space

Some near-body coding can concern defensive/contact-related space; reachability depends on limb/tool/action capability and constraints.

### SE-29

**Peripersonal ≠ reachable ≠ graspable ≠ visible.**

These relations can strongly overlap without being identical.

---

# 16. Tool use is a hard falsifier of fixed body-space boundaries

Iriki, Tanaka & Iwamura trained macaques to retrieve distant objects with a rake and found bimodal postcentral neurons whose visual receptive fields altered during tool use to include the rake or expanded accessible space.

### SE-30

**Body/action-centered spatial organization can be plastic on behavioral timescales.**

### SE-31

**The boundary of action-related body space need not equal anatomical skin extent.**

### SE-32

**Tool incorporation evidence does not imply literal ownership of tool tissue; physical body, body schema and action-accessible space remain distinct.**

---

# 17. Body schema is not the physical body

MF5-E provisionally uses `BodySchema` for action/perception-relevant structured body relation rather than a complete self-concept.

### SE-33

**Physical body geometry ≠ body-schema geometry.**

### SE-34

**Body schema ≠ conscious body image/self-concept.**

This distinction remains provisional because MF9 Experience and MF10 Social will later add richer self/body phenomenology.

---

# 18. Proprioceptive space contributes without vision

Body joint/muscle/tactile state supplies spatial relations among body parts and between body and action targets even when current vision is absent.

### SE-35

**Body-centered spatial organization is multisensory/sensorimotor, not visually constitutive.**

### SE-36

**Visual spatial dominance in some tasks does not make vision ontologically primary for all space perception.**

---

# 19. Vestibular/gravity reference introduces another spatial axis

The perceived direction of vertical/up depends on vestibular/gravity, body/proprioceptive and visual context cues.

Dyde, Jenkin & Harris experimentally separated subjective visual vertical (SVV) from perceptual upright (PU): under cue conflict, SVV tracked gravity more strongly while PU was more strongly body/visual-context weighted.

### SE-37

**`Up` is not one undifferentiated perceptual variable.**

### SE-38

`SubjectiveVisualVertical ≠ PerceptualUpright`.

### SE-39

**Gravity-centered, body-centered and visual-context reference contributions can be differently weighted by task.**

---

# 20. Physical gravity vector is not identical to perceived vertical

Vestibular lesions/body tilt/visual context can bias subjective visual vertical.

### SE-40

**Physical vertical ≠ estimated/perceived vertical.**

### SE-41

**Perceived orientation reference is a multisensory estimate/organization rather than direct readout of one receptor channel.**

# 21. Distinct tasks can access distinct gravity estimates

Studies comparing subjective visual and haptic vertical under body tilt report systematic dissociations, supporting multiple related estimates rather than one mandatory single scalar `gravity percept`.

### SE-42

**Different modality/task readouts may access distinct but coupled spatial-reference estimates.**

### SE-43

**Cue integration must not be collapsed into one universal maximum-likelihood mechanism; empirical weighting can be task-dependent and non-identical across measures.**

---

# 22. Body-centered space and gravitational space can conflict

A tilted body creates divergence among:

- body axis;
- visual frame;
- gravity vertical.

### SE-44

**Body-up ≠ gravity-up ≠ visual-context-up.**

### SE-45

The experienced/perceived upright can result from their integration rather than choosing one universal absolute axis.

# 23. Perceived distance is cue-conditional

Foley's experiments varied monocular, binocular and multicue viewing and found substantial changes in judged distance.

### SE-46

**Perceived metric relations depend on available cue structure and calibration.**

### SE-47

**Perceptual distance is not a context-free transform of physical distance.**

This preserves MF2-F and MF4-G distinctions.

# 25. Sensory substitution is a hard modality falsifier

Bach-y-Rita et al.'s tactile vision-substitution system projected camera-derived patterns onto the skin; trained blind participants could identify forms and other spatially structured stimuli, and related reports included movement/depth and environmental-object discrimination.

### SE-49

**Spatial organization is not intrinsically tied to one sensory receptor modality.**

### SE-50

**A nonvisual afferent channel can support spatially useful distinctions when sensorimotor/representational mapping is learnable and sufficiently structured.**

This does not imply that tactile-substitution phenomenology becomes literally normal vision.

### SE-52

**MF5 cannot define spatial standing by one modality-specific phenomenal quality.**

### SE-54

# 54. Primary/original literature anchors

- **Jan J. Koenderink & Andrea J. van Doorn (2008)**, `The Structure of Visual Spaces`, *Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision* 31, 171–187. Treats lateral visual-field structure as relatively determinate while depth from monocular cues is ambiguous/inferred; explores geometrical structure of visual spaces under cue ambiguity.
- **Jack M. Loomis, José A. da Silva, Naofumi Fujita & Sergio S. Fukusima (1992)**, `Visual Space Perception and Visually Directed Action`, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance* 18(4), 906–921, DOI 10.1037/0096-1523.18.4.906. Reports depth-vs-frontal interval perceptual distortion while open-loop walking/pointing to previewed targets remains comparatively accurate.
- **John M. Foley (1977)**, `Effect of distance information and range on two indices of visually perceived distance`, *Perception* 6(4), 449–460, DOI 10.1068/p060449. Verbal/manual distance responses differ and change with monocular/binocular/multicue viewing, showing response/cue dependence.
- **Leonardo Fogassi, Vittorio Gallese, Luciano Fadiga, Giuseppe Luppino, Michele Matelli & Giacomo Rizzolatti (1996)**, `Coding of peripersonal space in inferior premotor cortex (area F4)`, *Journal of Neurophysiology* 76(1), 141–157, DOI 10.1152/jn.1996.76.1.141. Reports visual/somatosensory bimodal near-body receptive fields, many somatocentered rather than retinocentric, and concludes spatial coding differs with effector computational needs.
- **Michael S. Graziano & Charles G. Gross (1993)**, `A bimodal map of space: somatosensory receptive fields in the macaque putamen with corresponding visual receptive fields`, *Experimental Brain Research* 97, 96–109, DOI 10.1007/BF00228820. Reports bimodal body-part-centered visual/somatosensory fields in nearby space.
- **Atsushi Iriki, Michio Tanaka & Yoshiaki Iwamura (1996)**, `Coding of modified body schema during tool use by macaque postcentral neurones`, *NeuroReport* 7(14), 2325–2330, DOI 10.1097/00001756-199610020-00010. Tool use altered bimodal receptive fields to include the rake or expanded accessible space.
- **Richard T. Dyde, Michael R. Jenkin & Laurence R. Harris (2006)**, `The subjective visual vertical and the perceptual upright`, *Experimental Brain Research* 173, 612–622, DOI 10.1007/s00221-006-0405-y. Separates subjective visual vertical from perceptual upright and demonstrates differing weighting of gravity/body/visual-context cues.
- **Paul Bach-y-Rita, Carter C. Collins, Frank A. Saunders, Benjamin White & Lawrence Scadden (1969)**, `Vision Substitution by Tactile Image Projection`, *Nature* 221, 963–964, DOI 10.1038/221963a0; related 1969 tactile-vision system reports show trained blind users extracting spatial/form/object information from camera-to-skin stimulation.

> **Perceptual space is the system-relative, stateful and potentially action-coupled spatial organization of sensorimotor evidence into body/world-relative loci, regions, directions, depths, surfaces and relations under uncertainty; its geometry, reference frames and fidelity are task/profile dependent and need not be identical to retinal coordinates, physical Euclidean geometry, action geometry or conscious spatial experience.**

Compact:

