# 34. Contentless experience problem

Claims of `pure consciousness`, `pure awareness` or `content-free awareness` are now treated as a genuine empirical/phenomenological challenge rather than dismissed or accepted by definition.

Metzinger et al.'s MPE questionnaire collected 1,403 usable reports and explicitly treated pure-awareness states as a phenomenological prototype rather than prejudging literal absence of all content. Winter et al. reported an expert meditator's `content-free awareness` episode, but this single-case neurophenomenological evidence does not settle ontology universally.

MF9-B therefore distinguishes:

```text
No Object-Directed Content
No Sensory Content
No Conceptual Thought Content
No Self Content
No Spatial/Temporal Content
Minimal Phenomenal Structure
Literally No Experiential Presentation/Character
```

These are not equivalent.

### B9-084
**`Contentless` is an overloaded negative label.**

### B9-085
A reported state can lack ordinary object/sensory/conceptual content while still possess phenomenological features such as clarity, openness, presence, wakefulness, stillness or awareness-as-such.

### B9-086
If literally no experiential presentation/character whatsoever remains, then it becomes unclear what distinguishes the claim from `no ExperienceStanding`; this is a live falsification boundary, not resolved by terminology.

### B9-087
MF9-B therefore preserves `MinimalExperienceStanding` as a candidate rather than freezing `contentless experience exists` or `contentless experience is impossible`.

# 44. Evidence anchors

1. **Tim Bayne (2010), *The Unity of Consciousness*, Oxford University Press, DOI `10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199215386.001.0001`.** Develops phenomenal field/unity, mereological unity and tests fragmentation/disunity cases; retained as a major unity route, not universal ontology fact.
2. **Tim Bayne & David Chalmers (2003), `What is the Unity of Consciousness?`, in *The Unity of Consciousness: Binding, Integration, and Dissociation*, DOI `10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198508571.003.0002`.** Distinguishes multiple unity notions and makes unity itself a substantive target requiring precise formulation.
3. **Michael Tye (1995), `A Representational Theory of Pains and Their Phenomenal Character`, Philosophical Perspectives 9, DOI `10.2307/2214219`.** Strong representationalist rival for pain/phenomenal character; used to keep content-character identity as a theory rather than base definition.
4. **Pierre Rainville, Gary H. Duncan, Donald D. Price, Benoît Carrier & M. Catherine Bushnell (1997), `Pain affect encoded in human anterior cingulate but not somatosensory cortex`, Science 277, 968–971, DOI `10.1126/science.277.5328.968`.** Hypnotic manipulation selectively altered unpleasantness without changing perceived intensity, supporting multidimensional pain phenomenology.
5. **Nikos K. Logothetis, David A. Leopold & David L. Sheinberg (1996), `What is rivalling during binocular rivalry?`, Nature 380, 621–624.** Stable/controlled sensory conditions with alternating perceptual dominance support separation of input from experiential organization/content.
6. **Casey O'Callaghan (2019), *A Multisensory Philosophy of Perception*, Oxford University Press, DOI `10.1093/oso/9780198833703.001.0001`.** Argues perceptual phenomenal consciousness can contain irreducibly multisensory character beyond independent unimodal components plus mere co-consciousness.
7. **A. D. Craig (2002), `How do you feel? Interoception: the sense of the physiological condition of the body`, Nature Reviews Neuroscience 3, 655–666, DOI `10.1038/nrn894`.** Interoceptive bodily-condition route and subjective feeling/self relevance; supports keeping bodily experience central rather than treating vision as universal template.
8. **Hugo D. Critchley et al. (2004), `Neural systems supporting interoceptive awareness`, Nature Neuroscience 7, 189–195, DOI `10.1038/nn1176`.** Empirical interoceptive-awareness evidence while leaving representation/awareness/feeling ontology distinct.
9. **Anil K. Seth (2013), `Interoceptive inference, emotion, and the embodied self`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 17, 565–573, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2013.09.007`.** Predictive/interoceptive-inference rival mechanism for feeling and embodied self; retained as a theory rather than definition.
10. **Alan Baddeley & Jackie Andrade (2000), `Working memory and the vividness of imagery`, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 129, 126–145, DOI `10.1037/0096-3445.129.1.126`.** Modality-specific interference changes subjective imagery vividness, supporting vividness as a decomposable experiential dimension distinct from stimulus strength.
11. **Thomas Metzinger et al. (2021), `The Minimal Phenomenal Experience questionnaire (MPE-92M)`, PLOS ONE / PMC8279394.** 1,403 usable meditation reports support serious empirical study of minimal/pure-awareness phenomenology while explicitly not prejudging literal absence of content.
12. **Goran Winter et al. (2020), `Content-Free Awareness: EEG-fcMRI Correlates of Consciousness as Such in an Expert Meditator`, Frontiers in Psychology / PMC7040185.** Single-case evidence for reported content-free awareness; useful as a boundary case but insufficient to settle universal ontology.

# 46. MF9-B verdict

MF9-B rejects the idea that experience is one undifferentiated `qualia blob` and also rejects the opposite mistake of importing a visual-object representation schema into every experience.

The reconstructed center is:

```text
EXPERIENCE OCCURRENCE
  = an experiential token for a subject

EXPERIENTIAL PRESENTATION
  = a phenomenally present aspect,
    prior to deciding whether it is representational/object-directed content

EXPERIENTIAL CONTENT
  = presentation with grounded content/aboutness under a declared route

PHENOMENAL CHARACTER
  = how the episode/aspect experientially feels/seems

DIFFERENTIATION
  = distinguishable experiential aspects

UNITY
  = a relation by which experiential aspects are co-experienced/part of a larger experiential organization
```

Then optional/rich structures include:

```text
modality / multimodality
field / figure-ground
spatiality
temporality
bodily/interoceptive character
valence/affect
perspective
presence/immediacy
intensity
clarity
vividness
ownership/agency/self content
```

The deepest corrections are:

> **Phenomenal unity and phenomenal differentiation are orthogonal rather than opposites. A unified experience can be richly differentiated.**

> **Experiential presentation sits below object-directed/representational content. This is required by pain, diffuse mood, bodily feeling and minimal-awareness cases.**

> **`Contentless experience` cannot remain a binary phrase. Absence of object, sensory, conceptual, self, spatial or temporal content are different claims; literal absence of every phenomenal presentation approaches `no experience` unless an additional differentiator is supplied.**

> **Intensity, clarity, vividness and valence are different phenomenal dimensions. Pain gives a direct experimental falsifier for one-dimensional phenomenology.**

> **Experienced space/time are not MF5/MF6 space/time. They are higher-level structures of how an episode is presented.**

MF9 now has a much clearer target structure against which theories of access and constitutive mechanisms can be tested.
