MF9-D introduces a typed theory stack:

```text
TheoryStack(T) = <

  SufficiencyClaim,
  Scope,

  AuxiliaryAssumptions,

```

### D9-001
**Theory ≠ one neural signature.**

### D9-002
**Failure of a biological implementation prediction ≠ automatic falsification of a computational core.**

### D9-003
Conversely, preserving a computational slogan after repeated implementation-level failures can become vacuous unless replacement realization rules and new falsifiers are supplied.

This distinction is essential in light of the 2025 Cogitate adversarial collaboration, which explicitly notes that empirical tests target biological implementations more directly than abstract computational cores and that theorists can revise implementations while retaining a core.

# 2. ConstitutiveBridgeStanding

Introduce:

```text
ConstitutiveBridgeStanding(M, P | Necessity, Sufficiency, Scope, Route, Σ)
```

where mechanism/property/organization `M` is claimed to constitute phenomenal target `P`.

Possible claims:

```text
M necessary for P
M sufficient for P
M necessary and sufficient for P
M merely correlates with P
M enables access/report about P
M modulates P
M realizes P only under boundary conditions C
```

### D9-004
**CorrelationStanding ≠ ConstitutiveBridgeStanding.**

### D9-005
**Access-enabling mechanism ≠ phenomenal-constituting mechanism by identity.**

### D9-006
All theories below must state necessity and sufficiency separately.

# 3. GNW — target clarification

Classic GNW develops from a global-workspace architecture with distributed specialized processors plus a widely connected workspace capable of broad information availability.

Dehaene, Kerszberg & Changeux (1998) explicitly distinguish specialized processors from a distributed global workspace recruited in effortful tasks.

Dehaene & Changeux (2011) characterize conscious access via late amplification, long-distance synchronization and ignition of a large-scale fronto-parietal network, with content globally available to multiple systems.

MF9-D therefore represents the strongest well-grounded GNW target as:

```text
GNW-CoreAccessClaim:
  selected information
  → nonlinear amplification / ignition
  → wide availability across multiple processors
  → flexible maintenance, combination, action and report
```

### D9-007
**GNW's clearest explanandum is conscious access/global availability.**

### D9-008
A stronger phenomenal reading requires an additional bridge:

```text
GNWConstitutiveBridge:
GlobalWorkspaceIgnition/Broadcast
→ ExperienceStanding
```

This bridge is not licensed merely by the definition of access.

# 4. GNW necessity claim

Strong form:

```text
For conscious content X:
ExperienceStanding(X)
→ GNW-like global ignition/broadcast
```

Potential support:

```text
masking / attentional blink
late widespread activity
P3-like/posterior-anterior amplification
flexible report and task use
prefrontal lesion effects on subjective access thresholds
```

Del Cul et al. (2009) found focal prefrontal lesions shifted masking thresholds for conscious access, especially subjective measures, while objective performance conditional on visibility was comparatively preserved.

### D9-009
This supports a causal PFC contribution to some visual-access/report regimes, but it does not isolate phenomenal constitution from metacognition/access.

### D9-010
`PFC contributes causally to awareness judgement/access` does not entail `PFC is universally necessary for every phenomenal experience`.

# 5. GNW sufficiency claim

Strong sufficiency would require:

```text
GNW-like ignition/global availability
→ ExperienceStanding
```

MF9-D finds this substantially underdetermined.

Artificial and organizational systems can implement:

```text
shared workspace
cross-module broadcast
central blackboard
flexible downstream routing
report generation
```

without independent evidence of phenomenality.

### D9-011
**Workspace architecture alone does not establish phenomenal sufficiency.**

### D9-012
A GNW phenomenal-sufficiency theory must state which architectural/causal properties exceed generic distributed information sharing.

# 6. 2025 adversarial evidence materially constrains GNWT

The Cogitate Consortium preregistered divergent IIT/GNWT predictions and tested 256 participants across fMRI, MEG and iEEG.

Key GNWT-relevant results:

```text
- face/object category information appeared in both posterior and some PFC regions;
- orientation information was robust in posterior cortex but mostly absent in PFC;
- the predicted PFC onset+offset ignition profile was generally not observed;
- some long-range connectivity evidence remained compatible with GNWT;
- overall, key GNWT predictions were substantially challenged rather than the entire framework simply refuted.
```

### D9-013
The 2025 study falsifies specific preregistered GNWT biological predictions more strongly than it falsifies the abstract proposition `some form of global availability matters`.

### D9-014
However, theory repair must not simply remove every failed prediction while retaining the label `global workspace` without new discriminative commitments.

---

# 7. 2026 report/no-report evidence further pressures a simple PFC=phenomenality identity

He et al. (2026) combined report and no-report masking with MEG and found:

```text
posterior occipital/temporal representations associated with visibility across both report and no-report conditions
prefrontal representations and long-range fronto-posterior coupling primarily when explicit report was required
```

The authors interpret this as evidence favoring posterior-centered perceptual awareness and a report/access role for PFC.

### D9-015
This is directly compatible with MF9-C's `PhenomenalTarget ≠ ReportPipeline` distinction.

### D9-016
It challenges strong versions in which PFC/global broadcasting is universally necessary for visual phenomenality, but does not eliminate PFC contributions to access, stabilization, report, metacognition or some conscious contents.

# 9. Recurrent Processing Theory — target clarification

Lamme's recurrent-processing route rejects a purely feedforward account of visual consciousness and assigns special status to recurrent/re-entrant interactions within sensory cortex.

Strong RPT-style claim:

```text
FeedforwardSweep
→ unconscious/behaviorally effective processing possible

LocalRecurrentProcessing
→ visual phenomenal awareness/content

Later widespread/frontoparietal processing
→ access, attention, report and cognitive use
```

### D9-019
RPT is therefore more directly a **phenomenal/content theory** than GNW's best-grounded access architecture.

### D9-020
It predicts a possible `ExperienceStanding without broad GlobalAvailabilityStanding` regime.

---

# 10. RPT necessity claim

Strong form:

```text
VisualExperienceStanding(X)
→ RecurrentProcessingStanding(X)
```

Support family:

```text
masking disrupts later recurrent interactions
recurrent sensory activity tracks visibility
feedforward processing can guide behavior without reportable awareness
TMS timing over visual cortex can disrupt awareness
```

Lamme (2006) explicitly proposes recurrent neural processing as central to visual consciousness while resisting behavioral/report gold standards.

### D9-021
RPT necessity is currently best specified for visual/sensory consciousness, not for all possible experience domains.

### D9-022
Generalization from visual recurrence to pain, mood, thought, interoception, dream and minimal experience requires additional evidence.

---

# 11. RPT sufficiency problem

A serious issue:

```text
recurrent processing is common in biological and artificial dynamical systems
```

so simple recurrence cannot be enough.

### D9-023
**Recurrence ≠ phenomenal sufficiency by nomenclature.**

A valid sufficiency theory must specify:

```text
which units/contents participate
which feedback topology
which temporal regime
which causal role
which bearer boundary
which level of recurrence
whether recurrence must alter the represented content
```

### D9-024
`there exists a feedback loop` is too weak to constitute ExperienceStanding.

---

# 12. RPT and no-report evidence

No-report paradigms were partly motivated by the possibility that frontoparietal signatures reflect report/access consequences rather than phenomenality.

Tsuchiya et al. (2015) explicitly argue for separating report-related neural activity from candidate NCCs.

He et al. (2026) observed posterior awareness-related dynamics preserved across report/no-report while prefrontal content coding emerged primarily with report.

### D9-025
These findings are directionally favorable to posterior/recurrent accounts over strong PFC-essential phenomenal accounts.

### D9-026
But no-report evidence still depends on proxy validity and does not directly observe phenomenality; it therefore does not by itself prove RPT sufficiency.

---

# 13. RPT provisional verdict

```text
Strength:
  directly targets phenomenal/content awareness;
  cleanly separates feedforward performance from recurrent awareness;
  gains support from report-confound reductions.

Weak point:
  recurrence criterion risks under-specification;
  visual-domain evidence may not generalize;
  sufficiency remains unproven.
```

### D9-027
**RPT remains a serious phenomenal-constitution rival.**

### D9-028
**Universal `Recurrence ↔ ExperienceStanding` is NOT frozen.**

# 14. Higher-Order theory is a family, not one theory

MF9-D refuses the label `HOT` as a single mechanism.

At least distinguish:

```text
Higher-Order Thought (HOT)
Higher-Order Perception / inner-sense routes
Higher-Order Representation (HOR)
Joint first-order + higher-order determination
Higher-Order State Space (HOSS)
metacognitive inference accounts
reality-monitoring variants
```

Rosenthal's classic HOT route says a first-order mental state is conscious when one has an appropriate higher-order thought representing oneself as being in that state.

Lau & Rosenthal (2011) defend empirical higher-order approaches and emphasize higher-order representations targeting first-order states.

### D9-029
**HigherOrderTheory ≠ Metacognition simpliciter.**

### D9-030
Each variant must specify the higher-order state's representational target, granularity, bearer relation and whether the higher-order state itself must be conscious.

# 15. Higher-order necessity claim

Strong form:

```text
ExperienceStanding(E)
→ suitable HigherOrderStanding(HO,E/B)
```

Potential support:

```text
subjective awareness can dissociate from objective discrimination
prefrontal/metacognitive manipulations alter subjective awareness/confidence
blindsight can be modeled as impaired metacognitive access to first-order signals
```

Rounis et al. (2010) reported that bilateral DLPFC theta-burst stimulation impaired metacognitive visual awareness without changing first-order discrimination.

### D9-031
This is evidence for a causal higher-order/metacognitive contribution to subjective evaluation, but the result is not stable enough to serve as universal constitutive proof.

A later replication/extension by Bor et al. (2017) found no such DLPFC impairment under more stringent analysis.

### D9-032
**The TMS evidence is mixed.**

# 16. Higher-order lesion evidence

Anterior PFC lesions can selectively impair perceptual metacognitive accuracy while leaving first-order perceptual performance comparatively intact.

### D9-033
This strongly supports typed separation:

```text
FirstOrderPerformance
≠ MetacognitiveAccuracy
```

### D9-034
But it does not decide whether phenomenal experience itself disappears or whether only higher-order access/evaluation becomes degraded.

This is exactly the gap between:

```text
Metacognition constitutive of phenomenality
vs
Metacognition required to know/report phenomenality
```

# 17. Empty higher-order representation problem

HOT-type theories must handle cases where:

```text
HigherOrderRepresentation(HO, S)
exists
but target first-order state S is absent or mismatched
```

Rosenthal accepts that a higher-order thought without the represented first-order state can still generate the relevant subjective appearance under the theory.

### D9-035
This creates a distinctive falsifiable commitment rather than a mere defect: phenomenal character can, in some HOT variants, depend more on higher-order representation than on matching first-order state.

### D9-036
MF9-B's `ExperientialPresentation ≠ ExternalReferent` makes this logically coherent, but empirical adequacy remains unresolved.

# 18. HOSS is narrower and therefore methodologically valuable

Fleming's Higher-Order State Space model explicitly characterizes awareness reports as metacognitive inference about a generative model of perceptual content.

Crucially, Fleming states that the model's modest target is **reportable awareness / awareness reports**, leaving non-reportable consciousness aside.

### D9-037
HOSS therefore should not be silently promoted from:

```text
model of awareness reports
```

to:

```text
complete theory of ExperienceStanding
```

without an extra bridge.

### D9-038
This narrower scope is a strength because it prevents explanatory overreach.

# 21. Predictive processing is not one consciousness theory

Predictive processing / predictive coding is a broad family of hierarchical generative/inferential architectures.

It can explain:

```text
perceptual content
prediction error
precision weighting
multisensory inference
body/self inference
agency/presence phenomena
metacognitive awareness reports
```

### D9-043
**PredictiveProcessingStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### D9-044
A system can perform predictive inference without that fact alone specifying which states are conscious.

Therefore a predictive theory of consciousness requires an added consciousness-specific constraint.

# 22. Seth-style predictive presence route

Seth's 2014 sensorimotor-contingency account uses predictive processing to explain **perceptual presence** — why some perceptual contents have a sense of actual presence that imagery/synaesthetic concurrents may lack.

Seth, Suzuki & Critchley's earlier interoceptive-predictive model targets **conscious presence**, proposing that successful suppression/matching of informative interoceptive prediction errors contributes to presence.

### D9-045
These models directly address an MF9-B structure:

```text
ExperientialPresenceStanding
```

rather than automatically explaining all of:

```text
ExperienceOccurrence
PhenomenalUnity
PhenomenalDifferentiation
ConsciousAccess
```

### D9-046
This is a scoped explanatory success, not a complete constitutive theory.

Another recurrent error:

```text

```

### D9-059
**IndicatorStanding ≠ ConstitutiveStanding.**

Ignition, recurrence, PFC decoding, confidence signals, prediction error and connectivity may each be:

# 31. Prefrontal cortex cannot be assigned one consciousness role

Current evidence requires at least:

```text
PFC sensory/content representations
PFC flexible access/control
PFC report generation
PFC metacognitive evaluation
PFC task relevance
PFC some causal contribution to subjective thresholds
```

### D9-060
**PFCStanding is functionally heterogeneous.**

The 2025 Cogitate findings include some task-independent category decoding in PFC but weak/absent orientation decoding and missing predicted offset ignition, while 2026 report/no-report MEG finds anterior content representation primarily during report.

### D9-061
Therefore neither `PFC irrelevant to consciousness` nor `PFC universally constitutes all conscious content` is supported as a simple universal claim.

# 32. Posterior cortex cannot be assigned one consciousness role either

Posterior sensory regions clearly encode rich perceptual content and recurrent dynamics, including under reduced report demands.

But:

### D9-062
**PosteriorContentRepresentation ≠ ExperienceStanding by identity.**

Unconscious sensory representations also exist.

### D9-063
A posterior theory still needs a criterion distinguishing conscious recurrence/content from unconscious posterior processing.

# 41. Evidence anchors

1. **Dehaene, Kerszberg & Changeux (1998), `A neuronal model of a global workspace in effortful cognitive tasks`, PNAS 95, 14529–14534, DOI `10.1073/pnas.95.24.14529`.** Original neuronal global-workspace architecture separating specialized processors from globally connected workspace neurons.
2. **Dehaene & Naccache (2001), `Towards a cognitive neuroscience of consciousness: basic evidence and a workspace framework`, Cognition 79, DOI `10.1016/S0010-0277(00)00123-2`.** Early workspace account connecting conscious processing to durable maintenance, novel combination and intentional behavior.
3. **Dehaene & Changeux (2011), `Experimental and theoretical approaches to conscious processing`, Neuron 70, 200–227, DOI `10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018`.** Canonical GNW conscious-access formulation centered on late amplification, synchronization and frontoparietal ignition/global availability.
4. **Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025), `Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness`, Nature 642, 133–142, DOI `10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1`.** Preregistered multimodal adversarial test; substantially challenges specific GNWT predictions including predicted PFC offset ignition and representation of some conscious dimensions.
5. **He et al. (2026), `Report-modulated prefrontal activity and consistent posterior representations during conscious visual perception`, Consciousness and Cognition 142:104075, DOI `10.1016/j.concog.2026.104075`.** Report/no-report MEG; posterior visibility/content representations across conditions, anterior content/global coupling primarily with explicit report.
6. **Del Cul et al. (2009), `Causal role of prefrontal cortex in the threshold for access to consciousness`, Brain 132, DOI `10.1093/brain/awp111`.** Focal PFC lesions shift visual masking conscious-access thresholds, especially subjective measures.
