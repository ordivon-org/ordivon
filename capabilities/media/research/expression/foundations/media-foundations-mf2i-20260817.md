# 1. Purpose

MF2-I deliberately attacks the provisional MF2 ontology rather than extending it by accumulation. The target is not individual visual phenomena but the five strongest attempted unifications produced by MF2-A→H:

1. resource-constrained distinction allocation;
2. latent-source assignment across space × time × modality;
3. object as persistent integration handle;
4. reality-constrained world-model inference;
5. priority-driven resource allocation.

The attack domains are vision, audition, somatosensation/proprioception, vestibular/body perception, sensory substitution, active sensing, ambiguous/degraded stimuli, occlusion/tracking, attention-awareness dissociations and artificial/robot perception.

The standard is strict: an abstraction survives only if it remains informative after nonvisual and non-object-centric cases are admitted. A formulation that can explain every possible result merely by renaming the hidden resource/source/priority is rejected as vacuous.

# 2. Falsification results

## F1 — Strong latent-source assignment does not survive

### Attack

The provisional synthesis suggested that spatial grouping, temporal correspondence and multisensory causal binding could be unified as latent-source assignment across `Space × Time × Modality`.

### Falsifiers

- Auditory streaming shows that organization into streams is real and can be bistable even when the acoustic input is unchanged; temporal coherence can determine stream formation independently of simple tonotopic separation.
- Ensemble and texture perception show useful perceptual organization at the level of summary statistics without preserving individual source/object identities.
- Visual–vestibular self-motion estimation concerns a body/world relation such as heading, not necessarily partitioning sensory elements into object-like external sources.
- Body ownership and visuo-proprioceptive recalibration concern attribution to body/self state and coordinate relations rather than ordinary external object assignment.

### Verdict

**Strong form rejected.** Source assignment is important but not universal.

### Reconstruction

Replace the universal claim with:

> **Perception performs latent causal/structural organization of observations. Source partition is one important subtype, not the universal form.**

Candidate organization outcomes include:

- source / cause partition;
- object token;
- auditory stream;
- event/process;
- surface/region/material;
- field/texture/ensemble statistics;
- body/self state;
- spatial or temporal relation;
- action-relevant variable.

This preserves the insight that observations must often be organized relationally while removing the object/source bias.

---

## F2 — Object as universal perceptual unit does not survive

### Attack

MF2-E treated a perceptual object as a spatiotemporally organized persistent integration handle over changing observations. This works well for many object-tracking cases, including occlusion.

### Falsifiers

- Multiple-object permanence tracking demonstrates that object maintenance through occlusion is capacity-limited and can collapse even for predictable motion.
- Ensemble perception can retain set statistics while losing individual members.
- Auditory scene organization naturally forms streams/events that need not correspond one-to-one with stable material objects.
- Texture, material, self-motion, vestibular orientation and diffuse body states are perceptually useful without an object token being the primary unit.

### Verdict

**Universal form rejected; typed form retained.**

### Reconstruction

Object continuity becomes one member of a broader persistence family:

> **Persistence is a typed relation that links structured perceptual states across time when continuity is functionally supported.**

Possible persistent handles include object tokens, event trajectories, streams, surfaces, body-part/body-state estimates, environmental fields and relational layouts. Some perceptual states require no individuated persistent handle at all.

`Same object` remains historical/continuity-based rather than appearance equality, but this is now explicitly an **object-specific** theorem rather than the definition of perception.

---

## F3 — Resource-constrained distinction allocation survives only as a boundary condition

### Attack

MF2-B/C/H repeatedly converged on finite resources: sensitivity, neural coding and attention allocate limited resolution, energy, time or processing.

### Falsifier

If every failure can be explained post hoc by an unnamed resource and every success by reallocating it, `resource allocation` becomes tautological. Ensemble coding further shows that reduced focal attention can coexist with precise statistical summaries: reduced local detail does not imply uniformly degraded perceptual structure.

### Verdict

**Strong explanatory form rejected; constrained form retained.**

### Reconstruction

Resource constraints are legitimate only when typed and falsifiable:

- name the constrained quantity: receptor dynamic range, samples, spikes, metabolic energy, time, memory, processing bandwidth, motor opportunity, sensor pose, etc.;
- identify the competing alternatives;
- predict the trade-off or failure signature;
- distinguish local precision loss from alternate compressed/summary representations.

Therefore:

> **Finite resources constrain perception, but perception is not defined as optimization of one universal resource.**

Efficient coding remains a model family, not an ontology mandate.

---

## F4 — Reality-constrained world-model inference needs a normative/epistemic split

### Attack

MF2-G proposed that mature perception combines evidence with learned world structure while preserving Reality's ability to correct the model, and that inferred/prior-generated detail should remain distinct from evidence-grounded detail.

### Falsifiers

- Conditioned-hallucination experiments show that learned priors can produce confident percept-like reports even when the expected auditory signal is absent.
- Artificial neural perception can be confidently wrong under adversarial perturbations that preserve human-perceived identity.
- Generative image restoration can produce plausible detail that is not source-grounded and may not carry an internal explicit marker that separates hallucinated from observed content.

### Verdict

**Core epistemic distinction retained; biological explicit-provenance claim rejected.**

### Reconstruction

Three levels must be separated:

1. **Phenomenon:** perceptual content can be influenced or even dominated by learned/internal structure.
2. **Epistemic audit:** external evidence, preserved evidence, inference and prior-generated content are objectively different causal/provenance classes.
3. **Implementation:** a biological perceptual system is not required to encode explicit provenance metadata for every feature. Whether and how brains represent confidence/source is an empirical implementation question.

`Reality-correctability` is therefore a normative robustness property of world-directed perception and an engineering requirement for reliable artificial perception, not a guarantee that every biological perceptual episode is veridical or successfully corrected.

Percept-like hallucination states are retained as a critical edge case. Whether all hallucinations should count as perception proper or as perceptual experience generated without a matching distal cause is deferred to MF9 Experience; MF2 does not use phenomenology alone to collapse perception into awareness.

---

## F5 — Priority-driven attention survives, but scalar-resource and single-map readings do not

### Attack

MF2-H defined attention as selective allocation/biasing of finite perceptual/cognitive processing and acquisition resources, with priority integrating salience, task relevance, history/value, uncertainty, urgency and action relevance.

### Falsifiers / stress tests

- Blindsight studies demonstrate attentionally enhanced discrimination without reported visual awareness; attention is not sufficient for awareness.
- Ensemble statistics can be represented with reduced focal attention, so attention does not simply gate all useful organization on/off.
- Active sensory substitution and embodied perception show that selection can change sensor motion and future evidence, not merely allocate a central internal pool.
- Distributed sensory/motor systems make a single universal scalar attention budget or one anatomical priority map unnecessary.

### Verdict

**Survives after narrowing.**

### Reconstruction

> **Attention is selective control of perceptual processing and/or information acquisition under competition, constraint or limited opportunity.**

This may alter gain, suppression, timing, routing, evidence accumulation, gaze/head/hand/body orientation, sensor pose or sampling policy. `Resource` remains acceptable only when typed; `priority` is an ordering/control variable, not necessarily one scalar or one neural map.

Attention ≠ salience ≠ priority ≠ gaze ≠ awareness remains frozen.

The necessity of attention for every form of conscious perception remains unresolved; its sufficiency is rejected by attention-without-awareness evidence.

# 3. Cross-domain reconstruction

## 3.1 Vision

Vision confirms that proximal images underdetermine distal states. Color constancy varies with context and task; explicit illuminant estimation is not required for constancy. Crowding demonstrates contextual loss/reorganization of local feature information. Bistable figures demonstrate multiple stable organizations from one physical stimulus. Ensemble statistics demonstrate precise set-level structure despite poor item-level detail.

Therefore visual perception cannot be modeled as a fixed pipeline from local feature extraction → object recognition. Organization may occur at object, set, texture, field, surface or relational levels.

## 3.2 Audition

Auditory streaming is the decisive anti-vision-centric case. Spectrotemporal components can be organized into one or several streams; the same stimulus can alternate between organizations. Temporal coherence may dominate simple spectral separation. Auditory organization therefore supports the general claim `parts + relations + history/context`, but rejects `contour/surface/object` as modality-general primitives.

## 3.3 Somatosensation, proprioception and body perception

The rubber-hand illusion demonstrates that vision, touch and proprioception can jointly alter body ownership/localization. Visuo-proprioceptive conflicts produce recalibration. Body perception is therefore not simply external-object inference with the body treated as another object; it includes self/body-relative estimates, ownership/localization and sensorimotor consistency.

## 3.4 Vestibular / self-motion perception

Visual and vestibular heading cues can be reliability-weighted, but experiments with eccentric gaze show that integration need not require transforming all cues into one common coordinate system before perceptual combination. This falsifies a hidden assumption that `integration = common representation frame`.

MF2 therefore freezes:

> **Cue integration does not imply representational fusion or one shared coordinate frame.**

## 3.5 Sensory substitution

Tactile-vision substitution and later vibrotactile systems demonstrate that physical carrier, receptor pathway, perceptual function and phenomenology cannot be collapsed into one modality label. Active exploration materially changes performance; oscillatory sensor/body movement can increase information pickup.

The correct abstraction is not `touch becomes vision`, but:

> **Perceptual function can be learned over novel sensorimotor contingencies while carrier/receptor/phenomenology remain separable axes.**

## 3.6 Active perception

Active perception survives strongly. Eye/head/hand/body/sensor motion changes the observation operator itself. Robot active-view systems likewise improve recognition or 3-D measurement by moving sensors to resolve occlusion/ambiguity. Perception is therefore not only inference over a fixed input stream; acquisition policy can be part of the perceptual loop.

## 3.7 Attention and awareness

Blindsight and masked-attention studies support a dissociation: selective attention can modulate processing without normal reported awareness. Inattentional-blindness work also shows that failure to report an unexpected stimulus does not by itself localize the point at which processing stopped. The exact depth of unconscious processing depends on paradigm and awareness criterion and remains contested.

MF2 freezes only the robust boundary:

- Attention is not awareness.
- Awareness report is not a transparent readout of all perceptual processing.
- Attention is not sufficient for awareness.
- Whether attention is necessary for every conscious percept remains unresolved and belongs partly to MF9 Experience.

## 3.8 Artificial / robot perception

Artificial systems are useful falsifiers because they remove assumptions about human phenomenology. Three lessons survive:

1. passive benchmark recognition is not equivalent to robust world-directed perception;
2. action/viewpoint selection can improve sensing under occlusion and sensor limitations;
3. learned priors and high task accuracy do not guarantee reality-grounded or adversarially robust distinctions.

Artificial perception therefore belongs inside MF2 only at the functional level: sensing, organization, uncertainty, active acquisition and correction. Consciousness, phenomenology and human modality labels must not be projected onto it.

      system-available               attention / priority

             │                             │

# 5. MF2 Perception Foundations v1 — FROZEN CANONICAL AXIOMS

The provisional PF-01→PF-72 list is superseded by this smaller canonical set. The old list remains historical evidence; numbering below is the frozen v1 namespace.

## A. Ontology and boundary

**PF1-01 — Distal/proximal separation.** Distal world/body state and proximal sensory evidence are not identical.

**PF1-02 — Selective observation.** Sensing/transduction is selective, stateful and condition-dependent.

**PF1-03 — Layer separation.** Perception ≠ signal propagation ≠ recognition ≠ semantic interpretation ≠ conscious awareness.

**PF1-04 — Structured discriminability.** Perception changes a system's structured discriminability among world/body-relative possibilities; it need not construct a complete world replica.

**PF1-05 — Uncertainty.** Ambiguity and uncertainty are intrinsic whenever multiple world/body states are compatible with available evidence.

**PF1-06 — Implementation neutrality.** Bayesian inference, predictive coding, ecological/direct perception, recurrent neural computation and other frameworks are model families, not constitutive ontology.

**PF1-07 — Closed-loop capability.** Perceptual systems may act to change future observations; active sensing is part of perception when acquisition policy is coupled to the perceptual task.

**PF1-08 — Multilayer modality.** Carrier, receptor, pathway, encoded variable, perceptual function and phenomenology are separate axes.

## B. Geometry, coding and organization

**PF1-09 — Typed geometry.** Physical, signal, receptor, neural/technical code, perceptual discrimination, semantic/task and action geometries must not be collapsed.

**PF1-10 — Multiple frames.** Multisensory use does not require all cues to be fused into one common coordinate frame.

**PF1-11 — Finite constraints.** Perception is resource-constrained, but every resource claim must identify the constrained quantity, competition and predicted trade-off; an unnamed universal resource is not explanatory.

**PF1-12 — Typed efficiency.** Efficient coding objectives are typed and conditional; no universal information/energy/sparsity objective defines perception.

**PF1-13 — Relational organization.** Perception organizes relations among distinctions; local features alone do not generally determine global organization.

**PF1-14 — Contextual grouping.** Grouping cues are probabilistic/contextual structural evidence, not absolute Gestalt laws.

**PF1-15 — Non-object organization.** Valid perceptual organizations include objects, events, streams, surfaces, fields, textures, ensembles, body/self states and relations.

**PF1-16 — Summary structure.** Perception may preserve useful aggregate/ensemble structure while discarding or failing to preserve individual item detail.

**PF1-17 — Source assignment is conditional.** Latent source/cause partition is important when the task/phenomenon requires attribution, but is not a universal prerequisite for useful perception.

**PF1-18 — General structural inference.** A broader admissible abstraction is latent causal/structural organization across space, time, modality and action history.

## C. Persistence, integration and body

**PF1-19 — Selective constancy.** Constancy is selective/task-conditional and distinct from invariance, equivariance, correspondence and persistence.

**PF1-20 — No maximum invariance.** Useful perception preserves sensitivity to relevant change while stabilizing nuisance variation; maximum invariance is not generally optimal.

**PF1-21 — Historical identity.** When object-token identity is represented, sameness is a historical/continuity relation rather than appearance equality.

**PF1-22 — Typed persistence.** Persistence can apply to object tokens, events, streams, surfaces, body states or layouts; not every perceptual state requires a persistent token.

**PF1-23 — Latent continuation.** Occluded/unobserved continuation is permitted when supported by dynamics/history, but correspondence can remain uncertain or non-identifiable.

**PF1-24 — Binding/integration/fusion separation.** Binding, cue integration and representational/perceptual fusion are distinct operations/outcomes.

**PF1-25 — Conditional reliability.** Cue reliability weighting is conditional on causal/structural compatibility and task; no modality has universal dominance.

**PF1-26 — Recalibration.** Persistent cross-cue conflict can update unimodal estimates and body/world-relative calibration.

**PF1-27 — Body is not just another object.** Proprioceptive, vestibular and body-ownership phenomena require body/self-relative organization in addition to external-object organization.

## D. Priors, evidence and reality correction

**PF1-28 — Typed top-down roles.** Prior, prediction, expectation, context and top-down signal remain distinct concepts.

**PF1-29 — Learned structure.** Learned history can constrain perceptual organization, especially under ambiguity; its influence is context/reliability dependent without requiring a universal Bayesian implementation.

**PF1-30 — Prior-induced error.** Strong or miscalibrated learned structure can bias perception and can produce percept-like false positives when current evidence is weak or absent.

**PF1-31 — Grounding/content separation.** Perceptual content and evidence-grounding are distinct axes: plausible or vivid content need not be source-grounded.

**PF1-32 — Provenance levels.** Observed/preserved/inferred/prior-generated are objectively different epistemic/provenance classes even if a biological implementation does not explicitly label them.

**PF1-33 — Reality-correctability.** For robust world-directed perception, models must remain in principle correctable by new evidence/actions; this is a normative robustness criterion, not a guarantee of moment-to-moment veridicality.

## E. Attention and active acquisition

**PF1-34 — Attention as selective control.** Attention is selective control of perceptual processing and/or information acquisition under competition, constraint or limited opportunity.

**PF1-35 — Salience/priority/attention separation.** Salience is a stimulus/context-derived contribution; priority integrates multiple selection-relevant factors; attention is the control/selection process.

**PF1-36 — Multi-source priority.** Priority may depend on salience, task relevance, learned value/history, uncertainty/information value, temporal urgency, action relevance and cost; no universal scalar equation or single neural map is asserted.

**PF1-37 — Multi-domain attention.** Attention may be spatial, feature-based, object-based, temporal, cross-modal, event/hypothesis-selective, covert or overt/active.

**PF1-38 — Attention/awareness separation.** Attention is not sufficient for awareness; the necessity of attention for every form of conscious perception remains unresolved.

**PF1-39 — Active acquisition.** Gaze/head/hand/body/sensor movement can be an attentional/perceptual operation because it changes the future observation geometry.

## F. Artificial systems and boundary to Representation

**PF1-40 — Functional portability.** MF2 principles apply to artificial perception only at the functional level—selective sensing, organization, uncertainty, active acquisition and correction—without anthropomorphic assumptions about phenomenology.

**PF1-41 — Robustness beyond benchmark accuracy.** Recognition accuracy alone does not establish perceptual robustness; closed-loop, distribution-shift, ambiguity and adversarial correction tests may expose failures of grounding.

**PF1-42 — MF2/MF3 boundary.** MF2 specifies what perceptual organization must distinguish/do functionally; MF3 asks in what form states stand for, encode, proxy or represent other states/domains and what correctness conditions such representation requires.

# 6. Frozen MF2 synthesis

> **Perception is the stateful, selective and potentially action-coupled organization and use of sensorimotor evidence that changes a system's structured discriminability among world/body-relative possibilities under uncertainty.**
>
> It may organize objects, events, streams, surfaces, fields, textures, ensembles, body/self states or relations; it need not entail semantic recognition or conscious awareness; and no single inferential, representational or neural implementation is constitutive. Learned structure can constrain interpretation, while evidence-grounding remains epistemically distinct from inferred/prior-generated content. In active systems, perceptual control may change future evidence by changing sensing itself.

This formulation is deliberately weaker than `perception = internal reconstruction of the world` and stronger than `perception = signal transduction`. The distinctive commitment is **system-level structured discriminability over world/body-relative possibilities produced by stateful sensorimotor organization**.

# 7. What MF2-I rejected

The following strong claims are now explicitly rejected in addition to prior MF2 rejections:

- all perceptual organization is latent-source assignment;
- every useful percept is fundamentally object-centric;
- persistence always requires an object token;
- cue integration requires one common coordinate frame;
- resource allocation is explanatory without naming a concrete constrained resource/trade-off;
- reduced focal attention means all useful scene structure is lost;
- biological perception must explicitly tag every perceptual feature with provenance metadata;
- reality-correctability implies actual veridicality on every episode;
- artificial recognition benchmark accuracy is sufficient evidence of robust perception;
- active sensing is merely downstream action rather than sometimes part of the perceptual acquisition loop.

# 8. Remaining unresolved questions after freeze

The following are intentionally not resolved by MF2 v1:

1. Whether attention is necessary for every form of conscious perception.
2. The depth and semantic reach of unattended/unaware processing in neurologically intact humans.
3. How biological systems represent confidence, uncertainty or source/provenance, if at all explicitly.
4. Whether hallucinations should ontologically count as perception proper, perceptual experience without matching distal cause, or multiple subclasses; defer to MF9 Experience.
5. Which computational objective best describes efficient sensory coding in each modality/task.
6. The exact neural implementation of crossmodal causal inference, recalibration and priority control.
7. How many coordinate frames are retained in multisensory systems and where transformations occur.
8. Which perceptual organization types deserve representation-level primitives in MF3 rather than remaining functional MF2 categories.

These unresolved items do not block the MF2 freeze because none is required to define the ontology boundary established above.

---

# 9. MF2 → MF3 boundary

MF2 ends when we can describe:

- what distinctions are available to a sensing system;
- how they are reorganized relationally under uncertainty;
- how continuity, body/world attribution and crossmodal structure are maintained;
- how learned history changes discrimination without becoming new evidence;
- how selection and active sensing control acquisition;
- what counts as perceptual failure or ambiguity functionally.

MF3 Representation begins when we ask:

- when does a state **stand for** another state/domain rather than merely covary with or discriminate it?
- what establishes correctness/misrepresentation conditions?
- what is the difference between implicit/distributed representation, explicit variables, symbols, icons, indices, embeddings and generative latent states?
- how do representation content, vehicle, format, geometry, compositionality and decodability differ?
- can perception be nonrepresentational in some mechanisms while still feeding representational systems?
- when does a perceptual organization become an internal model/proxy rather than a directly recruited sensorimotor disposition?

Therefore the next research round is:

**MF3-A — Representation Ontology: Representation ≠ Signal ≠ Perception ≠ Meaning.**
