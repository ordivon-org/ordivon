# 0. Purpose

MF9-A separated experience from access/report/metacognition. MF9-B reconstructed experiential structure. MF9-C now asks:

> **What does it mean for information/content/state to become functionally available to a bearer, and how do access, maintenance, reasoning, action, report, confidence and metacognition relate without being collapsed into phenomenal experience?**

The target is not one `access` bit. It is an availability topology.

Primary attack cases:

```text
Sperling partial report
attentional blink
masking
inattentional blindness
blindsight / relative blindsight
no-report paradigms
working-memory bottlenecks
confidence/metacognition dissociations
artificial systems with broad internal/workspace access
```

MF9-C does not decide whether global access is constitutive for phenomenal experience.

---

# 1. Availability is domain-relative

Introduce the general relation:

```text
AvailabilityStanding(X, B, D | Route, H, Σ)
```

meaning content/state `X` is usable or retrievable by bearer `B` for downstream domain `D` under declared route, horizon and conditions.

`D` can include:

```text
LocalProcessing
FeatureIntegration
WorkingMaintenance
WorkingManipulation
Reasoning
Decision
Planning
ActionSelection
MotorAction
Communication
Report
Metacognition
Learning
LongTermEncoding
CrossModalUse
CrossTaskTransfer
```

### C9-001
**Availability is not one binary primitive.**

### C9-002
`X available for D1` does not imply `X available for D2`.

### C9-003
**AvailabilityStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

```text
GlobalAvailabilityStanding(X,B|D_set,Route,H,Σ)
```

### C9-009
**GlobalAvailabilityStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding by definition.**

# 8. Reportability versus report

MF9-A already separated actual report from reportability. MF9-C decomposes the route.

```text
ReportabilityStanding(X,B | Channel,Conditions,Σ)
```

requires the capacity to transform X or a metacognitive judgment about X into communicative action under declared conditions.

Actual:

```text
ReportStanding(R,B,X | Channel,Σ)
```

requires a token communicative/report action.

### C9-019
**Reportability ≠ Report.**

### C9-020
**Reportability ≠ ConsciousAccess by identity.**

### C9-021
**Report ≠ Experience.**

# 9. Report-generation pipeline

A report should be modeled as a pipeline, not direct mind readout:

```text
Target State/Content X
    ↓
Access / Retrieval
    ↓
Possible Introspective/Metacognitive Estimate
    ↓
Report Decision / Selection
    ↓
Response Mapping / Linguistic or Symbolic Encoding
    ↓
Motor / Communication Execution
    ↓
Observer Interpretation
```

### C9-022
Failure at any later stage does not imply X was absent earlier.

### C9-023
Success at the output stage does not prove the report faithfully mirrors every phenomenal detail.

### C9-024
**ReportContent ≠ TargetExperienceContent by identity.**

# 11. Post-cue recoverability

Sperling's partial-report paradigm forces a crucial distinction.

Introduce:

```text
PostCueRecoverabilityStanding(X,B | Cue,H,Σ)
```

meaning information from a prior presentation can be selectively recovered after a cue delivered within a limited temporal window.

Sperling's 1960 experiments showed that partial-report cues delivered shortly after a brief array can reveal substantially more information than whole report alone, with rapid decay as cue delay increases.

### C9-027
**PostCueRecoverability ≠ ConcurrentWholeReportCapacity.**

### C9-028
**PostCueRecoverability ≠ ConcurrentGlobalAccess by identity.**

### C9-029
**PostCueRecoverability ≠ PhenomenalRichness by identity.**

This prevents a direct leap from high-capacity sensory/iconic availability to phenomenal overflow.

# 12. The overflow dispute is now properly typed

Block's overflow argument uses partial-report/change-detection style evidence to argue that phenomenal consciousness may outrun cognitive access.

Kouider et al.'s partial-awareness rival argues the evidence may instead reflect hierarchical access to partial representations and an illusion of rich phenomenology.

MF9-C preserves both as distinct hypotheses:

```text
Overflow Hypothesis:
  PhenomenalDifferentiation(E) > AccessProfileCapacity(E)

Partial-Awareness Hypothesis:
  Access occurs at selected levels/features while other information remains unconscious or weakly represented;
  apparent richness need not imply inaccessible phenomenal detail.
```

### C9-030
Sperling alone does not decide between these hypotheses.

### C9-031
A valid overflow test must independently estimate phenomenal standing and access standing rather than infer one from the other.

---

Example:


### C9-032
**Partial access ≠ zero access.**

### C9-033

### C9-034
A binary `seen/unseen` measure can hide multidimensional/partial access.

# 14. Threshold versus graded access

Sergent & Dehaene found all-or-none use of a continuous visibility scale in one attentional-blink paradigm, supporting a nonlinear/bifurcation model of conscious access in that context.

Dehaene et al. later formalized a taxonomy of subliminal, preconscious and conscious processing under GNW assumptions.

But partial-awareness results and content/feature-specific access block a universal conclusion that all access dimensions are globally binary.

### C9-035
**Access can exhibit threshold-like transitions along some routes while still having graded/multidimensional profiles overall.**

### C9-036
MF9-C rejects both universal axioms:

```text
AllAccessIsBinary
AllAccessIsGradual
```

### C9-038

### C9-039

# 40. Hard-case audit

## HC-C1 — Sperling whole report versus immediate partial report

More array information is selectively recoverable than can be emitted in whole report.

**Result:** ReportBandwidth ≠ PostCueRecoverability; phenomenal overflow remains unresolved. **PASS.**

## HC-C2 — Delayed partial report

Recoverability decays rapidly.

**Result:** initial availability ≠ maintenance duration. **PASS.**

## HC-C3 — Attentional blink seen/unseen target

Early sensory processing can remain while later access/report diverges.

**Result:** Local/SensoryProcessing ≠ ConsciousAccess. **PASS.**

## HC-C4 — Continuous visibility scale used quasi-categorically

**Result:** threshold-like access can occur in specific paradigms without proving all access/experience is universally binary. **PASS.**

## HC-C5 — Partial awareness

Coarse feature/level accessible while finer identity unavailable.

**Result:** access is level/content specific, not one bit. **PASS.**

## HC-C6 — Inattentional blindness

Unexpected event often not noticed/reported during demanding task.

**Result:** stimulus presence/salience ≠ flexible access/report. Zero phenomenal standing not automatically proven. **PASS.**

## HC-C7 — Blindsight attention cueing

Selective attention improves processing without ordinary awareness.

**Result:** Attention ≠ awareness/access/experience. **PASS.**

## HC-C8 — Matched discrimination, different subjective awareness

**Result:** objective task performance ≠ subjective-awareness report. **PASS.**

## HC-C9 — Paralysis/no motor report route

Internal availability may persist while overt report is unavailable.

**Result:** Reportability channel-specific; report ≠ access. **PASS.**

## HC-C10 — High confidence but wrong

**Result:** Confidence ≠ accuracy; bias ≠ metacognitive sensitivity. **PASS.**

## HC-C11 — Correct low-confidence discrimination

**Result:** first-order performance ≠ metacognitive evaluation. **PASS.**

## HC-C12 — Local unconscious priming

**Result:** LocalAvailability ≠ GlobalAvailability. **PASS.**

## HC-C13 — Rich workspace AI with no first-person report

**Result:** broad functional access can exist independently of verbal report and does not settle experience. **PASS.**

## HC-C14 — AI says `I vividly experience red`

**Result:** report/metacognitive-linguistic output ≠ phenomenal proof. **PASS.**

## HC-C15 — Corporation with organization-wide dashboard

**Result:** collective availability/broadcast ≠ collective phenomenal subject. **PASS.**

# 43. Evidence anchors

1. **George Sperling (1960), `The Information Available in Brief Visual Presentations`, Psychological Monographs 74(11), DOI `10.1037/h0093759`.** Partial-report paradigm: high immediate cue-dependent availability exceeds whole-report output and decays rapidly; does not by itself prove phenomenal overflow.
2. **Ned Block (2007), `Consciousness, accessibility, and the mesh between psychology and neuroscience`, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 30, DOI `10.1017/S0140525X07002786`.** Major phenomenal-overflow argument using accessibility/capacity evidence; preserved as one rival interpretation.
3. **Sid Kouider, Vincent de Gardelle, Jérôme Sackur & Emmanuel Dupoux (2010), `How rich is consciousness? The partial awareness hypothesis`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 14, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2010.04.006`.** Rival hierarchy/partial-access account challenging direct inference from limited report to inaccessible rich phenomenology.
4. **Stanislas Dehaene, Jean-Pierre Changeux, Lionel Naccache, Jérôme Sackur & Claire Sergent (2006), `Conscious, preconscious, and subliminal processing: a testable taxonomy`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 10, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2006.03.007`.** GNW-based distinction among subliminal, preconscious and conscious processing plus vigilance/report access.
5. **Claire Sergent & Stanislas Dehaene (2004), `Is Consciousness a Gradual Phenomenon? Evidence for an All-or-None Bifurcation During the Attentional Blink`, Psychological Science 15, DOI `10.1111/j.0956-7976.2004.00748.x`.** Threshold-like/all-or-none subjective visibility under attentional blink; treated as paradigm-specific evidence rather than universal ontology.
6. **Claire Sergent, Sylvain Baillet & Stanislas Dehaene (2005), `Timing of the brain events underlying access to consciousness during the attentional blink`, Nature Neuroscience 8, DOI `10.1038/nn1549`.** Preserved early sensory responses in unseen trials with later divergence; supports processing/access separation.
7. **Robert W. Kentridge, Charles A. Heywood & Lawrence Weiskrantz (1999), `Attention without awareness in blindsight`, Proceedings of the Royal Society B 266, DOI `10.1098/rspb.1999.0850`.** Spatial attention effects without ordinary visual awareness; hard anti-collapse case.
8. **Hakwan Lau & Richard E. Passingham (2006), `Relative blindsight in normal observers and the neural correlate of visual consciousness`, PNAS 103, DOI `10.1073/pnas.0607716103`.** Matched objective discrimination with differing subjective awareness reports; separates performance from subjective report.
9. **Daniel J. Simons & Christopher F. Chabris (1999), `Gorillas in our midst: sustained inattentional blindness for dynamic events`, Perception 28, DOI `10.1068/p281059`.** Strong evidence that salient unexpected events can fail noticing/report under competing attention; does not alone prove absent phenomenality.
10. **Stephen M. Fleming & Hakwan C. Lau (2014), `How to measure metacognition`, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 8:443, DOI `10.3389/fnhum.2014.00443`.** Separates confidence bias, metacognitive sensitivity and efficiency, motivating typed metacognitive measurement.
11. **Victor A. F. Lamme (2003), `Why visual attention and awareness are different`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 7, DOI `10.1016/S1364-6613(02)00013-X`.** Recurrent-processing rival that separates visual attention and awareness and supplies an architecture distinct from global-access identity.

# 45. MF9-C verdict

The central correction is:

> **`Access` is not one switch and `report` is not its definition.**

The more faithful structure is:

```text
Content / State X
  ├─ local processing availability
  ├─ feature/level-specific availability
  ├─ working maintenance/manipulation
  ├─ reasoning/decision use
  ├─ action use
  ├─ cross-domain/global availability
  ├─ reportability
  ├─ actual report
  └─ metacognitive monitoring/evaluation/control
```

with distinct bottlenecks, bandwidths, persistence windows and transfer routes.

The most important epistemic consequence is:

```text
ReportLimit
cannot directly measure
PhenomenalLimit
```

and likewise:

```text
PostCueRecoverability
cannot directly prove
PhenomenalOverflow
```

Sperling therefore becomes neither a proof of Block nor a proof of GNW/partial awareness. It is a hard constraint that any theory of access/experience must explain.

MF9-C also exposes the proper next confrontation:

```text
GNW:
  broad/global availability and nonlinear ignition

Recurrent Processing:
  local recurrent sensory processing may support awareness before global access

Higher-Order / Metacognitive:
  awareness requires a higher-order relation to first-order state

Predictive/Inferential routes:
  awareness tied to specific inferential/model architectures
```

We now finally possess enough separated ontology to compare these theories without confusing the thing explained with the mechanism proposed.
