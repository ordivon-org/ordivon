# 0. Purpose

MF9-A→F reconstructed experience largely using evidence from:

```text
report-capable adult humans
temporarily non-reporting adult humans
sleeping/anesthetized adults
brain-injured adults
```

A universal Experience foundation cannot freeze while its evidence rules silently assume:

```text
language
human neocortex
human-like motor report
adult cognition
familiar behavior
```

MF9-G therefore asks:

> **When direct adult verbal report is unavailable, what evidence can legitimately shift our credence toward or away from ExperienceStanding across infants, nonhuman animals, radically different nervous systems, and artificial systems?**

The target is not a list of beings declared conscious. The target is a **cross-bearer evidence calculus** that preserves target specificity, alternative explanations, transfer uncertainty and theory dependence.

# 1. The other-minds problem becomes an evidence-transfer problem

For another bearer B, outside B's first-person standpoint:

```text
ExperienceStanding(E,B)
```

is never directly observed in the same way B may undergo it.

Adult human report is powerful because it supplies a calibrated evidence route linking:

```text
stimulus / internal state
→ subjective judgement
→ report
→ behavior/neural measurements
```

But outside report-capable adult humans, the calibration bridge weakens or changes.

Introduce:

```text
CrossBearerExperienceEvidenceStanding(
  Ev,
  B,
  Target |
  Route,
  ValidationDomain,
  TransferBridge,
  Specificity,
  Sensitivity,
  AlternativeModels,
  Independence,
  CausalSupport,
  Scope,
  Uncertainty,
  Σ
)
```

### G9-001
**Evidence diagnosticity is bearer-relative and route-relative.**

### G9-002
The same observable behavior can carry different evidential weight in humans, infants, animals and artificial systems because its generation routes differ.

---

# 2. Same surface behavior ≠ same evidence weight

Example:

```text
Adult human:
"I see the red square"
```

is produced in a system where verbal report has been experimentally calibrated against psychophysics, neural signals and controlled manipulations.

An LLM string:

```text
"I see the red square"
```

may be produced by next-token generation trained directly on human linguistic patterns without any corresponding sensory state.

An animal's trained button press may be produced by stimulus discrimination and reinforcement without metacognitive access.

### G9-003
**SurfaceReportSimilarity ≠ EvidenceRouteSimilarity.**

### G9-004
**Behavioral resemblance must never substitute for a causal account of how the evidence was generated.**

This is the central anti-anthropomorphism and anti-counterfeit rule of MF9-G.

# 7. Infant consciousness: report is unavailable, not experience

Infants provide the cleanest test of report dependence because:

```text

+

```

### G9-012
**LackOfLanguage ≠ LackOfExperience.**

### G9-013
Infant evidence should be treated as a transfer from calibrated adult markers plus age-specific behavioral/neural validation, not as miniature-adult report.

# 8. Kouider et al. infant conscious-access marker

Kouider et al. (Science 2013) presented masked faces at different visibility levels to 5-, 12- and 15-month-old infants.

They found:

```text
late slow cortical response
+
nonlinear visibility-threshold profile
```

across age groups, analogous in functional form to late adult conscious-access signatures, but much slower in younger infants:

```text
~900 ms onset in 5-month-olds
~750 ms in older infants
```

### G9-014
This supports the claim that some neural machinery associated with thresholded perceptual access is present in infancy.

### G9-015
It does **not** establish:

```text
AdultMarker = InfantExperience by identity
```

because the marker itself inherits theory/calibration assumptions from adult consciousness research.

---

# 9. Development changes timing, not merely strength

The infant late response becomes faster and more sustained with age.

### G9-016
**DevelopmentalDifference ≠ EvidenceOfAbsence.**

A marker can be homologous in functional role while differing strongly in latency, amplitude, topology or threshold.

### G9-017
Cross-age evidence must therefore allow developmental transformation rather than requiring adult numerical thresholds.

# 10. Neonatal noxious processing

Neonates cannot verbally report pain, so behavioral and physiological proxies are traditionally used.

Primary EEG/NIRS work shows:

```text
noxious stimulation
→ cortical nociceptive responses
```

and later work shows these cortical responses can dissociate from facial/behavioral pain responses under stress.

### G9-018
**NociceptiveCorticalActivity ≠ PainExperience by definition.**

### G9-019
But cortical noxious processing + context-dependent modulation + behavioral/physiological convergence provides stronger evidence than reflex behavior alone.

### G9-020
The neonatal case demonstrates again:

```text
BehavioralResponseMagnitude
≠ InternalProcessingMagnitude
```

---

# 11. Infant pain evidence and the analgesia test

Slater et al. showed that sucrose could reduce behavioral pain scores without reducing pain-specific brain/spinal activity in newborns.

### G9-021
**Behavioral soothing ≠ demonstrated analgesia.**

This is a powerful cross-bearer lesson:

> An intervention that changes outward behavior without changing target-specific internal processing may alter expression rather than experience.

### G9-022
Any animal/AI evidence framework must therefore distinguish **expression suppression** from **state modification**.

---

# 12. Mammals: metacognition gives a functional bridge, not direct phenomenology

Hampton (PNAS 2001) tested rhesus monkeys in a delayed memory task where they could choose whether to take or decline a memory test before seeing the test options.

Monkeys declined more when memory was weak and performed better on self-selected than forced tests; probe conditions reduced simple external-cue explanations.

### G9-023
This is strong evidence for a functional capacity to monitor memory availability.

### G9-024
Hampton explicitly distinguished this from direct documentation of subjective conscious memory.

Therefore:

```text
FunctionalMetamemoryStanding
≠ ExperienceStanding
```

but it is a substantial positive indicator for higher-order access architecture.

---

# 13. Rat metacognition and alternative-model burden

Foote & Crystal (2007) reported uncertainty-monitoring behavior in rats using a duration-discrimination task with an option to decline difficult trials.

### G9-025
This expands metacognitive-like evidence beyond primates.

However later methodological work showed that uncertainty responses can sometimes be reproduced by lower-level reinforcement/aversion models.

### G9-026
**Metacognition evidence quality depends on eliminating associative alternatives, not on the label `uncertainty response`.**

### G9-027
Animal metacognition therefore belongs in an `AlternativeExplanationBurden` framework rather than a yes/no species list.

---

# 14. Birds break strict mammalian-cortex necessity

Nieder, Wagener & Rinnert (Science 2020) recorded single neurons in crow pallial endbrain during near-threshold visual detection.

They reported a two-stage neural pattern:

```text
early activity
→ physical stimulus intensity

later activity
→ crow's reported percept of presence/absence
```

with late activity predicting subjective choice beyond physical stimulus intensity.

### G9-028
This is unusually strong cross-bearer sensory-awareness evidence because it combines:

```text
near-threshold ambiguity
trial-by-trial perceptual report
stimulus/report dissociation
single-neuron dynamics
```

### G9-029
It undermines a strict claim that **laminated mammalian cerebral cortex is necessary for sensory-consciousness-like processing**.

### G9-030
It does not prove that every bird species or every crow state is phenomenally conscious.

Bird pallium is organized differently from mammalian neocortex.

### G9-031

```text

```

and:

```text
SameFunctionalRole

```

Both anti-anthropomorphic and anti-functionalist extremes are blocked.

# 16. 2023 crow presence/absence representation strengthens target specificity

Later crow work identified distinct neuron populations representing the subjective percepts `stimulus present` and `stimulus absent`, dissociated from motor-planning requirements.

### G9-032
This increases evidence that crow late activity is tied to perceptual state rather than merely response execution.

### G9-033
Still, `subjective percept` here is behaviorally inferred from a trained task; it is not verbal introspective testimony.

# 17. Cephalopods: pain evidence without vertebrate architecture

Crook (iScience 2021) studied octopuses after acetic-acid injury.

Evidence included:

```text
conditioned place avoidance of injury-associated context
conditioned preference for a context paired with local pain relief
lasting site-specific grooming
central processing of location-specific noxious input
abolition/modulation by local anesthetic
```

### G9-034
This is substantially stronger than simple nocifensive withdrawal because it implicates persistent internal state, learning, valuation and analgesic relief.

### G9-035
The best interpretation is strong positive evidence for an affective pain-like state in octopus, while preserving inferential uncertainty about phenomenality.

---

# 18. Analgesic relief is especially diagnostic

A simple reflex system can avoid a harmful stimulus.

A more demanding pattern is:

```text
injury
→ persistent altered state
→ learned avoidance of context associated with that state
→ preference for context associated with relief
```

### G9-036
**ReliefPreferenceStanding is more diagnostic of negative affect than immediate withdrawal alone.**

### G9-037
This cross-species assay is valuable because it targets the valenced internal state rather than merely nociceptive detection.

# 19. Distributed nervous systems defeat centralization assumptions

Octopus nervous systems distribute substantial sensorimotor processing into the arms/periphery while retaining central integration.

### G9-038
**Human-like centralization ≠ necessary condition for candidate experience.**

### G9-039
But distributed control creates a new bearer-boundary question:


MF9-G does not solve that subject-individuation problem; it records it for global falsification.

# 20. Insects: simple nociception is not enough

Insects clearly possess nociceptive systems.

MF9-G rejects:

```text

→ PainExperience
```

as invalid.

The relevant question is whether noxious responses are integrated with flexible valuation, context, learning and internal-state modulation.

# 21. Bumblebee motivational trade-offs

Gibbons et al. (PNAS 2022) gave bumblebees choices between differently rewarded feeders and noxiously heated feeders.

Bees:

```text
avoided noxious heat when alternatives were valuable
but increasingly accepted heat as alternative reward value fell
```

and used learned color cues in the trade-off.

### G9-040
This demonstrates context-sensitive central modulation of nociceptive behavior rather than a fixed withdrawal reflex.

### G9-041
It is **consistent with** pain capacity but does not alone prove pain phenomenality.

The paper itself appropriately uses this cautious formulation.

---

# 22. Insect affect-like state evidence

Bumblebee experiments have also reported dopamine-dependent positive judgment-bias changes after unexpected reward, and 2025 work reported positive affective contagion-like effects between bees.

### G9-042
These findings expand evidence for persistent valence-sensitive internal-state modulation in insects.

### G9-043
`EmotionLikeStateStanding` remains distinct from `ExperiencedValenceStanding`.

Affective-like behavior is a positive indicator, not an identity proof.

# 37. Stronger artificial evidence profile

If future AI evidence is to become persuasive, MF9-G expects convergence such as:

```text
1. architecture matches multiple independent theory-derived indicators;
2. indicators are mechanistically verified, not inferred from output;
3. causal ablations produce predicted conscious/unconscious-like dissociations;
4. internal state reports track hidden internal variables not trivially available from prompt/output;
5. behavior generalizes spontaneously across contexts;
6. training-data imitation explanations are controlled;
7. state transitions show reproducible internal reorganization;
8. valence-like states affect flexible long-horizon choice and can be relieved by targeted interventions;
9. competing non-phenomenal models are explicitly tested;
10. physical-substrate claims are analyzed when theories such as IIT are included.
```

### G9-066
Even this would constitute convergent evidence, not first-person proof accessible to the external observer.

# 50. Evolutionary convergence is evidence of possibility

# 64. Evidence anchors

1. **Kouider et al. (2013), `A neural marker of perceptual consciousness in infants`, Science 340:376–380, DOI `10.1126/science.1232509`.** Late nonlinear masked-face response in 5-, 12- and 15-month-olds, delayed relative to adults.
2. **Slater et al. (2010), `Oral sucrose as an analgesic drug for procedural pain in newborn infants: a randomised controlled trial`, Lancet 376:1225–1232, DOI `10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61303-7`.** Behavioral pain reduction without corresponding reduction in pain-specific brain/spinal activity.
3. **Jones et al. (2017), `Nociceptive Cortical Activity Is Dissociated from Nociceptive Behavior in Newborn Human Infants under Stress`, Current Biology 27:3846–3851.e3, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2017.10.063`, PMID `29199079`.** Stress-related dissociation between cortical nociceptive response and behavior.
4. **Hampton (2001), `Rhesus monkeys know when they remember`, PNAS 98:5359–5362, DOI `10.1073/pnas.071600998`.** Prospective decline-test metamemory evidence with explicit caution about subjective properties.
5. **Foote & Crystal (2007), `Metacognition in the rat`, Current Biology 17:551–555, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2007.01.061`.** Rat uncertainty-monitoring paradigm.
6. **Smith, Shields & colleagues methodological critiques / Smith (2008), `The comparative study of metacognition: sharper paradigms, safer inferences`, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 15:679–691, DOI `10.3758/PBR.15.4.679`.** Alternative reinforcement/associative explanations for animal uncertainty responses.
7. **Nieder, Wagener & Rinnert (2020), `A neural correlate of sensory consciousness in a corvid bird`, Science 369:1626–1629, DOI `10.1126/science.abb1447`.** Crow pallial single-neuron activity dissociating physical stimulus intensity from later perceptual report.
8. **Nieder et al. (2023/2024), `Conscious Experience of Stimulus Presence and Absence Is Actively Encoded by Neurons in the Crow Brain`, PMID `38165732`.** Distinct crow neuron populations tracking subjective present/absent percepts separately from motor planning.
9. **Crook (2021), `Behavioral and neurophysiological evidence suggests affective pain experience in octopus`, iScience 24:102229, DOI `10.1016/j.isci.2021.102229`.** Conditioned place avoidance, analgesic place preference, persistent grooming and central noxious processing.
10. **Gibbons et al. (2022), `Motivational trade-offs and modulation of nociception in bumblebees`, PNAS 119:e2205821119, DOI `10.1073/pnas.2205821119`.** Learned context-dependent heat/reward trade-offs consistent with centralized modulation and pain capacity.
11. **Solvi/Perry et al. (2016), `Unexpected rewards induce dopamine-dependent positive emotion-like state changes in bumblebees`, Science 353:1529–1531, DOI `10.1126/science.aaf4454`.** Positive judgement-bias and dopamine-sensitive affect-like state evidence.
12. **Bumblebee affective contagion study (2025), `Positive affective contagion in bumble bees`, Science, DOI `10.1126/science.adr0216`, PMID `41129631`.** Positive judgment-bias transfer after interaction with positively conditioned conspecifics.
13. **New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness (2024).** Expert synthesis: strong scientific support for consciousness attribution to mammals/birds and realistic possibility in all vertebrates plus several invertebrate groups; treated here as synthesis, not primary proof.
14. **Butlin et al. (2025; TiCS issue 2026), `Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 30:488–501, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011`.** Theory-derived indicator method; indicators as credence-shifting; theory plurality; positive/negative indicators; minimal implementation problem.
15. **Butlin et al. (2023), `Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness`, arXiv:2308.08708.** Earlier multi-theory indicator-property framework for artificial systems; retained as historical precursor to the peer-reviewed 2025 paper.
