# 0. Purpose

MF6-A separated temporal standing from coordinates, clocks, timestamps and generic sequence. MF6-B separated order, interval, duration and clock realization. MF6-C showed that relativistic time is typed into invariant causal order, reference-relative coordinate time/simultaneity and worldline-local proper time.

MF6-D asks a different question:

> **How does an organism/perceiver/agent organize temporal evidence into perceived order, simultaneity, duration, anticipation, biological phase and action timing—and which of those structures are actually the same thing?**

The strongest danger now is psychological/biological collapse:

```text
Clock Time = Perceived Time
Physical Simultaneity = Perceptual Simultaneity
Cannot Judge Order = Perceived Simultaneous
One PSS = One Perceptual Temporal Origin
Temporal Binding Window = Physical Coincidence Window
Perceived Duration = Clock Duration
Duration Illusion = Global Subjective Time Speed
One Internal Clock = All Timing Phenomena
Prospective Duration = Retrospective Duration
Attention = Internal Clock Rate
Memory Density = Perceptual Duration
Circadian Clock = Interval Timer
SCN = All Biological Time
Circadian Phase = Time
Oscillation = Time
Entrainment = Synchronization to Physical Time by identity
Neural Time Code = One Internal Clock
Decodable Elapsed Time = Explicit Temporal Representation
Action Timing = Passive Perceptual Timing
Intentional Binding = Agency by identity
Prediction Horizon = Future Itself
Subjective Present = Relativistic Global Present
```

MF6-D keeps the Media Foundations discipline:

```text
Phenomenon ≠ Computational Model ≠ Neural Implementation
```

and asks explicitly whether temporal findings force MF2 Perception Foundations to reopen.

# 1. Perceptual time is not physical/clock time

An observer can systematically misjudge duration, order or simultaneity while the physical timing of stimuli remains fixed.

### TD-001
**Physical/Clock Temporal Relation ≠ Perceptual Temporal Relation.**

### TD-002
Perceptual temporal claims are evidence-/system-relative organizations of sensory/sensorimotor information, not direct copies of timestamps.

### TD-003
Physical temporal fidelity is therefore a profile of perceptual performance, not a constitutive definition of perception.

---

# 2. Temporal order perception has finite discrimination thresholds

Human temporal-order judgments (TOJs) require sufficient onset asynchrony; performance varies with modality/task/training.

### TD-004
**Physical order can exist below reliable perceptual-order discriminability.**

### TD-005
`A physically before B` does not entail `observer can report A before B`.

### TD-006
Temporal order discrimination is graded/probabilistic rather than a perfect pointwise readout of physical event order.

---

# 3. Modality matters

Primary TOJ studies show auditory, visual and crossmodal judgments can differ in threshold/transfer characteristics; multisensory TOJs generally require comparing modality-specific timing estimates rather than reading one identical sensory timestamp channel.

### TD-007
**AuditoryTemporalDiscriminability ≠ VisualTemporalDiscriminability ≠ CrossmodalTemporalDiscriminability.**

### TD-008
Input modality can alter temporal precision/bias without changing target physical order.

### TD-009
No universal modality-independent perceptual latency scalar is frozen.

---

# 4. Temporal-order learning can be modality/task specific

Training studies show TOJ precision can improve while transfer is restricted/asymmetric across modalities and tasks.

### TD-010
**Temporal perceptual learning does not imply one central timing parameter was globally improved.**

### TD-011
Task/modality-specific learning is positive evidence for multiple or distributed timing transformations/decision rules.

### TD-012
This does not prove complete neural independence; it only rejects compulsory one-channel identity.

---

# 5. Simultaneity Judgment and Temporal Order Judgment are not interchangeable

Experiments using identical visual/audiovisual stimulation find different reaction-time patterns, PSS estimates, precision and training-transfer behavior for simultaneity judgments (SJ) versus temporal-order judgments (TOJ).

### TD-013
**SimultaneityJudgment ≠ TemporalOrderJudgment.**

### TD-014
Failure to discriminate order does not logically entail an experience/judgment of simultaneity.

### TD-015
A TOJ `50% A-first / 50% B-first` point can reflect order uncertainty rather than perceptual simultaneity.

### TD-016
`TOJ-PSS ≠ SJ-PSS` by universal identity.

---

# 6. Point of subjective simultaneity is task/model relative

`PSS` is an inferred psychophysical parameter from a declared response model/task.

### TD-017
**PSS ≠ physical simultaneity.**

### TD-018
**PSS ≠ one latent universal perceptual clock offset.**

### TD-019
PSS claims require modality pair, task, stimulus class, context, fitting model, uncertainty and adaptation history.

---

# 7. Perceptual simultaneity can tolerate physical asynchrony

Audiovisual events can be judged/bound as simultaneous over nonzero ranges of stimulus onset asynchrony.

### TD-020
**PerceptualSimultaneity ≠ PhysicalZeroAsynchrony.**

### TD-021
A temporal binding window is a psychophysical/integration profile, not the physical width of an event in spacetime.

### TD-022
Temporal binding can be asymmetric for audition-leading versus vision-leading input.

---

# 8. There is not necessarily one temporal binding window

Behavioral training experiments show auditory-leading and visual-leading audiovisual binding windows can differ in width/trainability and can show limited crossover.

### TD-023
**TemporalBindingWindow is modality-order/task/profile relative.**

### TD-024
`AVBindingWindow(A-leading) ≠ AVBindingWindow(V-leading)` can be empirically meaningful.

### TD-025
One scalar `the human temporal binding window` is therefore usually under-specified.

---

# 9. Temporal binding is not causal identity

Two sensory events inside an integration window may be grouped as one event even when physical generation relations are uncertain; two signals outside a window may arise from one physical source under propagation/processing delays.

### TD-026
**TemporalBinding ≠ PhysicalCommonCause by identity.**

### TD-027
Binding is an inference/perceptual organization using temporal evidence among other cues.

### TD-028
Temporal proximity is evidence for common-event organization, not proof.

---

# 10. Perceptual simultaneity recalibrates with recent history

Audiovisual adaptation studies show the PSS shifts toward recently experienced asynchrony.

### TD-029
**Perceptual temporal alignment is stateful/history-dependent.**

### TD-030
A fixed physical audiovisual relation can receive different simultaneity judgments after different adaptation histories.

### TD-031
Recent temporal statistics can recalibrate perceptual relation standing without changing physical event times.

---

# 11. Temporal recalibration can be rapid

Single-trial studies show PSS on the current trial can depend on the modality order/asynchrony of the preceding audiovisual trial.

### TD-032
**Temporal calibration need not require slow long-term adaptation.**

### TD-033
Perceptual temporal state can update on very short experience timescales.

### TD-034
The update can be asymmetric across stimulus order/modality, again opposing a single universal scalar correction.

---

# 12. Perceptual timing can track physical rather than reported order in recalibration

Rapid recalibration findings can depend on the physical order of the preceding inputs even when perceptual report differs.

### TD-035
**Perceptual report ≠ all internal evidence used for subsequent temporal calibration.**

### TD-036
Conscious judgment is therefore not automatically the only state variable in perceptual adaptation.

---

# 13. Sensorimotor time is action-coupled

Stetson et al. injected a fixed action→visual delay; after adaptation, physically post-action flashes at short delays were sometimes perceived as having occurred before the action.

### TD-037
**Action-Sensation temporal order is dynamically calibratable.**

### TD-038
A stable physical causal order can be perceptually reversed after sensorimotor delay adaptation.

### TD-039
`PerceivedActionBeforeEffect ≠ PhysicalActionBeforeEffect` universally.

---

# 14. Sensorimotor recalibration can transfer across sensory modalities

Heron et al. found temporal recalibration for visual, auditory and tactile sensory consequences and crossmodal transfer across adapt/test sensory modalities.

### TD-040
**Some sensorimotor temporal calibration has supramodal structure.**

### TD-041
This coexists with modality-specific perceptual timing effects; `supramodal component` does not imply all temporal processing is supramodal.

### TD-042
MF6 therefore supports layered timing mechanisms rather than either pure central-clock or pure modality-isolation extremes.

---

# 15. Sensorimotor recalibration depends on causal plausibility/history

The magnitude of action-effect recalibration falls for very large imposed delays, consistent with constraints on treating a sensory event as the action's likely consequence.

### TD-043
**Temporal calibration is coupled to causal/action priors, not only raw timestamp difference.**

### TD-044
Perceptual temporal organization can therefore depend on the inferred relation between events.

---

# 16. Voluntary action can change temporal sensitivity

Experiments comparing voluntary versus passive somatosensory events report better auditory-somatosensory TOJ precision under voluntary movement in the tested paradigm.

### TD-045
**ActionContext can change temporal discriminability.**

### TD-046
Action timing is not simply passive timing with an added motor label.

### TD-047
Sensorimotor predictions/efference-related processes are candidate contributors but should remain computational/implementation hypotheses rather than ontology.

---

# 17. Action-outcome learning can restructure perceived temporal order

When learned action→outcome associations are active and causally believed, predicted/congruent sensory outcomes can be biased toward the expected action-before-effect ordering.

### TD-048
**Causal/ActionPrediction can influence perceived temporal order.**

### TD-049
Temporal-order perception is therefore not independent of representation/agency priors.

### TD-050
This does not permit rewriting physical causal order; it is a perceptual/action profile.

---

# 18. Intentional binding is a phenomenon, not an ontology primitive

Haggard et al. found perceived times of voluntary actions and their sensory effects shifted toward one another relative to comparison conditions.

### TD-051
**IntentionalBindingPhenomenon ≠ Agency by identity.**

### TD-052
The existence/magnitude of binding is task/model dependent and should not serve as a universal scalar `sense of agency` measure in foundations.

### TD-053
Action-effect temporal attraction is one possible experiential signature among many, not the definition of volition.

---

# 19. Action timing and perceptual timing may dissociate

Studies can find different effects of multisensory timing manipulations on perceptual judgments versus motor/action outcomes.

### TD-054
**PerceptualTemporalProfile ≠ ActionTemporalProfile.**

### TD-055
A timing relation useful for control can differ from a timing relation reported explicitly.

This mirrors MF5-E/F:

```text
PerceptualSpace ≠ ActionSpace
```

as:

```text
PerceptualTime ≠ ActionTime
```

---

# 20. Prediction horizon is not future time itself

An agent may represent that an event is expected after delay `Δ`, before deadline `D`, or within a control horizon `H`.

### TD-056
**PredictionHorizon ≠ Future.**

### TD-057
ExpectedTime ≠ EventTime until evidence/realization establishes occurrence.

### TD-058
Temporal expectation is a representation/action structure over possible futures, not the target future event itself.

---

# 21. Anticipation can alter attention/action before the event

Temporal expectation can redistribute preparation/attention and improve or bias responses around predicted event times.

### TD-059
**TemporalPrediction can have present action/perceptual consequences without making predicted events actual.**

### TD-060
Anticipatory state belongs to agent/system state; MF7/MF8 will model transitions/agency further.

---

# 22. Perceived duration is not clock duration

Equal physical durations can be judged differently depending on stimulus/context/history.

### TD-061
**PerceivedDuration ≠ Physical/ClockDuration.**

### TD-062
Duration judgment has bias, variability and task/context profiles analogous to spatial perceptual distortion.

### TD-063
`DurationError` must specify physical/reference measure and perceptual task.

---

# 23. Oddball duration hard case rejects one global subjective time speed

Oddball/novel stimuli can be judged longer than repeated standards even at equal physical duration.

However experiments testing multiple temporal judgments show duration distortion need not co-occur with corresponding changes in perceived flicker frequency or auditory pitch.

### TD-064
**DurationDistortion ≠ GlobalSubjectiveTimeSpeedChange by necessity.**

### TD-065
A local duration judgment can change while other temporal judgments remain approximately unchanged.

### TD-066
Language such as `time slowed down` is therefore phenomenological shorthand unless a broader set of temporal observables shifts coherently.

---

# 24. Low-level stimulus properties can alter judged duration

Visual spatial-frequency manipulations can systematically change perceived duration even when physical duration is fixed.

### TD-067
**Perceived duration depends partly on stimulus-processing properties, not only a central elapsed-time counter.**

### TD-068
One duration illusion should not automatically be generalized into global temporal-rate change.

---

# 25. Attention influences duration judgments but is not Time

Prospective timing experiments show task load/attention allocation can alter duration estimates.

### TD-069
**AttentionToTime ≠ Time.**

### TD-070
Attention can modulate access/weighting of temporal evidence or timing computation without constituting physical temporal standing.

### TD-071
An attentional explanation is a computational hypothesis for some duration tasks, not a universal definition of subjective time.

---

# 26. Prospective and retrospective duration judgments differ

In prospective timing, participants know during the interval that duration will be judged; in retrospective timing they learn afterward.

Primary experiments show these paradigms can respond differently to processing load/context, supporting different information sources/processes.

### TD-072
**ProspectiveDurationJudgment ≠ RetrospectiveDurationJudgment.**

### TD-073
Prospective judgments can rely more on online temporal attention; retrospective judgments can rely more heavily on memory/contextual changes.

### TD-074
One `subjective duration` variable is therefore too coarse without judgment paradigm.

---

# 27. Experienced duration and remembered duration are distinct

### TD-075
**OnlineExperiencedDuration ≠ RetrospectivelyRememberedDuration by identity.**

### TD-076
A later memory-based report can change as memory representation/compression/context changes without implying the original perceptual experience changed identically.

### TD-077
Temporal phenomenology, temporal memory and explicit duration report must remain typed.

---

# 28. Information richness does not imply a universal duration law

More processed/contextual information can lengthen retrospective duration in some paradigms, while task load can shorten prospective duration in others.

### TD-078
**InformationAmount ≠ PerceivedDuration through one universal monotonic mapping.**

### TD-079
The effect depends on task, encoding, attention, memory and timescale.

---

# 29. Temporal report method matters

Duration reproduction, production, comparison, bisection and verbal estimation impose different memory/action/decision demands.

### TD-080
**DurationJudgmentTask ≠ DurationPhenomenon.**

### TD-081
A model fitting one response task should not be elevated to universal temporal perception without cross-task validation.

---

# 30. Temporal precision can scale with interval without proving one scalar pacemaker

Behavior often shows increasing variability with longer timed intervals in specific tasks, motivating scalar-timing models.

### TD-082
**ScalarVariabilityPattern ≠ ProofOfSinglePacemaker.**

Multiple population/state-space mechanisms can produce scale-dependent timing variability.

### TD-083
Computational equivalence at behavioral level does not identify implementation.

---

# 31. One internal clock is not required by current evidence

The combined evidence of task-specific SJ/TOJ mechanisms, modality-specific learning, duration-specific distortions, action-dependent recalibration and distributed neural timing is inconsistent with requiring one universal clock variable for all perceptual temporal phenomena.

### TD-084
**OneUniversalInternalClock is rejected as a constitutive necessity.**

### TD-085
This does not prove that pacemaker/accumulator mechanisms are never useful computational models for particular tasks.

### TD-086
`UsefulClockModel ≠ UniversalTemporalOntology`.

---

# 32. Neural elapsed-time decodability is distributed

Primary recordings show time-varying neural population activity in striatum and cortex can encode elapsed time during behavior.

### TD-087
**ElapsedTimeDecodableFromNeuralDynamics ≠ OneCentralNeuralClock.**

### TD-088
Multiple regions can carry temporal information with different decoding quality/task relationships.

### TD-089
Decodability also does not by itself prove explicit temporal representation; it must be linked to behavior/causal intervention/consumer use.

This inherits MF3/MF5-H:

```text
Decodability ≠ Representation
```

---

# 33. Striatal dynamics can predict duration judgments

Gouvêa et al. found striatal population dynamics contained elapsed-time information sufficient to predict rats' duration categorizations, and striatal inactivation impaired duration sensitivity in that task.

### TD-090
**Some neural dynamics participate causally in interval judgments.**

### TD-091
This supports neural implementation of timing but does not make striatum `the Time organ`.

### TD-092
`NecessaryForTaskPerformance ≠ UniversalTemporalGenerator`.

---

# 34. Dopamine can causally modulate interval judgments

Soares et al. measured/manipulated midbrain dopamine neurons while mice judged interval duration; transient activation/inhibition shifted timing judgments and suppression reduced behavioral temporal sensitivity.

### TD-093
**NeuromodulatoryState can causally alter temporal judgment.**

### TD-094
A change in judgment bias/sensitivity after dopamine manipulation does not imply dopamine concentration literally is Time or a universal clock rate.

### TD-095
Phenomenon, computational interpretation and neural mechanism remain separate.

---

# 35. Ongoing behavior can correlate with duration judgments

Rodents performing timing tasks can develop stereotyped behavioral sequences whose trial-to-trial variation predicts duration reports.

### TD-096
**Timing behavior and inferred internal temporal state can be entangled.**

### TD-097
Neural time codes must be tested against movement/behavioral-state confounds rather than assumed independent.

### TD-098
Embodied state sequences are candidate timing resources, not automatic proof that behavior itself is the complete timer.

---

# 36. Temporal cognition can be implemented through changing state, not explicit ticking

Any reproducible evolving system state can, in principle, support elapsed-time discrimination over a range.

### TD-099
**Temporal computation need not require an explicit pulse counter.**

### TD-100
State-space/population timing can be legitimate without becoming physical Time itself.

This prepares MF6-E computational time.

# 37. Biological timing is not one mechanism

Organisms exhibit timing over milliseconds, seconds/minutes, hours and circadian periods, with partially distinct mechanisms and interactions.

### TD-101
**BiologicalTiming ≠ OneBiologicalClock.**

### TD-102
Different timescales can be supported by different circuits/molecular mechanisms while interacting.

---

# 38. Circadian timing and interval timing dissociate

CLOCK-mutant mice with impaired circadian molecular machinery showed no reliable deficit in accuracy/precision for 10 s and 20 s peak-interval timing in the tested procedure.

### TD-103
**Circadian CLOCK protein is not necessary for normal second-scale interval timing in that task.**

### TD-104
This is a direct hard case against `CircadianClock = IntervalTimer` by identity.

### TD-105
It does not prove complete independence of all circadian/interval mechanisms or rule out modulation/interactions.

# 39. Circadian timing itself is distributed/multi-oscillatory

SCN research shows multiple autonomous cellular oscillators require coupling to generate coherent tissue output; forced desynchronization can split SCN components/outputs.

### TD-106
**CircadianSystem ≠ OneOscillator.**

### TD-107
A coherent behavioral circadian rhythm can be an emergent population/system property rather than one scalar clock state.

---

# 40. SCN is not all circadian timing

Food-anticipatory rhythmic behavior can persist in animals with SCN lesions under restricted feeding paradigms, supporting additional food-entrainable timing mechanisms.

### TD-108
**SCN ≠ AllCircadianTemporalStanding.**

### TD-109
The mammalian circadian system contains multiple pacemaker/oscillator routes and outputs.

### TD-110
`MasterClock` is functional shorthand for a dominant coordinating role in particular rhythms, not literal monopoly over biological temporal organization.

**CircadianPhase ≠ Time.**

# 42. Periodic rhythm does not prove a universal temporal coordinate

An organism may contain oscillators with distinct intrinsic periods/phases and coupling relations.

### TD-114
**Oscillation ≠ UniversalTimeCoordinate.**

### TD-115
Biological clocks are physical dynamical systems used to regulate/predict periodic conditions, not Time itself.

# 43. Entrainment is phase/rate coordination, not time identity

### TD-116
**Entrainment ≠ TargetTimeIdentity.**

### TD-117

### TD-119
**Jet-lag-like re-entrainment is a multi-component state transition, not one clock scalar instantly changing zone.**

### TD-120
Internal biological temporal coordination has latency/history and component-specific dynamics.

# 45. Circadian state can modulate interval timing without constituting it

# 76. Primary evidence anchors

- **Stetson, Cui, Montague & Eagleman (2006), `Motor-Sensory Recalibration Leads to an Illusory Reversal of Action and Sensation`, Neuron 51:651–659.** Injected action-feedback delays shift action/flash temporal-order judgments and can cause physically post-action flashes to be perceived as preceding the action.
- **Heron, Hanson & Whitaker (2009), `Effect before Cause: Supramodal Recalibration of Sensorimotor Timing`, PLOS ONE 4:e7681.** Action-effect temporal recalibration occurs for visual, auditory and tactile feedback and transfers across sensory modalities.
- **Vroomen et al. (2004), `Recalibration of temporal order perception by exposure to audio-visual asynchrony`.** Exposure to AV lag shifts subjective simultaneity/order estimates toward the adapted relation.
- **Van der Burg et al. / rapid-recalibration experiments (2013 onward).** Current audiovisual simultaneity judgments can depend on the physical modality order/asynchrony of the immediately preceding trial.
- **Love et al. (2013), `A Psychophysical Investigation of Differences between Synchrony and Temporal Order Judgments`; Matthews et al. (2016), `Simultaneity and Temporal Order Judgments Exhibit Distinct Reaction Times and Training Effects`.** SJ and TOJ can produce different PSS/precision/RT/training-transfer profiles under closely matched or identical stimulation.
- **Haggard, Clark & Kalogeras (2002), `Voluntary action and conscious awareness`, Nature Neuroscience 5:382–385.** Perceived action/effect times shift toward one another for voluntary actions in the tested paradigm; foundations retain this as phenomenon rather than agency definition.
- **Zakay (1993), `Relative and absolute duration judgments under prospective and retrospective paradigms`.** Prospective versus retrospective duration reports depend differently on online temporal processing versus remembered contextual/event information.
- **Pariyadath & Eagleman (2007), `The Effect of Predictability on Subjective Duration`.** Oddball duration distortion does not entail corresponding distortions in other temporal observables such as pitch/flicker, undermining literal global `subjective time expansion` readings.
- **Aaen-Stockdale et al. (2011), `Perceived time is spatial frequency dependent`.** Equal-duration visual stimuli can receive systematic duration biases as a function of low-level spatial-frequency content.
- **Gouvêa et al. (2015), `Striatal dynamics explain duration judgments`.** Striatal population dynamics predict duration categorization and striatal inactivation impairs duration sensitivity in the task.
- **Soares, Atallah & Paton (2016), `Midbrain dopamine neurons control judgment of time`, Science 354:1273–1277.** Dopamine-neuron manipulations causally shift interval judgments/sensitivity in mice.
- **Bakhurin et al. (2017), `Differential Encoding of Time by Prefrontal and Striatal Network Dynamics`.** Elapsed-time information is present in distributed cortical/striatal population dynamics with regionally different decoding fidelity.
- **Cordes & Gallistel et al. (2008), `Intact Interval Timing in Circadian CLOCK Mutants`.** CLOCK-mutant mice showed no reliable impairment in 10/20-second peak-interval timing accuracy/precision in the tested procedure.
- **de la Iglesia, Cambras, Schwartz & Díez-Noguera (2004), `Forced desynchronization of dual circadian oscillators within the rat suprachiasmatic nucleus`.** The SCN itself behaves as a coupled oscillator network whose components can be experimentally desynchronized.
- **Stephan (1983), `Circadian rhythm dissociation induced by periodic feeding in rats with suprachiasmatic lesions`.** Food-anticipatory rhythmic activity can be generated in SCN-lesioned rats, supporting non-SCN circadian timing routes.
