# 0. Purpose

MF9-H showed that major consciousness theories imply radically different developmental onset criteria:

```text
GNW        → sufficiently broad/global availability
RPT        → recurrent sensory processing
HOT        → higher-order representation
IIT        → intrinsic causal organization
AST        → attention-schema organization
SMCT       → sensorimotor mastery
Enactive   → autonomous embodied sense-making
Affect-first → felt homeostatic/affective organization
Illusionism → quasi-phenomenal states + introspective misrepresentation
```

Therefore asking only:

```text
"At what week/month does consciousness begin?"
```

presupposes the very theory under dispute.

MF9-I instead asks:

> **Which experiential capacities and evidence routes emerge when, how do they depend on one another, and is there any justified single developmental switch from non-experience to experience?**

# 7. Functional connectome evidence argues against a universal birth reset

Ji et al. (2024) modeled functional-network maturation from fetal to neonatal periods.

They found:

```text
some networks / connections
→ dramatic birth-associated change

others
→ gradual maturation across birth

others
→ relatively little immediate birth change
```

### I9-013
Birth is therefore a major heterogeneous perturbation, not a uniform whole-brain 0→1 event.

### I9-014
**BirthTriggeredConnectivityChange ≠ BirthTriggeredExperienceOnset.**

Network transition is evidence about physical capacity/organization, not direct phenomenality.

---

# 8. Structural and functional connectivity emerge prenatally

In-utero fMRI/dMRI work shows substantial fetal organization before birth.

Taymourtash et al. found a peak increase in thalamocortical functional-connectivity strength around 29–31 gestational weeks, with cortico-cortical connectivity increasing in a similar period.

Large perinatal diffusion studies find principal thalamocortical structural patterns already identifiable around 30 weeks and gradually refining thereafter.

### I9-015
**AnatomicalConnection ≠ FunctionalIntegration.**

### I9-016
**FunctionalConnectivity ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### I9-017
But these findings weaken any theory that requires all relevant cortical/thalamocortical organization to appear only after birth.

---

# 9. Small-world and resting-state organization are precursors, not consciousness signatures

Fetal imaging identifies resting-state networks and increasingly differentiated integration/segregation during the third trimester.

### I9-018
**RestingStateNetworkStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### I9-019
**SmallWorldTopology ≠ consciousness marker.**

Small-world organization is too generic across biological/artificial networks to identify phenomenality.

### I9-020
Its developmental importance lies in showing that large-scale coordination scaffolds are present before mature report or adult cognition.

---

# 10. Sensory responsiveness begins before any plausible report

Fetal auditory-evoked responses and stimulus-linked behavioral/autonomic changes are observed during later gestation, with response latencies and reliability changing with age and state.

### I9-021
**AuditoryEvokedResponse ≠ AuditoryExperience.**

### I9-022
A sensory response demonstrates transmission/processing capacity, not by itself conscious perception.

### I9-023
Nevertheless, sensory responsiveness places a lower-level constraint on theories of exteroceptive experiential development: a route cannot support auditory experience before the relevant sensory processing pathway exists.

---

# 11. Prenatal learning is real but not consciousness-specific

Partanen et al. exposed fetuses to a melody during the last trimester and found exposure-dependent neonatal ERP differences at birth and four months later.

Other late-gestation work finds learning/memory effects for voices, speech statistics and melodies.

### I9-024
**PrenatalLearningStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

### I9-025
**MemoryTraceStanding ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

Unconscious learning exists in adults; therefore learning is positive evidence of organized processing but not a consciousness-specific marker.

---

# 12. Hierarchical auditory rule learning is stronger than simple evoked response

Moser et al. (2021) used fetal MEG in 56 fetuses from 25–40 gestational weeks with first- and second-order auditory regularities.

A memory trace for the second-order regularity was reliably present only in fetuses older than 35 gestational weeks.

### I9-026
Second-order/global-rule sensitivity is more demanding evidence than simple sensory mismatch because it requires integration across a longer sequence/context.

### I9-027
However:

```text
HierarchicalRuleLearning
≠ ExperienceStanding
```

because local-global/global-deviance effects are not perfectly consciousness-specific even in adults and depend on paradigm/state.

---

# 13. Newborn local-global processing continues the late-fetal trajectory

Moser et al. (2020) found newborns detect local regularity violations broadly, while late responses to second-order violations appeared particularly in an active/vigilant physiological state.

### I9-028
This suggests continuity of a hierarchical processing architecture across birth rather than a birth-created architecture.

### I9-029
It also establishes:

```text
DevelopmentalCapacity
× CurrentState
→ ObservableMarker
```

The same developing brain may express a marker in one state and not another.

---

# 14. Fetal behavioral state is not adult wakefulness

Late gestation shows increasingly organized quiet/active sleep-like state patterns and state-dependent sensory/cognitive responses.

### I9-030
**FetalBehavioralState ≠ AdultWakefulnessStanding.**

### I9-031
**FetalBehavioralState ≠ ExperienceStanding.**

A fetus being in an `active` state does not prove connected consciousness; a `quiet` state does not prove no experience.

MF9-F's sleep-state firewall transfers directly to development.

# 19. Gestational thresholds are theory-conditional

A cortex-dependent theory may regard incomplete early cortical/thalamocortical organization as meaningful negative evidence.

A brainstem/affect-first theory may predict an earlier candidate onset if its required subcortical/homeostatic organization is available.

IIT may generate still different substrate/grain predictions.

### I9-041
**`No consciousness before week X` is not theory-neutral unless the necessary property and its absence are independently established.**

### I9-042
The earlier in gestation one goes, the more theory-dependent negative inference becomes.

# 20. Evidence bands, not consciousness bands

MF9-I uses rough developmental **evidence bands** only:

```text
Earlier than ~24 weeks:
  many cortical/global architectures invoked by major theories are very immature;
  evidence for experience is weak and highly theory-dependent.

~24–30 weeks:
  rapidly emerging thalamocortical/cortical organization,
  sensory responses and local functional networks;
  mostly precursor/capacity evidence.

~30–35 weeks:
  stronger integration, state organization and learning;
  still sparse consciousness-specific evidence.

>~35 weeks:
  strongest current prenatal evidence for hierarchical/global auditory processing;
  marker remains theory- and state-dependent.

Birth / newborn:
  dramatic environmental/physiological transition,
  plus continuity of multiple fetal processing architectures.

~5 months and later:
  much stronger cluster of adult-calibrated perceptual-awareness-like markers.
```

### I9-043
These bands are **not** frozen phenomenological boundaries.

# 21. Why 5 months matters without becoming an onset date

Kouider et al. showed a late nonlinear perceptual-response signature in 5-, 12- and 15-month-old infants, with substantially slower responses in younger infants.

Dehaene-Lambertz (2024) argues that, using adult global-workspace benchmarks, evidence is relatively solid in the second half of the first year but less solid before 5 months mainly because of limited data.

### I9-044
MF9-I therefore records:

```text
By ~5 months:
  strong convergent support for perceptual-awareness-like capacity
```

but rejects:

```text
Consciousness begins at 5 months.
```

### I9-045
A robust marker's earliest study age is an **upper evidential bound**, not necessarily the developmental onset.

# 24. Body-space coding itself develops postnatally

### I9-051

### I9-052
This is direct evidence for **phenomenal/perceptual enrichment without requiring first onset**.

An infant can plausibly experience touch while lacking mature external localization of that touch.

# 30. GNW/GWT developmental predictions

A strong GNW route predicts consciousness only when information can undergo sufficiently broad recurrent/global availability.

Late fetal/newborn hierarchical rule responses and infant late nonlinear responses are therefore theory-relevant.

### I9-062

```text
LocalGlobalMarker

```

### I9-063
GNW can support early onset only if a sufficiently functional global architecture exists before mature prefrontal cognition/report; it must not equate mature adult PFC with the entire workspace necessity.

# 31. RPT developmental predictions

RPT can allow an earlier sensory-experience onset because recurrent processing can exist within sensory systems before mature global access/metacognition.

# 32. Higher-order theory development splits the family

Traditional conceptual HOT versions require capacities for higher-order conceptual representation of mental states.

Those capacities plausibly mature relatively late.

Nonconceptual higher-order variants allow much earlier onset.

### I9-066
**HOT-family label does not determine developmental onset.**

### I9-067
Early infant sensory evidence pressures conceptual-HOT universal necessity much more strongly than nonconceptual higher-order variants.

---

# 33. IIT developmental prediction is likely graded but not currently computable

IIT does not require language/report and could assign experience whenever an appropriate intrinsic causal complex exists.

### I9-068
This suggests no privileged birth boundary by theory alone.

As neural causal organization differentiates, IIT may predict changing complex/Φ-structure rather than a single mature-cognition switch.

### I9-069
But fetal/infant functional-connectivity measures are **not exact IIT Φ**; realistic developmental IIT calculations are unavailable.

Therefore connectivity development is only indirect theory-relevant evidence.

# 34. AST developmental onset remains almost entirely open

AST requires more than attention:

```text
attention
+
internal simplified model of attention
+
control use / awareness attribution
```

Infants clearly deploy attention early, but:

### I9-070
**EarlyAttentionStanding ≠ EarlyAttentionSchemaStanding.**

### I9-071
There is currently no validated fetal/newborn marker that uniquely identifies an attention schema.

If AST requires sophisticated self/social modeling, onset could be late; if a minimal control model suffices, onset could be much earlier.

This is an unresolved theory-definition problem, not merely missing data.

---

# 35. Sensorimotor developmental onset exposes strong/weak SMCT

Fetuses and newborns have extensive movement and action–sensory regularities, but mature reaching, locomotion, external spatial coding and action skill develop later.

### I9-072
A strong theory requiring mature sensorimotor mastery predicts relatively late sensory consciousness.

### I9-073
Early sensory-awareness evidence pressures that strong version.

A weaker theory where primitive sensorimotor contingencies scaffold sensory quality can allow early experience with later enrichment.

### I9-074
**SensorimotorDevelopment shapes experience ≠ mature sensorimotor skill is required for experience onset.**

---

# 36. Enactive development predicts a continuity of embodied organization

Prenatal movement, interoceptive regulation and sensory coupling precede birth; birth radically changes the available world and autonomy demands.

### I9-075
Enactive theories therefore naturally model birth as **reconfiguration of coupling**, not a categorical creation of embodiment.

### I9-076
But they still owe a criterion for when embodied autonomous dynamics first become experience-supporting rather than merely living regulation.

# 39. Illusionism creates a developmental dilemma

Strong illusionism explains classic phenomenality through introspective misrepresentation of quasi-phenomenal states.

But sophisticated phenomenal concepts and introspective theories emerge relatively late.

### I9-081
If strong illusionism requires mature phenomenal concepts for any relevant `inner life`, it predicts an implausibly late onset relative to infant sensory/affective evidence.

### I9-082
It can instead posit:

```text
early quasi-phenomenal sensory/affective states
→ later introspective phenomenality representation
→ later explicit phenomenal concepts/report
```

But then it must clarify what the early `inner life` amounts to before illusion-generating cognition matures.

# 41. The developmental manifold model

Current evidence is best reconstructed as partially ordered trajectories:

```text

   ├─> sensory responsiveness
   ├─> state organization
   └─> interoceptive/affective precursors?

sensorimotor coupling
   ├─> bodily organization
   └─> progressively richer spatial/action structure

   ├─> candidate early exteroceptive experience
```

### I9-084

# 46. Positive and negative developmental evidence remain asymmetric

### I9-092

### I9-093

```text

+ age-appropriate sensitivity
+ state control
```

# 47. Individual variation blocks exact universal age switches

Gestational development, premature birth, illness, sensory history and neural maturation vary between individuals.

### I9-094
Even if a specific neural capacity were necessary for ExperienceStanding, its developmental attainment would not necessarily occur at the exact same gestational/postnatal age in every bearer.

### I9-095
A universal exact age cutoff is therefore biologically implausible unless tied to a robust invariant developmental event.

No such invariant has been demonstrated.

# 50. Provisional developmental evidence verdict

Current evidence supports the following **confidence ordering**, not ontological thresholds:

```text
Second half of first year:
  strong evidence for perceptual-awareness-like capacity.

Newborn / term-equivalent period:
  substantial but marker-dependent evidence for organized perception,
  hierarchical processing, body organization and state-dependent access-like processing.

Late third trimester, especially >~35 GW:
  meaningful candidate evidence for hierarchical/global auditory processing,
  alongside rapidly maturing network/state organization.

Earlier gestation:
  evidence increasingly dominated by prerequisites/precursors
  and increasingly dependent on constitutive theory.
```

### I9-100
**Exact first ExperienceStanding onset remains unresolved.**

### I9-101
No date/week/month is frozen as a universal consciousness boundary.

# 54. Evidence anchors

1. **Bayne, Frohlich, Cusack, Moser & Naci (2023), `Consciousness in the cradle: on the emergence of infant experience`, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 27:1135–1149, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2023.08.018`.** Methodological/theoretical synthesis favoring early-onset accounts while explicitly rejecting certainty about a precise onset.
2. **Dehaene-Lambertz (2024), `Perceptual Awareness in Human Infants: What is the Evidence?`, Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience 36:1599–1609, DOI `10.1162/jocn_a_02149`.** Stronger perceptual-awareness evidence in the second half of year one; evidence before 5 months remains sparse/less solid.
3. **Moser et al. (2021), `Magnetoencephalographic signatures of conscious processing before birth`, Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience 49:100964, DOI `10.1016/j.dcn.2021.100964`.** Second-order auditory rule-learning response reliably present in fetuses older than 35 GW; state dependence.
4. **Moser et al. (2020), `Magnetoencephalographic signatures of hierarchical rule learning in newborns`, Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience 46:100871, DOI `10.1016/j.dcn.2020.100871`.** Newborn local/global hierarchical processing with late second-order response dependent on active/vigilant state.
5. **Taymourtash et al. (2023), `Fetal development of functional thalamocortical and cortico-cortical connectivity`, Cerebral Cortex 33:5613–5624, DOI `10.1093/cercor/bhac446`.** Prenatal thalamocortical/cortico-cortical functional-connectivity development with strong increase around 29–31 GW.
6. **Oldham et al. (2025), `Perinatal development of structural thalamocortical connectivity`, Imaging Neuroscience 3, PMID `40800876`.** Principal structural thalamocortical patterns present around 30 weeks and progressively refined.
7. **Ji et al. (2024), `Trajectories of human brain functional connectome maturation across the birth transition`, PLOS Biology 22:e3002909, DOI `10.1371/journal.pbio.3002909`.** Heterogeneous birth-transition trajectories: dramatic changes in some networks and gradual/limited changes in others.
8. **Partanen et al. (2013), `Prenatal music exposure induces long-term neural effects`, PLOS ONE 8:e78946, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0078946`.** Prenatal melody exposure leaves exposure-dependent neonatal and 4-month ERP effects.
9. **Kouider et al. (2013), `A neural marker of perceptual consciousness in infants`, Science 340:376–380, DOI `10.1126/science.1232509`.** Late nonlinear perceptual-response marker at 5, 12 and 15 months, with slower latency in younger infants.
10. **Filippetti et al. (2015), `Newborn Body Perception: Sensitivity to Spatial Congruency`, Infancy 20:455–465, DOI `10.1111/infa.12083`.** Newborn sensitivity to spatially congruent synchronous visuotactile body-related events.
11. **Meltzoff, Saby & Marshall (2019), `Neural representations of the body in 60-day-old human infants`, Developmental Science 22:e12698, DOI `10.1111/desc.12698`.** Body-part-specific somatosensory EEG organization at two months.
12. **Ali, Spence & Bremner (2015), `Human infants' ability to perceive touch in external space develops postnatally`, Current Biology 25, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2015.08.055`.** Adult-like external spatial coding of touch appears between four and six months in the tested paradigm.
13. **Goupil & Kouider (2016), `Behavioral and Neural Indices of Metacognitive Sensitivity in Preverbal Infants`, Current Biology 26:3038–3045, DOI `10.1016/j.cub.2016.09.004`.** 12- and 18-month preverbal infants monitor confidence/errors and adapt behavior.
14. **RCOG (2022), `Fetal Awareness: Updated review of Research and Recommendations for Practice`.** Authoritative clinical evidence synthesis: fetal pain requires network-level organization; local connectivity prominent around 28 weeks with longer-range connectivity increasing after 30 weeks; noxious/innocuous differentiation reported around 33 weeks.
15. **Weiss et al. (2024), `Trajectories of brain and behaviour development in the womb, at birth and through infancy`, Nature Human Behaviour 8:1251–1262, DOI `10.1038/s41562-024-01896-7`.** Birth as punctuate event within a continuous developmental trajectory involving prenatal physiology/perception and postnatal recalibration.

# 56. MF9-I verdict

MF9-I rejects three opposite simplifications:

```text
A. Birth creates consciousness.
B. One cortical connection/week creates consciousness.
C. Any fetal processing/learning proves consciousness.
```

The best current reconstruction is:

```text
PhenomenalDevelopmentManifold
```

in which multiple capacities emerge on partially independent trajectories.

The evidence suggests:

```text
prenatal sensory/network/state organization
→ increasing developmental prerequisites

late third trimester
→ meaningful candidate hierarchical/global processing evidence

birth
→ major heterogeneous recalibration, not universal reset

newborn/early infancy
→ substantial organized sensory/body/state processing

~5 months onward
→ stronger adult-calibrated perceptual-awareness evidence

later infancy
→ increasingly explicit confidence monitoring, external body-space coding,
   self/world organization and eventually report/introspection
```

The key theoretical consequence is:

> **The first ExperienceStanding token, if there is a discrete first token at all, need not coincide with the first mature global-access marker, first metacognition, first explicit self, first report, or birth.**

And the key epistemic conclusion is:

> **Current evidence does not justify a precise universal developmental consciousness timestamp. It supports increasingly strong, theory-sensitive intervals and a graded enrichment of multiple experiential capacities.**

MF9 remains provisional.
