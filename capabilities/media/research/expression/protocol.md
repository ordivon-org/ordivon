# Expression production protocol

## Purpose

This is the single cross-medium production loop for Ordivon Agents. It operationalizes [`creative-system.md`](./creative-system.md) without turning creative work into a workflow engine.

```text
FRAME → BIND → EXPRESS → RENDER → AUDIT → DECIDE
```

The stages are cognitive responsibilities. They do not require six files, six approvals, a state machine, or a fixed sequence of tool calls. An Agent may move backward when a rendered artifact falsifies an earlier assumption.

## 1. FRAME — intended experience

Establish what this work is for before choosing a style or tool.

Recover at least:

- audience / observer class;
- encounter and task;
- desired experiential outcomes;
- explicit non-goals;
- why a new or changed artifact is warranted.

A valid result may be `no change needed`. Production is not required merely because a loop began.

## 2. BIND — truth and focalization

Bind expression to the authorities that own reality.

Recover at least:

- exact source/evidence identity where factual currency matters;
- current, historical, target, inferred, hypothetical, and unknown states as distinct classes;
- the work's focalization or information entitlement;
- facts the work may state;
- implications the work must not create.

Studio does not gain authority over a source domain by binding it for expression.

## 3. EXPRESS — medium-native strategy

Select relevant evidence, durable priors, medium craft, and current context only as needed. Then choose how the medium should carry the intended experience.

Useful inputs include:

- tension profile;
- hierarchy and attention strategy;
- narrative / information-release structure;
- rhythm and temporal structure;
- medium-specific craft priors;
- deliberate departures from convention.

Transfer event models, focalization, tensions, and experiential relations across media when useful. Do not mechanically copy geometry or surface style.

## 4. RENDER — observe the real artifact

The authoring representation is not the final perceptual fact.

Inspect the actual medium output: browser surface, responsive state, frame, video, audio, image, page, interaction, presentation, or another real delivery form.

Diagnostics may prove that a manipulation exists; they do not by themselves prove beauty, interest, trust, dread, clarity, or another human experience.

## 5. AUDIT — recover explicit and implicit semantics

Ask what an observer can infer from the artifact without access to the Agent's rationale.

Audit, where relevant:

- explicit factual claims;
- source / product / temporal state;
- probability or confidence implied by balance, scale, or repetition;
- severity, trust, or outcome implied by color and emphasis;
- direction or location implied by placement;
- causality or trend implied by order and motion;
- absence versus unknown implied by negative space;
- maturity or authority implied by polish and framing;
- medium-specific accessibility, legibility, timing, audio, and interaction failures.

A rendered semantic defect is a real defect even when every sentence is literally correct.

## 6. DECIDE — stop, revise, or promote

Choose one of three normal outcomes:

```text
revise
no-op
promote
```

Route failures to the earliest wrong assumption:

```text
wrong target             → FRAME
wrong authority/boundary → BIND
wrong expression         → EXPRESS
render/implementation    → RENDER / implementation
```

Before promotion, recheck any upstream fact whose currency matters. Creative correctness does not prove publication currentness.

## Human calibration

Human or expert response is not a mandatory stage. Use it when the remaining material uncertainty is genuinely about human experience, interpretation, culture, or consequence and cannot be resolved from source truth, mechanical inspection, mature craft, or rendered semantic audit.

One observer remains one observation.

## Research branch

Research is not part of every production. Enter a research branch when a durable uncertainty survives normal production, for example when:

- the same failure repeats across works;
- Agent judgment remains unstable under controlled comparison;
- media disagree about a supposedly transferable prior;
- a new medium exposes a missing concept;
- real audience/world evidence repeatedly contradicts a retained prior.

Research may use literature retrieval, reference decomposition, controlled transformation, ablation, contrastive reconstruction, human/expert calibration, or longitudinal observation. The result returns as scoped evidence, not as an automatic universal rule.

## Learning boundary

After DECIDE, classify retained learning by the narrowest scope that explains the evidence:

```text
artifact-local observation
        ↓ repeated within one medium
medium-profile candidate
        ↓ survives materially different media
cross-medium core candidate
```

Do not invent a reusable principle merely because a production finished.
