# Foundational media topology

## Purpose

Ordivon Studio needs a media world model that is broader than its current production equipment without degenerating into one profile per platform, file type, genre, or distribution format.

This document defines a **provisional seven-foundation topology** for research:

```text
Writing
Still Visual
Audio
Motion / Video
Interactive
Spatial / 3D
Live / Realtime
```

The seven are research hypotheses, not seven permanent ministries. A foundation survives only if it exposes an observer relation, manipulable variable, semantic failure, render/inspection boundary, or hard constraint that cannot be represented adequately by the cross-medium Core plus the other foundations.

Existing active Writing and Motion / Video profiles remain authoritative. Web remains an externally owned consumer profile. Existing Still / Graphic, Audio / Music, and Interactive baselines remain provisional. Spatial / 3D and Live / Realtime begin as candidates and must earn profile authority through real evidence.

## What counts as a foundational medium

A foundation is not defined by MIME type, tool, platform, industry name, or monetization channel. It is defined by the relation between an artifact and an observer.

A candidate foundation should survive several pressure tests.

### 1. Affordance test

Does it expose a class of expressive control that the Core and other foundations cannot represent without losing important structure?

### 2. Semantic-failure test

Can it accidentally imply facts, causality, state, probability, location, identity, agency, urgency, certainty, or affect in a way that requires medium-specific audit?

### 3. Encounter test

Does the observer encounter it through a materially different control relationship: simultaneous field, authored timeline, self-paced symbolic sequence, state-changing action, embodied viewpoint, or unrehearsed shared present?

### 4. Render / inspect test

Is the real perceptual artifact observable through a boundary that materially differs from source representation and from the other foundations?

### 5. Constraint test

Does it introduce hard technical, accessibility, synchronization, spatial, latency, safety, or delivery constraints that cannot be treated as mere platform context?

### 6. Ablation test

If the candidate profile is removed and an Agent receives only the Core plus the other profiles, do repeated production failures appear that the candidate would have predicted or prevented?

A profile that fails these tests should be narrowed into a specialization, encounter profile, distribution context, or tool adapter rather than retained as a foundational medium.

## The seven provisional foundations

### 1. Writing

**Observer relation:** symbolic language unfolds through a reader-controlled semantic sequence. The reader can normally pause, reread, scan, skip, quote, and change pace without changing the canonical text.

**Irreducible controls:** proposition order, syntax, cadence, paragraph and section structure, rhetorical distance, voice, quotation, citation, metaphor, explicitness, information release, title/lead/ending.

**Characteristic failures:** fluent prose upgrades weak evidence; paragraph adjacency invents causal or evidentiary relation; metaphor imports false properties; title/body certainty diverges; compression erases observation/inference/decision boundaries.

**Real artifact:** complete semantic text and, when publication affects meaning, its rendered reading surface.

**Likely specializations:** technical explanation, argument/report, narrative, script, advertising/social copy, dialogue. These are not new foundations by default.

### 2. Still Visual

**Observer relation:** meaning is carried by a persistent simultaneous visual field rather than an authored temporal sequence.

**Irreducible controls:** composition, figure/ground, position, scale, visual weight, depth cues, light, color, texture, symbol, crop, negative space, typography-as-image, viewing distance.

**Characteristic failures:** position implies unsupported relation or bearing; scale implies importance/probability; color assigns outcome/trust/severity; crop removes qualifying evidence; diagram geometry claims causal structure; polish implies maturity or authority.

**Real artifact:** the actual raster/vector/printed/displayed image under relevant crop, scale, color and viewing conditions.

**Likely specializations:** photography, illustration, graphic design, information graphic, diagram, thumbnail/key visual. Specialization is earned only when the shared still-visual profile repeatedly under-explains production failures.

### 3. Audio

**Observer relation:** a time-based acoustic field is perceived without requiring a visual carrier. Temporal access, masking, loudness, spatial hearing and playback context matter even when the semantic content originated as text.

**Irreducible controls:** voice/prosody, timing, rhythm, meter, pitch, harmony, timbre, dynamics, spectral balance, silence, ambience, effects, stereo/spatial field, loudness, masking and auditory attention.

**Characteristic failures:** prosody changes certainty or emotion; loudness becomes emphasis; music assigns valence before evidence permits it; masking destroys intelligibility; silence implies absence or threat; spatialization invents location; normalization/playback changes intended balance.

**Real artifact:** the audible master through representative playback chains, not waveform/source settings alone.

**Likely specializations:** speech/narration, sound design/ambience, music. They remain under Audio until evidence shows that one shared profile cannot explain their distinct failures.

### 4. Motion / Video

**Observer relation:** the author controls a visual temporal sequence; sequence, duration and change are part of the expression. Audio may be synchronized but is not required.

**Irreducible controls:** shot/scene duration, cut, movement, velocity, acceleration, hold, reveal, continuity, montage, temporal density, animated typography/graphics, audiovisual synchronization.

**Characteristic failures:** order implies causality; movement implies state transition; edit compression hides uncertainty; direction implies bearing; repeated pulse/countdown invents urgency; one attractive frame hides broken pacing or continuity.

**Real artifact:** continuous playback plus exact frames/time ranges and technical media inspection.

**Current status:** active Studio profile with real Remotion/Resolve/OTIO consumers.

### 5. Interactive

**Observer relation:** an observer action can alter subsequent state, information, path, timing, or consequence. The artifact is partly realized through the encounter rather than being only replayed.

**Irreducible controls:** affordance, agency, action space, feedback, state legibility, response timing, reversibility, recovery, progressive disclosure, uncertainty, navigation, input modality, consequence.

**Characteristic failures:** affordance suggests an action that does something else; feedback implies success before commitment; hidden state breaks causal understanding; irreversible action lacks sufficient information; latency looks like failure; branching reveals privileged information; interaction creates dark-pattern pressure.

**Real artifact:** representative state/action trajectories, including error and recovery paths, not screenshots or source code alone.

**Boundary:** interaction does not require a live external producer. A deterministic offline game or local application can be deeply interactive without being Live / Realtime.

### 6. Spatial / 3D

**Observer relation:** objects and observers occupy explicit coordinate/reference spaces; viewpoint, orientation, depth, occlusion, scale, locomotion or embodiment can change what is perceivable and meaningful.

**Irreducible controls:** world/reference frames, camera or head pose, projection, field of view, scale, depth, occlusion, lighting, spatial audio relation, locomotion, reach, anchoring, parallax, persistence and world-relative placement.

**Characteristic failures:** coordinate frame mismatch invents location; apparent scale misstates size/importance; occlusion hides required evidence; camera motion creates sickness or false motion; an AR anchor implies physical correspondence that is not real; perspective or embodiment changes perceived distance, threat, ownership or reachability.

**Real artifact:** rendered scene from materially different viewpoints and, where embodiment matters, the actual XR/3D encounter rather than a single 2D capture.

**Boundary:** a pre-rendered 3D movie is Motion / Video at encounter time even if its production used 3D tools. Spatial / 3D is foundational when the spatial relation itself survives into the observer encounter or materially governs the work's semantics.

### 7. Live / Realtime

**Observer relation:** production and encounter share a present in which relevant future events are not yet fixed. The producer/Agent must act under live uncertainty, latency and possible audience/world feedback.

**Irreducible controls:** live pacing, improvisation, turn-taking, moderation, delay, correction, handoff, interruption, queueing, audience feedback, synchronization, contingency, failure recovery, liveness signaling.

**Characteristic failures:** prerecorded certainty is falsely implied during uncertain events; latency is mistaken for refusal/failure; stale state is presented as live; an irreversible statement/action occurs before verification; moderation cannot keep pace; correction arrives after the misleading claim has propagated; audience feedback drives unstable escalation.

**Real artifact:** the event trace plus the observer's time-bound encounter, including delays, interruptions, corrections and state changes. A polished replay is insufficient evidence for live behavior.

**Boundary:** Live / Realtime is not merely low-latency Interactive. Interaction asks whether observer action changes state. Liveness asks whether the relevant future is still being produced while the observer is present. A broadcast can be live but minimally interactive; an offline game can be interactive but not live.

## Composition model

Most familiar media forms are compositions, not additional foundations.

```text
production form
=
foundational media
+ specialization(s)
+ encounter profile
+ distribution context
+ tool / delivery constraints
```

Examples:

```text
podcast
= Writing + Audio

video essay
= Writing + Still Visual + Audio + Motion/Video

website
= Writing + Still Visual + Interactive
  + optional Motion/Video + Audio

presentation
= Writing + Still Visual
  + sequential encounter
  + optional Live + Audio

game
= Interactive + Writing + Still Visual/Motion + Audio
  + optional Spatial/3D + Live

livestream
= Live + Audio + Motion/Video
  + optional Interactive

AR experience
= Spatial/3D + Interactive + Still/Motion + Audio
  + optional Live

social post
= Writing and/or Still Visual and/or Motion/Video
  + platform distribution context
```

TikTok, YouTube, X, Instagram, Xiaohongshu, browser, cinema, headphones, projection and headset are therefore primarily distribution/encounter contexts unless evidence shows a genuinely irreducible observer relation.

## Cross-foundation invariants

The seven do not replace the Art & Expression Core. Every foundation still inherits:

- intended experience before style;
- source truth and focalization boundaries;
- explicit separation of current / historical / target / inferred / hypothetical / unknown;
- rendered consequence over source rationale;
- semantic audit of implicit as well as explicit claims;
- provenance, rights and exact artifact identity;
- accessibility and alternate-representation obligations where applicable;
- local → medium → cross-medium evidence promotion;
- `no-op` as a valid production decision.

## Open boundary questions

The topology deliberately leaves several boundaries falsifiable:

1. Should Spatial representation and embodied/XR encounter later split?
2. Do speech, sound design and music remain one Audio foundation or graduate separate profiles?
3. Does data visualization remain a Still/Interactive specialization or expose a separate representational grammar?
4. Does performance/theatre belong under Live plus other foundations, or expose a distinct embodied-social medium?
5. At what point does an Agent-mediated conversation require both Interactive and Live rather than one profile?

These questions should be answered by production failures and ablations, not taxonomy preference.
